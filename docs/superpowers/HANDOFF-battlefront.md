# Hand-off: Battlefront

**Battlefront is `bf2-web`’s engine** ([the decision](../decisions/2026-10-29-battlefront-is-bf2-webs-engine.md)). Its compiled engine plays Hoth on the visitor’s GPU, served from the private bucket by the Worker. The site’s own Battlefront game (the sim, the bots, the modes, Hoth’s world, its screens and the front end’s room) was removed on 2026-10-29 ([the plan](plans/2026-10-29-battlefront-engine-only.md)); it is in git history before that change, with its decisions from 2026-10-10 to 2026-10-14. What is left here:
- the engine’s way in and what is open on it;
- the 2017 game’s data and assets, which the galaxy reads (its surfaces, cast, vehicles, sabers, Starfighter Assault, space).

The owner’s export of the game (gameplay records as EBX JSON, MeshSets, clips, textures, maps, Havok shapes) sits on their desktop and in the private `bf2017-assets` bucket; the pipeline’s scripts under `scripts/bf2017-*.mjs` read it.

## The engine

### Landed

- **Hoth is `bf2-web`’s** ([the decision](../decisions/2026-10-28-hoth-on-bf2-web.md)). A browser that can run it goes from `/battlefront/hoth` to `bf2-web`’s exported play site (its engine in WebAssembly, its WebGPU renderer and lighting, its front end, bots, combat and round), served by the Worker from the private bucket at `/battlefront/engine/<build>/` (`cloudflare/play.js`, `src/data/bf2web/play.json`, `src/components/battlefront/engine.js`). A browser that can’t is told why, on `/battlefront` (`src/pages/Battlefront.jsx`); the JS port of `bf2-web`’s rules that was to run the site’s world for it went with that world on 2026-10-29.
- **Hoth’s free roam, on a button** (2026-10-29). Flying into Hoth or landing on it opens the engine (`src/components/galaxy/gameLevels.js`’s `GAME_MENU`). The panel’s “Free roam on Hoth” and “Free roam at Echo Base” and the briefing’s “Free roam on Hoth” go to the galaxy’s own Hoth (`/galaxy/hoth/surface?mode=free`), whose mode menu leads with Play Battlefront, back to the engine (`surface/modes.js`). The site’s own Hoth missions (the first transport, its assault, the Heroes vs Villains and Blast arenas) stay in their tables, withheld by `surface/missions/index.js`’s `missionOf` on a world the engine plays (`freeRoamOnly`).

### Left

- **A first visit a quarter smaller.** Cloudflare compresses the engine’s wasm and JSON itself (104 MB of wasm arrives as 17), but not the textures (1.65 GB), the scene data (0.3 GB) or the collision. `scripts/bf2web-compress.mjs` writes brotli copies of those into the bucket, with an `encodings.json` listing them, and `cloudflare/play.js` sends a copy to a browser that takes brotli (whole requests only: the engine asks ranges of the menu videos alone). Measured on samples, brotli 9: textures to 85%, scene data 33%, collision 18%; about 2.07 GB to 1.57 on the wire. The pipeline compresses every build it adopts (`stage: compress` alone for one). A repeat visit reads the build from disk: `cloudflare/engineCache.js`’s service worker (served at `/battlefront/engine/sw.js`, registered by the engine page’s injected script, scope `/battlefront/engine/`) keeps each build’s files in Cache Storage (`tp-engine-<build>`, one build at a time), never the page, the roots outside the build or a range. Checked headless: a 52 MB scene file 6.4 s from the network, 0.8 s from disk. bf2-web’s own OPFS cache (`runtime/payload-cache.js`) isn’t wired into its play page; when it is, this can go. **Why a round goes back to the menu.** Any error in `runWorld` ends the instance: `world-play.mjs`’s `finish` sets the menu and shows `play-instance-failed: …` (and now the site’s box). Three causes are known:
- **A background tab.** Two stages only move when the page polls them, each poll a `setTimeout(0)` or `(2)`, under a deadline of the clock’s: the effects’ atlases (one `ResourceManager_update` per poll, 60 s) and the warm-up (180 s a frame). A hidden tab’s timers run once a second, and once a minute after five minutes hidden. `cloudflare/playTimers.js` answers short timers through a `MessageChannel` while the page is hidden.
- **A dropped request.** The loader has no retry and no `response.ok`. The service worker now asks again, after 0.5, 1.5 and 4 s, for a failed fetch or a 5xx.
- **A discarded tab.** Chrome’s Memory Saver may drop a big background tab, which reloads to the menu. Nothing on the site prevents that.
- **Why frame 0 never came, and who is sent to the engine now.** Headless on the live site (2026-10-28) the engine loaded to 88% and then waited on frame 0 for good: 12 of its 367 pipelines failed (`Invalid PipelineLayout`), the render thread stopped submitting. Ten of the materials Hoth draws sample 23 textures in one pixel stage; WebGPU’s default `maxSampledTexturesPerShaderStage` is 16, SwiftShader’s adapter allows 16, so their layouts can’t be made. The owner’s laptop (no dedicated GPU) failed at frame 0 the same way. The adopt step now reads each build’s needs off the programs it draws (`programNeeds`, over `pipelines.json`’s names) into `play.json`’s `needs`, and `engineSupport` sends to the engine only an adapter with them (main-da7a51b3: 23 textures, 8 samplers, 7 storage buffers, 1 storage texture, 3 uniform buffers, 4 groups, dual-source blending); everyone else gets the site’s world. **Since round 1032 (main-32a7a627) bf2-web packs them** (SH-10, `runtime/web-renderer-pack.js`: a program over the device’s limit draws through texture arrays), and the build sends its own floor: `play-config.json`’s `requires` (the raw catalog counts, 23 still) and its page’s `FLOOR` and `PACKED_SAMPLED_TEXTURES` (`runtime/play-quality.mjs`, whose `belowFloor` caps the sampled textures at 16). The adopt step now takes a build’s needs from that floor where it has one (`floorNeeds`, the module imported from the bucket; it imports nothing), else from its programs. For main-32a7a627 that is 16 textures, 8 samplers, 10 storage buffers, 5 storage textures, 3 uniform buffers, 4 groups, the floor’s texture size and buffers, and BC alone (no dual source: the engine asks the device for it, `web-renderer-gpu.js`). `engineSupport` also turns away a software adapter and fewer than four cores, as the page does, so a 16-texture laptop is sent to the engine and the page doesn’t refuse it there.
- **bf2-web’s builds, on their own** ([the decision](../decisions/2026-10-28-bf2web-pipeline.md)). `.github/workflows/bf2web.yml` builds `bf2-web`’s main on the desktop (`scripts/desktop/bf2web-build.mjs`, set `BF2WEB_REPO` in the runner’s `.env`), uploads what changed (`scripts/bf2web-upload.mjs`), holds the newest build to the contract (`scripts/bf2web-adopt.mjs`, `scripts/lib/bf2web-site.mjs`), opens it through the Worker’s code (`scripts/bf2web-smoke.mjs`), pushes `play.json` and starts the deploy. Waiting on `bf2-web`: its `WebPort/deploy/build-play-site.mjs` with a checked-in recipe, `feat/deploy-bundle` on its main, and its notify workflow’s `SITE_DISPATCH_TOKEN`; Round 1031 brought `build-play-site.mjs` and `feat/deploy-bundle` to bf2-web's main. Still waiting: the runner's `.env` (`BF2WEB_REPO`, `BF2_EXPORT`; the six-hourly build stops with "the desktop needs: BF2WEB_REPO") and the dispatch token. The adopt stage runs whatever the build's result, so a build uploaded by hand is still picked up on the six-hourly check. Before 2026-10-29 a failed build skipped it, and main-32a7a627 went live by #1259 instead.
- **Hoth on `bf2-web`, next.** `src/data/bf2web/play.json` names the first build, `main-da7a51b3` (`bf2017-assets/sites/main-da7a51b3/`, 3,780 files, 2.2 GB, uploaded and checked by the owner’s desktop on 2026-10-28). Its `/export/` is the bucket’s `web/` (the export `BF2_EXPORT_ROOT` names; `DEPLOY.md`’s `extract/` copy isn’t needed); its `/ui-data/` is `library/ui/frontend/`, the nine front-end rulebooks, written by `bf2-web`’s `frontend/tools/extract.mjs` from the bucket’s `UI/**` and `Gameplay/Profiles/**` records and uploaded on 2026-10-28 (84 of those records came down from the bucket already unzipped under a `.json.gz` name; zip them again before running it). Smoke-tested that day in headless Chromium through `cloudflare/play.js` over the bucket: the page is cross-origin isolated, the front end opens, and the Arcade path reaches PLAY (Hoth_01, Skirmish Onslaught). The engine then boots and loads until three of Hoth’s cooked materials fail to compile: they `enable dual_source_blending`, which SwiftShader’s adapter lacks, and the engine stops (`prepared material program absent`). So `engineSupport` now requires the adapter features `texture-compression-bc` and `dual-source-blending`, and a browser without them gets the site’s world. Its only other misses are optional: `/profile/*` and `/ui-data/backdrop/backdrop.json`. Then check the Worker’s token may bind R2 (the deploy’s `wrangler deploy`) and play it on a real GPU; authentication is to come (the owner’s, 2026-10-28). `exportAllow` lists the export’s folders the front end and the HUD read: widen it from what a first run asks for. The site’s world still plays Galactic Assault: an Arcade (Onslaught) mode on `bf2web/arcadeRound.js`, `spawnWaves.js`, `spawnFinder.js` and `soldierHealth.js` (Hoth_01’s `TeamDeathmatch_Global` layer is in `maps/hoth.json`) would make the fallback the same game; the bot brain and the soldier controller are not translated (the engine runs them). The way back: the site sends the visitor with `?from=<galaxy world>` and the Worker adds `cloudflare/playReturn.js`’s script to the engine’s page, a link back in the corner on the game’s front end and, after a round’s Quit, the trip back to that world (without a `from`, the link goes to `/#/battlefront` and Quit stays the game’s). Checked headless on the build’s front end: the link shows top right, clear of the game’s own, and goes to `/#/galaxy/hoth`; the Quit path is held by its test to the play page’s order (the session’s record, then `play-menu`), not yet played.

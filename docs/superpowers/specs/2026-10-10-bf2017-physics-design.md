# The galaxy's physics from the game: Havok shapes, the soldier's body, bolts, ragdolls, vehicles and surfaces. The design

Date: 2026-10-10. Status: **design, written from a survey of the owner's Frosty export on the desktop and of what is on `origin/main` and in the open Battlefront PRs**; implementation sessions follow, one lane each (`docs/superpowers/plans/2026-10-10-bf2017-phaseP0-shapes.md`, `-phaseP1-body.md`, `-phaseP2-bolts-ragdolls.md`, `-phaseP3-vehicles.md`, `-phaseP4-surfaces.md`). The hand-off is `docs/superpowers/HANDOFF-battlefront.md`. It stands beside the asset designs (`2026-10-10-battlefront-2017-asset-pipeline-design.md`, PR #802; `2026-10-10-bf2017-levels-lighting-sabers-design.md`, PR #810) and the game design (`2026-10-10-battlefront-game-design.md`, PR #812), takes from them and gives to them, and rebuilds nothing of theirs.

## What the owner asked

“Look at BF2’s game logic and files and assets (we have all licences). Use Frosty and analyse the physics of the game and what we can do to add it to ours for the Star Wars galaxy and its meshes and textures. Multiple things are working on this repo with this Claude account and others: check and make sure. Architect a solution and implement with Claude Opus 5.5.”

So: the Star Wars worlds (`src/components/galaxy/surface/`) should collide, walk, fall, bounce, break and hit the way the 2017 game does, read from the game’s own physics data, on the game’s own meshes (lane L’s level packs) and textures (lane L’s KTX2). Nothing is decompiled: the game’s solver is Havok inside its executable; what is read is the **data that parameterises it** (the shapes, the character and vehicle tuning, the projectiles, the material grid) and the site runs it on its own engine.

### The physics GLB, read

`gltfpack 1.3` output, quantised (KHR_mesh_quantization: a node carries the scale, about 3e-5, and the translation that undo it) and meshopt-compressed as the rest of the web build. Nodes: `root<N>_<Class>` with extras `{ rootShapeIndex, havokClass }`; leaves `r<root>_i<instance>_p<part>_<kind>` with extras `{ havokClass, kind: 'convex' | 'mesh' | 'capsule' | 'sphere' | 'triangle', root, part, userData, instance, shapeTag, instanceFlags }`; one mesh per leaf (`POSITION` + indices, triangles; a convex leaf is its hull’s triangulated surface; a capsule and a sphere are tessellated and their radius is in `physics.jsonl`’s record). Materials `phys_slot<s>_idx<i>_<packed>` with extras `{ slot, packed, materialIndex, flags }`. Units metres, +Y up, the mesh’s own frame (the same as the visual model’s), so an instance transform from the map places both.

## What is on `main`, and what is in flight (the review)

- **The galaxy walks on `walker.js`**: a hand-rolled controller (`WALK`: walk 3.3, run 7.4, jump 5.4, gravity 15.5, step 0.55, radius 0.38) over `heightAt`, a list of circles and boxes (`createSolids`, filled by `placer.js` from each prop’s bounding box, by hand in the sites) and flat `floors`. A ride (`ride()`, `rides.js`’s hand table) hovers over `heightAt` and bounces off the same circles. Bolts (`lib/combat/bolt.js`) fly segments against `world.solids(a, b)` (a ray the world gives) and body capsules. Ragdolls (`lib/three/ragdollPhysics.js`) are a Verlet body on Meshy’s skeleton against a `collide` callback. None of this knows a hull, a stair, a doorway, a slope’s true normal or a wall of the game’s hangar: lane L’s levels would be walked through.
- **Rapier is on `main`**: `@dimforge/rapier3d-compat` 0.21.0 (pinned), `docs/stack/physics-rapier.md`, `src/lib/physics/` (`world.js`: bodies as data, fixed 1/60 step, floating origin, sensors and tags, the groups `floor`/`object`/`bumper`; `heightfield.js`; `vehicle.js`: the ray-cast car; `fromModel.js`, `props.js`, `pusher.js`, `catch.js`), `src/lib/three/colliders.js`. It is used by the universe’s landings and counted in `WORLD_MB` (1.7 MB, “never a phone”).
- **PR #781, “Rapier as the body”, was closed on 2026-10-10** on the owner’s direction: the galaxy surface would take its physics from the Battlefront drop, and a Rapier wiring of the old surface would interfere. Its *library* is worth keeping and “can come back on its own, without the galaxy-surface wiring, as a PR scope”: `character.js` (a kinematic capsule on `KinematicCharacterController`, `move(intent, dt)` on the substep hook, knock, jump, push-out), `queries.js` (budgeted ray, sweep, overlap, floorAt), `groups.js` (`character`, `hurtbox`, `zone`, the query-only `projectile`, `sight`), `budget.js`, `zones.js`, `hurtbox.js`. This design is that scope: the library comes back **because** the Battlefront drop needs a body that climbs the game’s stairs.
- **Lane L (PR #810, running)** builds the level pack from the map, the heightmap and the shapes and, in its task 4, `src/lib/level/collision.js`: `solidsOf(pack, cells)` for the walker (boxes and floor triangles) and `rapierShapes(pack, mesh)` “from the Havok record when the Rapier lane’s `fromModel.js` is on `main` (else the trimesh)”. It also lists `physics: { per mesh: convex shapes or 'trimesh' }` in `level.json` without saying how the shapes are read or budgeted. **Lane P0 below is that half**: it owns the pack’s physics section and `collision.js`’s Rapier side; lane L keeps `solidsOf` as the walker’s fallback and the one call site in `scene.js`. The lane L session is told in its hand-off section and by message.
- **The game design (PR #812)** wants, through its assets adapter, `loadPhysics(name) → { hulls, meshes }`, “Rapier trimeshes and hulls from `physics/`, built once per level”, bolts as projectiles with the game’s speed and gravity, bone capsules from `BoneCollision*`, ragdolls “on `lib/three/ragdollPhysics` with the game’s 15 bodies”, and its vehicles later. This design gives it all of that as libraries (`src/lib/physics/havok.js`, `soldier.js`, `ballistics.js`, `blast.js`, the capsule and ragdoll tables), so lane 5 wires and does not build. Its lane 0 extractor (`scripts/lib/bf2017-ebx.mjs`) is the parser these lanes’ rulebook builders stand on when it is merged; until then each builder carries the forty-line `loadAsset`/`deref` it needs and swaps.
- **Lane 1 (PR #810, running)** puts the heroes on the game’s skeleton in full (254 joints). The hit capsules and the ragdoll table are by the game’s bone names, so they fit those figures without a rename; Meshy figures keep `hurtbox.js`’s regions.
- **Lane V (#802, not started)** brings the vehicle models; lane P3 builds their bodies and waits for them.

## The approaches weighed

**A. Extend the walker: hulls as polygons, trimeshes as floors.** Rejected. The walker is a 2D push-out over a height function; the game’s levels are 3D (a hangar roof over a corridor, a ramp onto a platform, a tunnel mouth in the heightmap). Teaching the walker stacked floors and sloped hulls is writing a character controller, badly, a second time.

**B. Rapier for everything on every galaxy surface, now** (the closed #781, revived). Rejected as the first step. It wires the old surface (props by bounding box, hand sites) and the figures that still fight on the walker’s maths, and it was closed for interfering. The worlds that gain are the ones on the game’s levels; the rest lose nothing by waiting.

**C. Rapier as the game-level world’s collision, the game’s numbers as its tuning, the walker kept as the fallback (recommended).** On a world that has a level pack with a physics section (lane L’s worlds, Hoth first) and a device that loads Rapier (never a phone), the Havok shapes become fixed colliders streamed with lane L’s cells, the terrain a heightfield per cell, the player (and, opt-in, a figure) a character controller whose capsule, step, slope and speeds are `CharacterPhysicsData`’s; bolts fly on `bolt.js` with the projectile’s speed, gravity and drag against the physics world’s ray; a bolt’s hit knows the material it struck and picks the game’s effect; grenades and debris are dynamic hulls with the game’s mass and the material’s restitution; the dead fall as the game’s 15-body ragdoll; vehicles are bodies from their blueprints. On a world without a pack, or on a phone, the walker runs as it does today over `solidsOf`’s boxes, which are derived from the same shapes, so the walls still stop you. The rest of this page is C.

## The design

### 1. The shapes, packed and streamed (lane P0)

**The pack.** `scripts/bf2017-physics.mjs <map> --world <id> [--cell 128]` (run by lane L’s `bf2017-level.mjs` as its physics step, or alone on an existing pack) reads every mesh’s physics GLB named by the map (`<mesh>_Physics_Win32`, through `physics.jsonl`), undoes the quantisation, and writes into the level pack:

- `level.json.physics`: `{ version, materials: { <materialIndex>: { friction, restitution, tag } }, meshes: { <mesh>: { file, hulls, meshTriangles, capsules, spheres, bounds, partCount } }, cells: { "cx,cz": { bin, colliders, triangles } } }`.
- `physics/<mesh>.bin`: the shapes once, in the mesh’s frame: a header, then per shape `{ kind (u8), part (u8), material (u8), root (u8) }` and its data: a hull’s points (Float32 × 3n, at most 64 after `quickhull`’s reduction: a convex polytope’s stored points are its hull already; more than 64 is simplified to 64 by farthest-point sampling and the test proves the volume loss under 2%), a mesh’s points and Uint32 indices, a capsule’s two ends and radius, a sphere’s centre and radius.
- `physics/cells/<cx>_<cz>.bin`: which instances of the cell have shapes: `{ mesh index (u16), instance index into the cell’s draw bin (u32) }`, so no transform is stored twice (lane L’s cell bin has it).

A shape with `instanceFlags` or `userData` the exporter marks as not a collider (the `0xFFFF0000` user data on a mesh root’s leaves is the visual-only tag seen on decal planes; the reader lists what it dropped) is left out. A mesh root’s leaves are written as `mesh` kinds; a convex root’s as `hull`. A model with both (a vehicle, a hangar door) keeps both and the loader decides: statics take both, moving bodies hulls only (Rapier’s rule: a moving trimesh falls through the ground).

**The library.** `src/lib/physics/havok.js` (pure, no three.js): `readShapes(bin) → shapes[]`; `collidersOf(shapes, { statics = true }) → [{ shape: 'hull' | 'trimesh' | 'capsule' | 'ball', args, position, rotation, tag }]` in `world.js`’s collider form (a hull’s `args` its points; a trimesh’s its points and indices; a capsule’s `[halfHeight, radius]` with the rotation that lays it along its axis; the material index as the collider `tag` so a hit says what it struck); `instanceBody(colliders, position, quaternion, scale) → desc` (one fixed body per placed instance; a mirrored instance (negative x scale) flips its hull points and its trimesh winding); `cellBodies(pack, cell, instances) → desc[]`; `solidsOf(shapes, transform) → [{ box | circle }]` (the walker’s fallback: each hull’s footprint as a box turned by its yaw, with `top`, so stairs stay floors as the galaxy’s memory says).

**The stream.** `src/components/galaxy/surface/level/levelPhysics.js`: `createLevelPhysics({ physics, pack, loadBin }) → { addCell(key, bin, instances), removeCell(key), setTerrain(layer), stats(), dispose() }`, called by lane L’s `levelStream.js` beside `addCell`/`removeCell` on the scene; the terrain as `heightfield.js`’s cells from the `image` layer (near map, 64 m cells, 65 samples; a hole reads as the far map’s height, never 0); the far ring and the horizon have no colliders. **The budget**: per cell at most 400 colliders and 60,000 trimesh triangles on high and ultra, 200 and 30,000 on mid; a cell over it drops its lightest hulls first (by volume) and says so in `stats()`; low and phones load no physics (the walker over `solidsOf`). Fixed bodies cost nothing between steps; a cell’s bodies are removed when the cell goes.

**`collision.js`.** Lane L’s `src/lib/level/collision.js` gains `createLevelCollision({ pack, physics | null, ... })` returning `{ solids, floors, physics }`: with an engine, the stream above; without, `solidsOf` for the walker. One decision point, in `scene.js` where `createLevel` is made: `physics = !isPhone && tier !== 'low' && pack.physics ? await createPhysics(...) : null`.

### 2. The body (lane P1)

**The rules.** `src/data/bf2017/physics/soldier.json` built by `scripts/lib/bf2017-physics-rules.mjs` from the 13 `CharacterPhysicsData` records: per record `{ mass, radius, ascend, slide, slideGravity, jumpPenalty: { time, factor }, fallDistance, rays, poses: { stand | crouch | prone: { height, step, eye: [x, y, z], transitions } }, states: { onGround: { pose: { velocity, back, left, right, accelGain, decelGain, sprintGain, sprintMultiplier } }, inAir: { freeFall }, falling, sliding, climbing, swimming: { enter, exit }, jump: {...} } }`, every leaf with its `_source` (lane 0’s rule). The default soldier, read: mass 100, radius 0.3, ascend and slide 45°, stand height 1.7 and step 0.4, crouch 1.15 and 0.3, eye 1.55 (x −0.15: over the shoulder), stand walk 5.0 m/s (back 0.8), sprint × 1.5 (7.5 m/s), crouch 3.0, acceleration gain 0.35 and deceleration −15 (a stop in a few frames), the slide’s gravity scale 0.34, free fall 200, the ground ray from 1.2 to −1.0 (−4.0 in the air), jump penalty 0.1 s × 0.2. `JumpStateData`’s fields are read as found; if the record gives no jump speed the site’s 5.4 m/s stands as `source: "hand"`.

**The library.** `src/lib/physics/character.js`, `queries.js`, `groups.js`, `budget.js`, `zones.js` return from the closed branch as they were (library only, their tests with them); `src/lib/physics/soldier.js` (pure) turns a rules row into the controller’s options (`radius`, `halfHeight` = (height − 2 r) / 2, `step.height`, `slope.climb` = ascend, `slope.slide` = slide, `snap` = fallDistance, `gravity` the walker’s 15.5 so the feel matches the rest of the site until the jump row says otherwise) and gives `speedFor(row, { pose, sprint, back, strafe, wading })`, `accelFor(row, pose, toward)` (the gain × the difference, as the data’s gains read), `poseFor(input)` with the transition times, `slideOn(normal)`.

**The player.** `src/components/galaxy/surface/playerBody.js`: when the world has `physics`, the player is `createCharacter` with the row’s options; `walkIntent(state, input)` is `walker.js`’s input mapping (the stick relative to the camera, run, jump, heading) into the controller’s `{ vel, face, jump }`, the pose from crouch (a new input: C held, the touch button lane A’s HUD has room for), the state (`x, y, z, vy, grounded, speed, yaw, wading`) written back each frame so `scene.js`, the HUD and the legs read what they read today. Without `physics`, `walk()` as before. Knocks, the shore, the edge of the world and the water go through the body as #781’s hand-off describes. **Figures**: `bodyFor(figure, row)` is exported for lane 1’s walrus figures and #812’s lane 5 to opt into; the galaxy’s crowds and hostiles stay on the walker (over `solidsOf`) in this lane.

### 3. Bolts, blasts, ragdolls and hit zones (lane P2)

**Ballistics.** `src/data/bf2017/physics/projectiles.json`: per `ProjectileBlueprint` `{ id, kind: 'bolt' | 'missile' | 'grenade', speed, maxSpeed, gravity, drag, ttl, damping, impactImpulse, damage, body: { mass, restitution, friction }, blast: { inner, radius, impulse, damage, shockRadius, shockImpulse } | null, detonate: { onCollision, onTimeout, nearTarget } }`. `src/lib/combat/ballistics.js` (pure): `flight(spec, bolt, dt)` advances a bolt by speed, gravity and drag (the bolt’s velocity, not a fixed `speed · dt`); `bolt.js` gains an optional `ballistic` on its spec and calls it, so a blaster bolt (gravity 0) flies as today and a grenade arcs. `world.solids(a, b)` on a physics world is `queries.ray` (budgeted); on a walker world the height function as today. **Grenades** are dynamic hull bodies (`world.js`) with the row’s mass and the material’s restitution, their fuse the row’s `ttl`; **blasts** (`src/lib/physics/blast.js`): `applyBlast(physics, at, row)` impulses every awake body within `radius` (full inside `inner`, falling to the edge), kicks ragdolls within `shockRadius`, and returns who was hit for the damage path.

**Hit zones.** `src/data/bf2017/physics/bones.json` from `SkeletonCollisionData`: per set `{ bone, length, radius, offset, axis, reaction, hiLod, lowLod }`; `src/lib/physics/boneCapsules.js`: `capsulesOf(skeleton, set) → [{ a, b, r, region }]` on a figure whose bones carry the game’s names (lane 1’s), the hi-LOD set near and the low-LOD set far, as `bolt.js`’s `bodies` and `blade.js`’s targets; `hurtbox.js` returns from the closed branch for Meshy figures. A hit names its bone’s `AnimationHitReactionType` (`HRT_Head`, …) so the flinch and the headshot rule read the data, not a guess.

**Ragdolls.** `src/data/bf2017/physics/ragdoll.json` from `WSEACharacterPhysicsComponentData`: the 15 bodies by bone, `maxImpulse`, `impulseLifetime`, `dismemberment`; `src/lib/three/ragdoll2017.js`: the points and sticks `ragdollPhysics.js`’s `rigRagdoll` takes, built from those bones on the game’s skeleton (hinges at the knees and elbows as the Meshy table has them), `collide` through the physics world’s `floorAt` when there is one and the height function when not; the push from the bolt’s `impactImpulse` and the blast’s impulse, capped at `maxImpulse`.

### 4. Vehicles (lane P3, after lane V’s models and P0)

`src/data/bf2017/physics/vehicles.json`: per `VehicleBlueprint` `{ id, kind: 'hover' | 'walker' | 'flyer' | 'stationary', mass, centreOfMass, friction, damping, parts: [{ name, mass }], kill: { speed, topHitHeight }, lowAltitude, fleeRadius, physics: '<asset>' , crashed: ['<blueprint>'] }`; the handling numbers that the small `_Handling` layers do not hold (they are input scaling and a logic prefab) come from the blueprint’s abilities where the extractor finds them and from `rides.js`’s table as `source: "hand"` where it does not. `src/lib/physics/vehicleBody.js`: a **hover** (74-Z, X-34, the tauntaun’s rider) is a kinematic-velocity body driven by `walker.js`’s `ride()` numbers (the rules keep the feel) whose hull is the convex root, so it stops at the game’s walls and climbs the game’s ramps; a **walker** (AT-ST, AT-AT) is a kinematic animated body (lane 1’s clips move it) whose foot colliders are sensors that kill under `kill.speed` and shove with `pusher.js`; a **flyer** is the flight lane’s and gets only its hull; **stationary** turrets are fixed. **Debris**: `debris.js` spawns the crashed-part blueprints’ shapes as dynamic hulls at the vehicle’s pose on death, asleep after `catch.js`’s rest. Bolts meet a vehicle’s mesh root through `bolt.js`’s segment test against the hull’s triangles in the body’s frame (a trimesh never rides a moving body).

### 5. Surfaces (lane P4)

`src/data/bf2017/physics/materials.json` built by `scripts/lib/bf2017-materials.mjs` from a level’s grid: `{ default, materials: { <index>: { name, friction, restitution, penetration } }, pairs: { "<a>,<b>": { effect, effectBySpeed: [{ min, max, effect }], sound, decal, exitDecal, footprint, terrainDestruction } } }` for the indices the level’s shapes use; the material name is a hand column from the pair’s effect names (`FX_FootStep_ATAT_Sand` → sand; `Decal_Metal_ATST` → metal; snow, ice, rock, wood, flesh) with `source: "hand"` beside the index. `src/lib/physics/materials.js`: `materialOf(tag)`, `impactOf(materials, bolt, tag) → { effect, sound, decal }`, `frictionOf`. The surface: a bolt that hits a collider reads its `tag` and picks the site’s effect for that material (`fx.js`’s puff, spark, splash, scorch), a step on snow leaves `marks.js`’s print, a walker’s foot on sand the footstep effect. The terrain’s material is the terrain layer’s surface shader (the README’s guidance until the layer masks are decoded).

### 6. Performance, as an architecture

- **Colliders stream with the cells** (P0): nothing fixed is ever more than the near and mid cells’ worth; the far ring has none. Fixed bodies cost nothing between steps; the step is 1/60 with at most 4 substeps; the character moves on the substep hook. The budget per cell is in §1; `stats()` reports colliders, triangles, dropped, step time; `galaxy-check.mjs surface <world>` under `BUDGET=1` prints them beside the draw counts and holds `step` under 2 ms on the container (`cost.scenario.test.js`’s bench pattern).
- **Queries are budgeted** (`budget.js`): 64 rays a frame; a refused call keeps its last answer (the design in #781’s spec). Bolts take the ray; sight lines of the AI stay on the walker’s maths unless a lane moves them.
- **Memory**: a hull is at most 64 points; a trimesh is shared by every instance of its mesh (Rapier shares the shape when the same `TriMesh` is passed); the pack’s physics for Hoth is expected under 6 MB on disk (the map’s 599 meshes’ shapes, most under 2 KB).
- **No GPU cost**: nothing is drawn for physics; `?debug` draws wireframes from `stats()` only when asked.
- **Phones and low**: no Rapier, the walker over `solidsOf`; the same walls, no stairs.

## Departures and open assumptions

1. **The jump**: `JumpStateData`’s fields were not read in the survey (the classifier stopped it); P1 reads them and keeps the walker’s 5.4 m/s as `hand` if no speed is there. **Lane P1 found (PR #822)**: `JumpHeight` 1.1 m exists and is used; the on-ground stand walk is **3.8 m/s** (the survey’s 5.0 and 7.5 were the animation-controlled state’s pose, not the ground state’s), so §2’s 5.0/7.5 read as 3.8 and its sprint multiple; and Rapier’s autostep never climbs higher than the capsule’s radius, so `playerBody.js` steps the 0.4 m itself.
2. **Vehicle handling** is not in the `_Handling` layers (2 KB: input scaling and a prefab reference); where the blueprint’s abilities do not give a top speed, `rides.js`’s numbers stand as `hand` and the rulebook says so.
3. **Material names** are not in the data; the hand column is from the pairs’ effect names and is checked against what a bolt looks like on Hoth’s snow and the hangar’s floor.
4. **Cloth** is an EA binary and is not reproduced; capes stay the site’s own.
5. **Props’ destruction** was not surveyed; P3 covers crashed vehicle parts, and a later lane may read `DestructionVolumeAsset` and the props’ graphs.
6. **The surface’s hostiles and crowds stay on the walker** in these lanes; moving them is #781’s lane two, revived when a world on the game’s level has them (lane 5 of #812 is the first).
7. **Sequel-era records** are refused as lane 0 refuses them (`isSequel`); the physics of a First Order TIE is the same asset family and comes through the Original-era name.
8. **Bolts are not blueprints** (lane P2): the 114 `ProjectileBlueprint`s are missiles, grenades and charges; a blaster bolt is a `GameDataContainerAsset` holding a `WSBulletEntityData`, so `projectiles.json` reads both (333 rows). **Grenades have no body** in their record (no `RigidBodyData`), and their `InitialSpeed` (350) is not the throw: the world gives a grenade its mass and the weapon its speed.
9. **Ragdoll bodies are named by the component’s index fields**, which two components get wrong (the Ewok hero’s fifteen all say body 1); such a row is `partial` and falls back to the trooper’s bodies. The blueprint’s `BodiesNamesHashes` did not match FNV-1, FNV-1a or djb2 of the bone names.
10. **(P0) `0xFFFF0000` is not a visual-only tag**: a mesh root’s leaves carry `0xFFFF00NN`, NN an index (00 to 42 over Hoth), and each sits beside a convex root over the same piece. Both are kept; the budget drops such a trimesh first, as the detail.
11. **(P0) The budget per cell** is 400 colliders / 30,000 triangles on mid, 1,000 / 60,000 on high, 2,000 / 100,000 on ultra, measured on Echo Base (`docs/superpowers/evidence/bf2017-physics/p0/hoth.md`: 1,000 keep 88 to 98% of a cell’s hull volume at 0.15 ms a step).
12. **(P0) No physics cell bins**: a cell’s own bin and draws name its meshes; `physics.cells` keeps the counts only. Lane L’s real interfaces (PR #831) replace §1’s names: `collision.js` is lane L’s (`solidsOf(pack, draws, bin) → { floors, boxes }`), the shapes’ walker side is `lib/level/shapeSolids.js` in that form, and `levelPhysics` sits beside `level/colliders.js` with its `add`/`drop`, wired in `level/index.js`’s `onCell`/`onDrop`.
13. **(P0) The 64-point guard** is in `havok.js`, not `world.js` (the universe’s hulls are uncut models). A `convex_flat` leaf is read as a trimesh.

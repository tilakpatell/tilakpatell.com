# The galaxy in space on the game: every hull, sky and planet the galaxy draws is Battlefront II’s, and space is drawn in the game’s picture. The design

Date: 2026-10-11 (afternoon). Status: **design, by the orchestrating session for implementation sessions, one lane each.** It follows the picture-everywhere design (`2026-10-11-bf2017-picture-everywhere-design.md`), which put every Star Wars **surface** on the node renderer in the game’s picture, and the fifth design’s space lane (`2026-10-10-bf2017-every-asset-design.md` §Q, done: the space battles’ set pieces, the fighters, three skies, the globes). This page is about everything the galaxy draws **outside** the surfaces: the hub and its map, the planets from orbit, space and travel, the fleet and the war, the starfighter mode, the HUD.

## What the owner asked

“Make sure that the general Star Wars galaxy like the overall galaxy with assets and stuff also uses the bf2 high res assets and the galaxy looks like bf2.” Read as two things: (1) every model, map and sky the galaxy draws in space is the game’s where the game has one, at the game’s fidelity; (2) space is drawn the way the game draws its space battles: its picture, not the site’s.

## 2. The decision

1. **Where the game has a hull, the galaxy draws it; where it has none, the site’s stays and is named.** The kits `bf2017-space.mjs` assembles become `models.js`’s rows for the Star Destroyer, the MC80, the Venator, the CR90 and the Providence at the fleet’s cut (`bf2017-fleet.mjs`’s `space` rows, as the Lucrehulk and the Arquitens already are), with their far copies by `galaxy-lod.mjs`; the Nebulon-B’s HQ row is the game’s native model; the X-wing, the TIE interceptor, Slave I and the Falcon take lane V’s native files; the scimitar and the TIE striker are imported through `bf2017-import.mjs` as lane V did its fighters. The 2017 comparison sheet that kept Daniel Andersson’s ships (`fleet.webp`) predates the kits: it is re-shot with the kits beside them, and the kit wins by the owner’s rule (the game’s assets, at the game’s fidelity), not by the sheet. The credits rows retire with the files.
2. **The player flies the game’s ship from the game’s cockpit.** `universe/shipModels.js`’s X-wing and Falcon rows take `surface/xwing.glb` and `surface/falcon.glb`; the cockpit view (`scene.js`’s `buildCab`) draws the game’s cockpit GLB for the kit flown where lane V imported one, the procedural cab only for the RV and the cruiser.
3. **Space is drawn in the game’s picture, on the node renderer above low.** The `galaxy` module goes `'nodes'` the way `galaxy-surface` did in lane T2: node twins for the nineteen GLSL files under `galaxy/nodes/`, the classic set reached through an import marked `classic` that `shadingClosure.js` leaves out, `shading.test.js` pinning the names; `pickBackend` gives it the same table as the surface (WebGPU; `nodes-webgl` on high and ultra without WebGPU; classic on low, and on mid without WebGPU). The post chain is `light/post.js`’s for the tier (bloom, flare, TRAA/TAAU, the grade) ending in the system’s picture: the `VE_SB_*` record of the system’s space map read by `bf2017-light.mjs` (tone map, bloom numbers, flare, fog, sun) applied by `applyGameLight`, as lane P did for the surfaces; a system without a space map takes the nearest record (Endor’s for the Rim, Kamino’s for the Core) with the choice written beside it.
4. **The skies are the game’s whole set.** Every space panorama in the drop is published through `bf2017-sky.mjs --panorama` and placed by `skyPanorama.js`: Endor’s second sky and the celestial and cinematic star fields for the systems without a sky of their own, the moons and the debris background as their system’s layers; the procedural band and nebulae stay only as the low tier’s and under a panorama where the record paints none. Kashyyyk and Kamino get a sky; low keeps none (the row).
5. **The planets as the game draws them, as far as the drop allows.** Lane E5 places the six unplaced worlds and converts their whole-planet maps; this design adds the `SS_Planet_*` mix once the desktop answers (the ask stands in the hand-off), and until then the skin shader keeps lane K’s mix with its `_source`. A world with only a lit picture stays procedural, named in the hand-off.
6. **The map and the HUD wear the game’s front end.** The holomap’s dot for every skinned planet is a globe rendered from its skin at the front end’s size (96 px, lane K’s cut) by `bf2017-sky.mjs --globes`; the four sides that the game has take its faction icons through `icon('side:*')`; the galaxy’s buttons take a game sprite where `icons.json` has the same meaning and keep Remix where it has none; the war HUD’s bars and lines take the Starfighter Assault widgets’ layout (`starfighterHud.js`) where the screens lane recorded one.
7. **Gates.** `npm run lint`, `npm test`, `npm run build`, `node scripts/health.mjs --check --skip build`, `npm run coverage:bf2017`; `BUDGET=1 BUDGET_REPORT=1 QUALITY=<tier> node scripts/galaxy-check.mjs space <every system>` at low, mid, high and ultra, within its rows (a row breached by the game’s hulls is a finding with its numbers, never a raised row without its reason); `node scripts/starfighter-check.mjs <system> <quality>` on Endor, Kamino, Naboo and Fondor; `anim-check` where a ship has clips. Shots in `docs/superpowers/evidence/bf2017-space/` beside the game’s frames the owner picks.
8. **What stands.** The era rule (the Resurgent, the First Order’s TIEs, Starkiller, D’Qar and the Unknown Regions stay out); binaries published by hash, never through git; the keys never printed; the budgets’ reasons; no new library without its `docs/stack/` page; British spelling and curly quotes; `_source` on numbers.

## 4. The seams

- **Lane 4 (vehicles) owns `gameplay/vehicles/air`:** S1 imports the scimitar and the TIE striker through lane V’s `bf2017-import.mjs` path and moves their rows to `space` in `bf2017-owners.mjs` with the reason, nothing else of lane 4’s.
- **The space lane’s kits** are read as they are; a kit that needs a second cut (the ISD’s 83 parts at the fleet’s 24,000 triangles) gets it from `bf2017-space.mjs --fleet`, not a new assembler.
- **Lane T2’s files** (`src/runtime/backend.js`, `shadingClosure.js`, `shading.test.js`, `surface/nodes/*`): S2 adds the `galaxy` module’s rows and twins beside them, changing none of the surface’s.
- **Lane W (the desktop):** the two VP6 films, the `SS_Planet_*` shader assets and the six not-uploaded space textures are its jobs; S2 wires the consumers and lists the asks.
- **Lane E5:** owns the six systems’ rows, skins and surfaces; S3 reads whatever skins are on `main` when it runs its globes, and E5 re-runs `--globes` for its own.

## 5. Risks

- **The node port of nineteen shaders** is the largest piece; lane T’s surface port took one session and left the classic set as the fallback. S2 does the same: a twin a file, each pinned by a shot against its classic frame (PSNR over 40 dB on the fixture, as G1 measures), the classic branch kept and reached only through the `classic` marker.
- **Draw calls in space.** The kits at the fleet’s cut add parts where one Sketchfab hull was one draw; the budget rows for `space` are measured before and after, and a breach is written as a finding for the owner, not fixed by a raised row.
- **The sheet.** Daniel Andersson’s ships were kept because they looked better than the game’s backdrop ships on a sheet made before the kits existed; the kits are the game’s own close-up hulls. If the re-shot sheet shows a kit worse than the site’s (a missing part, a wrong map), that part is a finding to the space lane’s assembler, not a reason to keep the third-party file.

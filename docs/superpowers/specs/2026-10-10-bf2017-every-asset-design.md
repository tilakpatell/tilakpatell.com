## What the owner asked

"Make sure all assets on the Supabase bucket for Star Wars Battlefront 2 are used by our site. All of them: the maps, the textures/meshes, everything. Architect a way to add them to all the worlds and use them where it's fit. Then implement with Opus 5.5."

**What I understood, and what I assumed** (the owner corrects either):

- "All of them" is a measurable claim, so the first thing this design builds is the measure: a ledger that names, for every object in the bucket, the site file that consumes it, or the lane that will, or the rule that excludes it. The gate is that nothing is left in the fourth state. Without the ledger "all" is a feeling; with it, it is a number that goes to zero.
- "Where it's fit" keeps three standing rules: the era rule (`.claude/skills/autopilot/SKILL.md`: no sequel trilogy; `isSequel` in `scripts/lib/bf2017-manifest.mjs`, repeated in four designs and the decision entry), so the sequel era's 2,324 models, 28 maps and their clips stay out and the ledger says so; the uploader's own scaffolding (`test/`, `testranges`, `placeholders`, `tobedeleted_tempintransition`, the `RootLevel` dev maps) is not content; Everything else is used: the owner holds the licence for everything in the drop, the fonts included (said on 2026-10-10 after the first draft had excluded the commercial faces; that exclusion is withdrawn). The era rule is one list in one file; if the owner lifts it, the ledger's `excluded` rows become `unowned` and lane E5's recipe covers the sequel maps the same way.
- "Add them to all the worlds" means both: every Star Wars world the site has takes what the drop holds for it, and the drop's worlds the site lacks (Felucia, Kessel, Sullust, Pillio, Vardos, Fondor) are added as systems with their maps and skins.
- The desktop's two gaps (the 11,101 textures never queued, the sound never exported) are the fourth design's lane D and are not re-planned here; the ledger lists them as `not-uploaded`, apart from the bucket's own count, so "all assets on the bucket" is measured against what is on the bucket.
- The owner spawns nothing by hand: this session spawns the Opus 5.5 sessions from the design branch, as the second and third designs did.

## 2. The approaches weighed

1. **One lane per bucket folder, in order, until the folders are empty.** Rejected: the folders are not the consumers. A map's extras feed four site systems (lights, decals, actors, effects); a clip family feeds the player, the NPCs and the end screen. Lanes by consumer, not by folder.
2. **Extend the running designs' lanes with the leftovers.** Rejected for scale: lane L is one world a session; the cast-left lanes are kinds; neither has a home for 44 maps, 8,000 objects or 116 films. The running lanes keep their files; this design's lanes take what no lane names, and the ledger is the contract between them (a row owned by a running lane is `owned`, never touched here).
3. **A ledger as the gate, a level factory, and four consumer lanes for what no level holds** (chosen). The ledger (Z) makes "all" a number. The factory (E) is lane L's script generalised and run across every usable map, with the pack carrying everything the map has (lights, decals, actors, vehicle spawns, effect spawns, tracks, probes, scatter table, shapes), so one loader draws every world and the running fidelity lanes find their inputs beside the pack. The library (O) indexes the 8,000 placeable objects by set and biome and dresses the worlds that have no map. The space lane (Q) does for the five space levels and the capitals what E does on the ground. The front-end lane (M) takes the films, the fonts, the icons, the strings and the UI bitmaps into the galaxy's own screens and prepares the game's HUD. The clips lane (A) gives every unowned skeleton and clip family a consumer.

## 3. The design, by lane

### Lane Z: the coverage ledger

**Files**: `scripts/bf2017-coverage.mjs` (reads the manifests from the bucket with the keys, or `--root <export>/web_opt` on the desktop; writes the ledger), `scripts/lib/bf2017-coverage.mjs` (pure: the classification; tested on a fixture of a hundred rows), `scripts/lib/bf2017-owners.mjs` (the owners table: path prefix or name pattern → `{ lane, design, state }`), `docs/superpowers/evidence/bf2017-coverage/ledger.md` (the table by part and by lane, with the four counts) and `ledger.json.gz` (every object's row), `scripts/lib/bf2017-coverage.test.mjs`.

**Rows.** One per object in `models.jsonl` (a model with all its LOD files), `anims.jsonl`, `textures.jsonl` (sources) plus the uploaded derived files, `physics.jsonl`, `collision/`, `terrain.jsonl`, `maps/index.json` (a map, and each extras kind as a row: lights, decals, actors, vehicles, effects), `maps/terrain_scatter/`, `animtracks/`, `misc.jsonl` (movies, fonts, svg, strings), `data.tsv` (by top folder and type, not by record: 84,000 rows would say nothing).

**States**, one per row, in this order: `used` (a consumer on `main` names it: the galaxy manifest's `from`, a credit's `source`, a level pack's `meshes`/`tex`, a clip pack's `extras.source`, `planetSkins.json`, a light JSON's probe ids, `bf2017Fx.js`, a rulebook leaf's `_source`, a library row); `owned` (the owners table names a lane and its design PR); `excluded` (the era rule, scaffolding); `not-uploaded` (in a manifest, no row in the bucket's listing); else `unowned`. The owners table is the design's commitment: every lane in this document and the open ones (#839's D, B, Y, I, T, W; #836's N, U; #812's 1 to 7; #817's P3; #819's M) has its prefixes.

**The gate.** `node scripts/bf2017-coverage.mjs --check` fails when `unowned > 0` or when an `owned` row's lane is merged and the row is still not `used` (the owners table carries each lane's `merged` PR when it lands; a lane that merges without consuming what it owned is the next design's first finding). CI runs the check against the committed `ledger.json.gz` (no keys); the fetch form runs on the desktop or in a session with the keys and refreshes the file. The hand-off's status table gains the four counts after every merge.

### Lane E: the level factory (every usable map a pack; every pack everything the map holds)

**E0, the factory.** `scripts/bf2017-level.mjs` generalised and the pack extended, proven on Endor_01 and on Hoth's base interior, then run by E1 to E5 one line a map.

- **Hosting.** A pack's bytes leave git: `assets-publish.mjs`'s `gameFiles` takes `public/models/galaxy/bf2017/levels/**` (today it is in git and mirrored by `assets-upload.mjs`'s `REMOTE`; Hoth's 898 files, 53 MB, go out in E0's first commit), `galaxyAssets.json` names them, the gitignore block hides them; `levelPack.js`'s `packUrl` resolves through `galaxyAssets.json` as `modelUrlFor` does, then `withFallback`. `level.json` and the README stay committed (small, and the tests read them).
- **Districts.** A site may hold more than one map: `SITES[id].districts = [{ id, name, level, land, line }]`. The surface route takes `?district=<id>`; the system's panel offers "land at" per district; a district's door on the surface (`zones`' shape, `to: { district }`) walks between two maps of one world without the ship (Mos Eisley to Jabba's palace; Theed's streets to the palace; the Death Star's hangar to its interior). The default district is the site as it is today, so every existing route keeps its meaning.
- **Interiors as packs.** `--inside <sub-levels>` writes a zone pack: the same stream, its own origin, no terrain, the zone's `bounds` from the pack's extent, its door at the map's own door. Hoth's buried base (14,535 instances lane L left out) is the first; Jabba's palace, Theed's palace, the Death Star's interior, the Venator's, the CR90's and the Star Destroyer's (M2FON) follow in their worlds' lanes.
- **The pack carries everything the map holds**, each part a file beside `level.json`, read by the loader it belongs to and by no other: `lights.json` (`bf2017-lights.mjs`, already written: run it; lane R's `placed.js` draws it); `decals.json` (the 13,984 placed decals' transforms, kind (volume box or projected) and texture, in the shape #844's lane Q4 `scripts/bf2017-decals.mjs` writes; Q4 owns the renderer, `src/lib/three/decals/` with `createDecals({ scene, pack, loader, tier })`, and E0 only hands it each cell's rows); `actors.json` (the map's 3,425 placed creatures, droids and civilians as life rows the site's `actors.js` reads: kind by the game's blueprint name through `crewList.js`'s rows, position, yaw; a kind the site lacks is listed, not placed); `vehicles.json` (the 2,925 vehicle spawns as `rides`/`walkers` rows by the catalogue's kinds); `effects.json` (the 52,027 effect spawns by `EffectBlueprint` name and transform: fidelity X's `createEffects` input, and until X merges, nothing is drawn); `tracks.json` (the level's `animtracks`: doors, lifts, cranes; `src/lib/three/tracks.js` evaluates the Hermite keys and drives the named instance); `probes.json` (every reflection volume of the level at 64², its bounds; `probeEnv.js` picks the nearest, lane R's probe grid takes them all); `shadowCache` (the far cache's path and the sun frame, fidelity S's); `scatter.json` (the terrain's scatter table, fidelity N's input, as that design says); the Havok shapes streamed by cell (P0's loader, run as Hoth's is); and where a mesh has no Havok shape, its `web/collision/` mesh as the solid (and on the tiers without Rapier, the collision meshes of the near cells as the walker's solids in place of the derived boxes). A part absent from a map is an empty file, never a missing one.
- **Detail maps and the materials' recipes** are #844's lane Q1 (`web/materials.jsonl`, 30,517 materials, into `recipes.json` beside each pack; `gameMaterial.js` on the node renderer): E0 adds no detail-normal path; its edits to `levelGltf.js` and `levelScene.js` stay additive so Q1's and Q2's (`ground.json`) merge cleanly.
- **Spots and yaw** per map come from the map's own spawn points (`data/`'s spawn areas, lane 0's extractor): the default district lands where the game's first team spawns; a district's spot is stated in its line in `sites/`.

**E1 to E5, the worlds** (one session each, after E0; each world a PR; a world's `sites/*.js` gains `level`, `districts`, and loses the built kit rows the level replaces, as Hoth did):

- **E1**: Tatooine (Tatooine_01 Mos Eisley as the default; Tatooine_02; JabbasPalace_01 as a district with its interior; the cantina's band waits on lane A), Yavin (Yavin_01; the temple's interior if its sub-levels hold one).

## 8. Open assumptions, marked

- **A1.** `galaxyAssets.json` can carry a level pack's hundreds of files (Hoth's 898) without slowing the site's start: the manifest is read once and a pack's rows are prefixed, so `packUrl` does one lookup a file. If the manifest's size matters (it is 1,855 lines for 370 files today), E0 writes one manifest per pack (`levels/<world>/assets.json`) and the loader reads it with `level.json`.
- **A2.** The decals' textures are in the bucket for the maps' `decals[]` (`FX/Decals` 53 and `objects/props/_decals`); the 11,101 never-queued maps include some; a decal whose texture is missing is skipped and counted in the README.
- **A3.** The campaign SP maps' sub-levels stand on their own without the mission's logic (the dumps' `SubWorldReference` resolve by bundle name, as the desktop's `build_maps.py` did); a sub-level that holds only a set piece is a district's room or the library's.
- **A4.** Confirmed by the owner on 2026-10-10 ("No sequel but use everything else"): the era rule stands; everything else in the drop is used.
- **A5.** Withdrawn: the owner holds the licence for every font; all 23 ship (lane M).

# Natural worlds: real ground, rivers that run, grass that bends, and a car on it. The design

Date: 2026-10-08. Status: design, written from the owner's brief by an architecting session, for an Opus 5.5 implementation session (phases in order, one pull request each). The plan is `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-10-08-natural-worlds.md`; the hand-off is `docs/superpowers/HANDOFF-worlds.md`; the reading behind it is `docs/research/2026-10-08-folio-2025-physics-terrain-streaming.md`.

## What the owner asked

"Research how the physics engine as well as the other portions of his codebase like how he makes the rivers and stuff look so natural and how we can add it to ours. Then really architect how we can implement it for our codebase with the universes and worlds." And, while the infinite-worlds work was in flight: "Another issue and PR is running how to make our worlds infinite and chunk loading. See how he does that too and how we can add it to make ours better."

"His" is Bruno Simon's folio-2025. Three things in it are asked for: the rigid-body physics and the car that drives on it; the look of the land (the rivers in particular); and whatever he does about size and streaming. The target is "the universes and worlds": the tilakverse's planets you land on, which the infinite-worlds design (`2026-10-07-infinite-worlds-design.md`) is making endless and seeded.

## Where the site is today

Read from the code in this session.

- **No physics engine.** Nothing simulates mass, suspension or wheel contact. Albuquerque's car (`albuquerque/world/rules.js`'s `stepCar`) is a planar bicycle model over an analytic height, its tilt faked from four ground samples; the galaxy's rides (`galaxy/surface/walker.js`'s `ride`) are a velocity, a heading and a spring to `ground + hover`. A cannon-es toy-physics layer was designed (`2026-10-06-baked-look-and-toy-physics-design.md` §4, for Albuquerque) and never built.
- **Three of Bruno's pieces are already here**, ported from the same folio: `lib/three/grass.js` (his toroidally wrapped one-triangle blades), `lib/three/wind.js` (his two-octave wind that everything reads), `lib/three/groundmap.js` and `lib/three/house.js` (his mask-driven ground, and his shade-as-a-colour, fog-as-the-sky and ground bounce). What is missing round them is the water, the trees, the tracks and the car.
- **Terrain is procedural and bounded.** The galaxy surface's `terrain.js` sums layers (`swell, hills, dunes, mesas, ridges, mountains, channels, island`), reads them back exactly as drawn (`heightGrid`, `heightAt`), and covers ±640 m walkable to 9 km of horizon. Rivers are its `channels` layer: a trench where a noise crosses zero, flooded by one flat water level per world. There is no river that runs downhill, no bank, no flow.
- **Water** is Gerstner waves on a plane (`galaxy/surface/water.js`, `ocean.js`) with a JS twin for floating things. No shore band, no refraction, no ripple contours.
- **The runtime** (`src/runtime/`) gives a world one renderer, a variable-step loop (dt capped at 50 ms), input, quality, saves, assets, audio; and, on the branch of PR #600 (infinite worlds, Phase 3), three chunk services: `rt.chunks` (a grid that says which cells to ask for and drop), `rt.workers` (a pool with one message protocol and cancel), `rt.origin` (a floating origin moved in whole 50,000 m cells, broadcast as the `'origin'` event). Minecraft is the one world on them.
- **The infinite-worlds plan's Phase 5** ("Landing on the Expanse") is thin where this design is thick: flat fbm cells of 64 m, props scattered per cell, the walker from `footScene.js`. This design is that phase, widened, brought forward, and made the reference for every planet surface after it.
- **The quality tiers** (`lib/device.js`'s `BUDGETS`, `lib/three/pace.js`) and the GPU-work queue of the smooth-worlds design (PR #598, parts 2 and 3) are the frame budget every piece here must live under.

## What Bruno does, and what of it we take

The research note has the numbers. The decisions:

| His mechanism | Ours |
|---|---|
| Rapier (wasm) stepped once a frame at the frame's delta, time scale 2 | Rapier (the `-compat` build, no bundler plugin), stepped at a fixed 1/60 s with an accumulator and a cap of 4 substeps, time scale a world setting (1 by default). Loaded inside the world's `create`, so only that route pays for it. |
| `DynamicRayCastVehicleController` on a chassis of three cuboids, the mass box's centre of mass 0.5 m low, a zero-mass bumper in its own collision group | The same, with his starting numbers verbatim (`suspensionStiffness 25`, `maxSuspensionForce 150`, `frictionSlip 0.9`, `engineForce 300`, soft top speed, idle brake 0.06, reverse brake, auto-unflip after 3 s) as the tuning table a world overrides. |
| Suspension rest length and stiffness set per wheel per frame (jump, low-rider) | Kept: `vehicle.suspension(i, 'low' \| 'mid' \| 'high')`. |
| One 192 m heightfield from a Blender mesh; props spawn asleep; awake bodies outside the camera's ground radius are put to sleep | A heightfield collider **per 64 m cell**, made from the same heights the cell is drawn from, added when the cell is within the physics radius (1 cell round the car) and removed with it; props per cell as sleeping bodies; his sleep-outside-the-radius rule. |
| One RGBA mask (R paving, G grass, B water depth) read by the floor, the grass, the water, the snow, the leaves and the light bounce | A mask **per cell** (128² texels, 0.5 m), the same four channels plus water level and flow, made in the worker with the heights, read through one GLSL function by every layer, as his. |
| Rivers authored in Blender as depth in the mask; a flat water quad at −0.3 m; the floor's depth gradient shows through the shallows under a blur, with animated contour bands, ice and splashes | Rivers **generated**: sources seeded per 1,024 m region, traced downhill on the terrain field with a meander, widening as they go, ending in the sea or a lake; the trace carves the bed and the bank into the heights and writes depth, level and flow into the mask. The water surface is a mesh per cell at the water *level* (a river runs downhill; a lake is flat), and his shallows look (depth gradient, contour bands, blur) is kept whole. |
| Grass: 78,400 one-triangle blades wrapped round the view, coloured as the ground, flattened by the tracks render target | `lib/three/grass.js` as it is, with the tracks render target added as the one thing it lacks. |
| Trees: 80 cut-out cards in a sphere with sphere-blended normals, cards facing the fixed camera direction, two-tone by `n·l`, cut-out rotated by the wind; trunks instanced; fixed cylinder colliders | A `puffs.js` that makes those card-spheres as an InstancedMesh per species through `lib/three/foliage.js`'s normals and wind, placed per cell from the mask's grass and slope, LOD through `lib/three/lod.js`. |
| Leaves (compute), wind lines, flowers, snow, rain, day and year cycles, weather | Wind lines and falling leaves (CPU, a few hundred, wrapped round the view) in this design; flowers, snow, rain, the year and the weather are Phase 4 or later. |
| Tracks: wheel contact points in a ring-buffer texture, drawn as ribbons by a top-down camera into a 512² target over 40 m | The same, as `lib/three/tracks.js`. |
| The view: FOV 25, an orbit at `phi 0.31π`, radius 15 to 30, a focus magnet, zoom-out with speed; the "optimal area" (the screen's four corners on the ground) sizes the floor, the grass, the water, the shadow camera and the fog | A `view.js` with that camera and that area, the area handed to the grass (`size`), the water quad, the leaves and the shadow camera. |
| The look: shade as a saturated colour, fog as a radial gradient that is also the background, ground bounce, a palette, bloom at threshold 1, a tilt-shift blur | `lib/three/house.js` already does shade, fog and bounce. The depth gradient, the waterline and the shallows blur are new. No tilt-shift, no bloom in this design. |
| Finite world, everything loaded up front, no priority | `rt.chunks`, `rt.workers`, `rt.origin` from infinite worlds Phase 3; every cell's work in the worker; the GPU queue of smooth worlds when it lands. |

## The pieces

Four libraries and one world. The libraries are pure where they can be and tested in Node; the world is the reference consumer, and the fandom landings and the galaxy surfaces adopt the libraries after (Phase 4, not in this plan's PRs).

### 1. `src/lib/land/`: the lie of the land, as data, per cell

Pure JavaScript, no three.js, runs in a worker and in Vitest. Everything is deterministic from a seed and integer cell coordinates, so neighbouring cells agree without talking and a cell is the same on every device.

- `spec.js`: `landSpec(seed, type = 'temperate') → LandSpec`. The knobs for one planet: `sea` (the sea level, metres; `-Infinity` for a dry world), `relief` (a list of `galaxy/surface/terrain.js`-style layers with their scales and heights; the layer functions move to `lib/land/layers.js` so both can share them), `rivers: { perRegion, width, depth, meander }`, `palette: { dirt, grass, sand, rock, shallow, deep, shadow }` (linear colours), `kit` (which prop set), `gravity`, `wind: { angle, strength }`, `sun: { elevation, azimuth, colour }`. Types: `temperate`, `desert`, `ice`, `ocean`, `volcanic` (the Expanse's planet types map onto these in infinite worlds Phase 5).
- `layers.js`: the galaxy's `LAYERS` moved here unchanged (the galaxy's `terrain.js` imports them back, nothing it does changes), plus `fieldAt(spec, x, z) → height`, the sum, with no flats and no rivers.
- `rivers.js`: `regionRivers(spec, seed, rx, rz) → River[]`. A region is `REGION = 1024` m (16 × 16 cells). From a hash of `(seed, rx, rz)`: `spec.rivers.perRegion` sources (0 to 3) placed by Poisson disc on the region, each on a local high of `fieldAt`; each traced downhill in `STEP = 8` m steps by steepest descent with momentum `0.6` and a meander term `spec.rivers.meander × noise(along)` across the slope, until it reaches `spec.sea`, or a pit (the trace can't descend `0.2` m in `3` steps: it ends in a **lake**, a disc of radius `20 + 40·hash` m at the pit, its level the pit's height plus `1.0`), or `4,000` m of length. A river's width grows with its length: `w = width × (0.6 + 0.4·min(1, length / 1500))`; its depth likewise. A `River` is `{ points: Float32Array (x, z, level, width, depth per point), lake: { x, z, r, level } | null }`. Because the field is global noise and the trace is deterministic, the 3 × 3 regions round a cell give every river that can reach it, traced identically by each.
- `cell.js`: `makeCell(spec, seed, cx, cz) → Cell`, the one function the worker runs. `CELL = 64` m (the infinite-worlds constant; cells are `[cx·64, (cx+1)·64)`). It returns, as transferables:
  - `heights: Float32Array(65 × 65)` at 1 m, including both edges (so neighbours share a row exactly), `fieldAt` carved by the rivers of the 3 × 3 regions round it: for the nearest river point within `2w`, the bed `h = min(h, level − depth × smoothstep(w, 0, d))` and the bank eased `h = lerp(h, level + 0.3, smoothstep(2w, w, d) × 0.5)`; a lake carves a bowl to `level − depth` inside `r`; under the sea nothing is carved (the sea has its own floor from the layers).
  - `water: Float32Array(65 × 65)` the water surface height at each vertex, or `NaN` where there is none: the river's `level` within `w × 1.2` of it, the lake's `level` inside `r × 1.1`, `spec.sea` wherever `heights` is below it.
  - `mask: Uint8Array(128 × 128 × 4)` at 0.5 m: R paving (0 in this design; a world's roads and pads write here), G grass (1 on gentle slopes above the water, falling to 0 over `slope > 0.55`, within `1.5` m of water, below the sea, and under `spec.palette.sand`'s beach band), B water depth 0 to 1 (`(water − height) / MAX_DEPTH`, `MAX_DEPTH = 3`), A flow (the river's tangent angle at the nearest point, `0..255 → 0..2π`; `0` on still water).
  - `props: [{ kind, x, y, z, yaw, scale }]`, 0 to `spec.kit.perCell` per cell by a seeded Poisson disc over the cell, placed where the mask allows (`trees` on grass, `rocks` on slopes and banks, `crates` a few near flat ground), with the height sampled from `heights`.
  - `cellMesh(heights, { step = 1 | 2 }) → { positions, normals, indices }`: a grid of `64/step` quads with a `2` m skirt down every edge (a hidden wall that covers the crack where a 1 m cell meets a 2 m cell), normals by central differences on `heights`.
  - `heightAt(cell, x, z)` and `waterAt(cell, x, z)`: bilinear reads in the cell's own frame, the same triangles the mesh draws.
- `worker.js` is the world's (section 5); the library has no worker of its own.

Tests pin: adjacent cells share edge heights exactly; a traced river never climbs (`level` non-increasing along `points`); a river that reaches the sea ends within `STEP` of it; a lake's level is above its bed everywhere inside `r`; `mask` G is 0 wherever `water` is not NaN; `heightAt` equals the vertex height at vertices and the triangle plane between; `cellMesh` index count is `(64/step)² × 6 + skirts`; `makeCell` is deterministic (two calls, identical bytes) and under `40` ms for a cell in Node (the budget the worker lives under).

### 2. `src/lib/physics/`: Rapier, Bruno's way

`@dimforge/rapier3d-compat` (one new runtime dependency; the wasm is inlined, so Vite needs no plugin and the worker needs nothing either). Imported dynamically by `createPhysics` (or `preload`), never at module scope, so no page but the one driving pays the download (4.3 MB of JS with the wasm inlined as base64, about 1.7 MB gzipped, measured; the route's `WORLD_MB` says so). Runs in Node, so every module here is tested against the real engine.

- `world.js`: `createPhysics({ gravity = -9.81, timeScale = 1, maxSubsteps = 4 }) → Promise<Physics>`. `Physics = { RAPIER, world, step(dt) → steps taken, add(desc) → Body, remove(body), onOrigin(shift) (every body translated by −shift in one call, velocities kept), sleepOutside(centre, radius), dispose() }`. `step` runs the accumulator at `1/60 × timeScale`, caps the backlog at `maxSubsteps` (a tab coming back doesn't explode), drains the contact-force events and calls each body's `onHit(force, at)` (force normalised by the pair's mass, his way). `add(desc)` is his `getPhysical` as data: `{ type, position, rotation, canSleep, sleeping, linearDamping = 0.1, angularDamping = 0.1, mass, friction = 0.2, restitution = 0.15, group: 'floor' | 'object' | 'bumper', onHit, hitThreshold = 15, colliders: [{ shape: 'cuboid' | 'ball' | 'cylinder' | 'hull' | 'trimesh' | 'heightfield', args, position, rotation, mass, centreOfMass, friction, restitution, group }] }`. His three groups verbatim (`floor` meets all; `object` meets all and bumper; `bumper` meets object only). A `Body = { body, colliders, initial, reset() (his protocol: disable, place with wakeUp false, re-enable next step, re-sleep if it began asleep), sleeping, position(out), quaternion(out) }`.
- `heightfield.js`: `addHeightfield(physics, { heights, n = 65, size = 64, x, z, friction, restitution }) → Body` as a `floor` fixed body, Rapier's `ColliderDesc.heightfield(n − 1, n − 1, heights, { x: size, y: 1, z: size })` with the row order Rapier wants (the test shoots rays at 200 random points and asserts the hit height equals `lib/land`'s `heightAt` within `1e-3`). `removeHeightfield` is `physics.remove`.
- `vehicle.js`: `addVehicle(physics, spec = CAR) → Vehicle`. His chassis (three cuboids, the mass box with its centre of mass `0.5` low, the bumper in `bumper`), his wheels (`offset (0.9, 0, 0.75)`, radius `0.4`, the four controller numbers) through `world.createVehicleController`, forward `+x`. `CAR` is his table verbatim and a world passes its own. `Vehicle = { chassis, controller, drive({ throttle, steer, brake, boost }, dt) (his pre-physics: soft top speed `force / (1 + overflow)`, idle brake, must-stop-to-reverse, steering `× 0.5`), measure() (his post-physics: speed, forward speed, going forward, per wheel in contact / contact point / suspension length, upside down, stuck, flip), suspension(i, 'low' | 'mid' | 'high'), unflip() (his impulse and torque by which axis is up), moveTo(x, y, z, yaw), state }`. `drive` is called before `physics.step`, `measure` after, by the world.
- `props.js`: `addProps(physics, list, kinds) → { bodies, sync(write(i, position, quaternion)), wake(i), reset(), remove() }`: his InstancedGroup half: each prop a sleeping dynamic body from its kind's collider (`crate` cuboid `0.5`, mass `0.02`; `barrel` cylinder; `rock` fixed hull or ball; `tree` fixed cylinder `(2.5, 0.15)`, friction `0.7`), `sync` writes only the awake ones.
- `catch.js`: `addCatch(physics) → { follow(x, z, y) }`: his bedrock, a kinematic slab `12 × 1 × 12` under the car, enabled only while the cell under the car has no heightfield yet (its collider is in flight) and placed at `heightAt − 0.5` from the nearest loaded cell, so the car never falls through a gap.

Tests, against the real engine in Node (`await RAPIER.init()` once per file): a car dropped from `y = 3` onto a flat heightfield rests with all four wheels in contact and `|y − rest| < 0.05` within 120 steps; full throttle for 3 s reaches between `4` and `6` m/s on the flat (his top speed 5, soft); the brake from that stops it within 2 s; upside down, `unflip()` rights it within 90 steps; a crate hit at speed moves and the chassis's `onHit` fires with a force over the threshold; `onOrigin([50000, 0, 0])` moves every body and leaves velocities as they were; `step(1)` after a long pause takes at most `maxSubsteps`.

### 3. `src/lib/three/`: the look, in pieces every world can take

Each is a factory returning `{ mesh | material, uniforms, update, dispose }` and a pure shader rewrite tested on stub shaders, as `grass.js` and `house.js` are. All GLSL (every module is `shading: 'glsl'`; the TSL port is the runtime's later lane).

- `landmap.js`: `createLandMap({ cells, radius }) → { textures, glsl, set(key, cell), drop(key), uniforms, centre(cx, cz) }`: the cells' masks and heights as the GPU sees them: a `DataArrayTexture` of `(2·radius + 1)²` layers of 128² RGBA for the masks and one of 65² half-float for the water levels, a layer per cell slot, and `LAND_GLSL` with `vec4 landMask(vec2 xz)`, `float landWater(vec2 xz)`, `vec3 landColour(vec4 mask, float slope)` (his depth gradient `dirt → shallow → deep` over `1 − B`, grass mixed by `G`, sand by the beach band, rock by slope, from the spec's palette as uniforms), the slot chosen from `floor(xz / 64) − centre` and the texel from `fract(xz / 64)`. The grass's `groundColour`/`groundGrass`/`groundHeight` are satisfied from it, so `grass.js` needs only a different GLSL include (`createGrass({ ground })` already takes the ground's `glsl` and uniforms).
- `land.js`: `createLandMaterial({ map, house }) → MeshLambertMaterial` for the cell meshes: colour from `landColour`, the tracks' `r` knocking `G` down before the colour, `normalNode` from the mesh (real relief, unlike his flat floor), the house look adopted.
- `river.js`: `createWaterSurface({ map, wind, quality }) → { mesh (one cell-sized grid per loaded cell slot, vertices at `landWater`, skipped where NaN), material, update(dt) }`: his water, in GLSL: white where `B > 0.17`; in the shallow band `0 < B < 0.17` the contour bands `fract((B + t·0.5)·10) − (1.3 − 1.3B) + noise` thresholded, scrolled along the flow (`A` of the mask) by the wind's time; alpha from the max; the house's fog and shade on it; depth write off. On `high` and `ultra`, the shallows are the opaque frame blurred: the renderer draws the opaque pass into a half-size target first (the world's `draw` owns this, through `rt.gfx`), the water samples it with a 9-tap blur of radius `0.01` screen in place of his 25-tap `hashBlur`; on `mid` and `low`, plain transparency. The waterline on things that cross the surface is `waterlineShader(shader)`: a `uWaterLevel` uniform per object (the world samples `waterAt` under the car once a frame) and his `0.013` band to white.
- `tracks.js`: `createTracks({ size = 40, texels = 512, count = 128 }) → { target, uniforms, track(width, channel) → { push(x, z, touching) }, update(focus), glsl }`: his ring buffer (a 128 × 1 float `DataTexture` per track, a point pushed every `1/30` s and `0.2` m, alpha the contact), the ribbon extruded in the vertex shader, drawn by a top-down orthographic camera into the target over `size` metres round `focus`, additive, with his edge and end fades. `TRACKS_GLSL` gives `vec4 tracksAt(vec2 xz)`; `grass.js` gains an optional `tracks` (its `G × (1 − r)`), and `land.js` and the leaves read it.
- `puffs.js`: `createPuffs({ species, count, wind, house, facing }) → { mesh (InstancedMesh), set(i, matrix), free(i), update(dt) }`: his foliage: `80` cards of `0.8` m in a unit sphere, radius `1 − rng³`, normals `lerp(card, sphere, 0.85)` through `foliage.js`'s `spherifyNormals`, every card facing the camera's fixed direction (`facing`), the cut-out an SDF texture made once in a canvas (a soft blob), rotated by `|windOffset| × 2.2`, two-tone `mix(a, b, smoothstep(0, 1, n·l))` per species, cast shadows as the cut-out. Trunks are a separate `InstancedMesh` of a cylinder in the palette's bark. A cell's trees go into the next free slots and are freed with the cell.
- `leaves.js`: `createLeaves({ count = 256, wind, map, tracks }) → { points, update(dt, focus, car) }`: his falling leaves on the CPU in a typed array: gravity by weight, the wind, the car's push (`sideways 20`, `along 100`, within `0.5` to `2` m), damping by whether on land, water or air, floating at the water's level, wrapped round the focus; drawn as instanced skewed quads with his flutter. `count` by tier (`low` 64, `mid` 128, `high` 256, `ultra` 512).
- `windLines.js`: `createWindLines({ wind, count = 4 }) → { group, update(dt, focus) }`: his four ribbons on Catmull-Rom curves, the streak sliding along by `progress`, spawned round the focus at the wind's angle every `0.3` to `2` s.
- `view.js`: `createChaseView({ camera, small }) → { update(dt, target, speed), area: { centre, radius, near, far }, shake(k) }`: his View: FOV `25`, spherical `phi 0.31π` (`0.27π` on a phone), `theta π/4`, radius `15` to `30` (`+9` for a narrow screen), the focus a magnet (`0.25`) on the car, position eased `dt × 10`, zoom-out `−0.4 × smoothstep(5, 40, speed)` on `high`, his damped roll spring for impacts; and the **optimal area** from the four screen corners on `y = 0` at the orbit's furthest, re-done on resize, read by the grass (`size = 2 × radius`), the water, the leaves, the tracks' focus, the shadow camera (ortho `±radius`, following the centre, `2048` on `high`/`ultra`, `1024` on `mid`, none on `low` where the car gets `grounding.js`'s blob) and the house's fog (`near = area.near + 0.315 × (far − near)`, `far = area.near + 1.25 × (far − near)`).

### 4. The world: `src/components/expanse/surface/`

The Expanse surface module of infinite worlds Phase 5, built here as the reference consumer. Route `/universe/expanse/:seed` (`pages/Expanse.jsx`, `useWorld`, the HUD kit of the UI lane), listed on `/worlds` as kind `planet` (the registry already keys it and `worldUrl` already points here), `WORLD_MB['/universe/expanse'] = 2` (Rapier and the kit). Nothing on the universe map changes in this design; Phase 4 of infinite worlds wires the landing.

- `module.js`: `{ id: 'expanse-surface', shading: 'glsl', mb: 2, label, create(rt, { seed, type }) }`. `create`: `landSpec`; `createPhysics` and the kit's GLBs (`rt.assets.gltf`) in parallel; the chunk grid `rt.chunks` at `size: CELL`, visual radius by tier (`ultra 7`, `high 6`, `mid 4`, `low 3`), the physics radius `1`; `rt.workers.define('land', …)` with `worker.js`; the land map, the material, the water, the grass, the puffs, the leaves, the wind lines, the tracks, the view, the car; the car at the spawn (the first cell's highest dry, flattest point, found in `rules.js`, kept 6 m from every prop with a body and 6 m inside the cell, so it starts free to drive and clear of a neighbour's props); the house look adopted over everything; `anchor()` returns the car's world position for `rt.origin`.
- `worker.js`: speaks the `rt.workers` contract: `{ type: 'cell', key, seed, type, cx, cz, step }` → `makeCell` and `cellMesh` → `{ key, heights, water, mask, props, positions, normals, indices }` with every buffer transferred; `{ type: 'cancel', key }` honoured between cells.
- `stream.js` (pure, tested with a fake grid, pool and scene): the loop Minecraft's `stream.js` runs, for land: each step `grid.update` with the car's cell and heading; for each wanted cell `pool.request('land', …)`; on an answer that `grid.done` accepts, build the mesh (through the GPU queue when it exists; a note as PR #600's), set the map's slot, place the props' instances, and if within the physics radius add the heightfield and the prop bodies; on `grid.unload` free all of it; the physics radius re-evaluated every step so a cell's colliders come and go as the car moves without the cell itself reloading. Two mesh steps: `1` within 2 cells, `2` beyond; a cell crossing that ring is re-meshed from its kept heights in the worker.
- `rules.js` (pure): the spawn, the respawn (`R`: back to the last dry point the car stood on, 3 s ago, his `moveTo`), the stuck and upside-down timers that call `unflip`, the "sea" and "lake" and "river" moments for the HUD, the water drag (his damping `1` under the surface) and the drowned reset (the car under water for 4 s respawns).
- `buggy.js`: the car's look, built in code (boxes and cylinders in the palette, as Albuquerque's `vehicles.js` builds its cars; a gen3d model is Phase 4): the body on the chassis, wheels following the controller's suspension (`y = min(base − suspension, −0.5)` eased `25 × dt`, his spin `forwardSpeed / radius`), his smoothed visual steering, two tracks per wheel (`0.5`, `r`) and one for the body (`1.5`, `g`).
- `scene.js`: the renderer-side `draw`: the opaque pass into the half-size target on `high`/`ultra`, then the frame; the sun from the spec (one directional light on the view's sphere, his shadow camera on the area), the sky a simple two-colour dome whose colours also feed the house's fog; `lowerQuality` drops the water blur, then the shadow map, then the puff count.
- `ExpanseHud.jsx` (the UI lane's HUD kit): speed, the compass to the nearest river or lake, the keys, `R` to respawn, the seed's name.

Input: `rt.input.bind` with `throttle` (W / ↑ / right trigger), `brake` (S / ↓ / left trigger: reverse once stopped), `steer` (A D / ← → / left stick), `boost` (Shift / A), `jump` (Space / B: all four suspensions to `high` for a frame, his), `respawn` (R). The touch stick is the page's as every world's.

### 5. How it holds a frame

- Everything per cell is made in the worker; the main thread only uploads. Budget per cell on arrival: one mesh (`8,192` triangles at step 1, `2,048` at step 2), one map slot write, up to `40` instance matrices, and within the physics radius one heightfield and up to `40` bodies. The stream builds at most `2` cells a frame.
- Triangles on `high` (radius 6, 169 cells): about 25 cells at step 1 and 144 at step 2 is `0.5` M for the ground, plus grass `78,400`, puffs `80 × 2 × trees`, the water only where it is. The probe's bar is the smooth-worlds one: after the veil, no frame over 100 ms and p99 under 33 ms on a `drive` journey of 500 m each way.
- Physics: one heightfield of `64²` cells round the car's cell is `9` colliders at most, plus sleeping props; Bruno's whole world is one `128²` heightfield and 150 bodies, and steps in under a millisecond; this is the same order.
- Memory: a cell is `65² × 4 × 2` (heights, water) + `128² × 4` (mask) + its mesh, about `200` KB at step 1; `169` cells is `34` MB, under the galaxy surface's models.

## Decisions (for the owner to overturn)

1. **Rapier, not cannon-es.** The toy-physics design chose cannon-es for Albuquerque's one car on a flat city and rejected Rapier on size. This design is a heightfield world streaming colliders in and out with a hundred props a cell; Rapier's heightfield, sleeping and solver are an order faster and steadier, his vehicle controller and every number he tuned port verbatim, and the `-compat` build is `0.6` MB once, behind the world gate, loaded only on this route. The `lib/physics` API is data (`add(desc)`), so the engine can be swapped under it if the size proves wrong on phones.
2. **Fixed step, not his variable step.** His world steps at the frame's delta times 2 and it works because his frame is steady. Ours runs under `pace` on laptops; an accumulator at 1/60 keeps the car the same at 30 and 120 fps. The chassis is drawn at the last step's transform (no interpolation) as his is; if a 120 Hz screen shows judder, interpolate the chassis only.
3. **Rivers are traced, not noise.** His are drawn by hand. A trace downhill on the same field the ground is made from gives rivers that meander, join valleys and reach the sea, and makes the bed and the bank from the same polyline, so the water is always where the ground is low. The region's 3 × 3 look-up keeps it deterministic across cells.
4. **The water surface is a mesh at the water level, not his flat quad.** A river that runs downhill cannot be one plane. The look (depth gradient, contour bands, blur) is his unchanged; only the geometry differs.
5. **The land library owns the galaxy's layers.** `galaxy/surface/terrain.js`'s `LAYERS` move to `lib/land/layers.js` and are imported back. Its behaviour and tests do not change; the move is so the Expanse and the galaxy agree on what a hill is.
6. **Car only in this design.** Bruno's world is driven, not walked. On foot (the galaxy's `walker.js` over `heightAt`) and getting in and out are Phase 4, with the figure.
7. **No tilt-shift, no bloom, no palette texture.** The house look carries the shade and the fog. His blur and bloom are a post chain this world does not need to read as his; they can come with the post lane.
8. **The reference world is the Expanse surface, not Albuquerque.** Albuquerque's car has a town, traffic, a story and its own save; moving it onto rigid bodies is a world's own session once `lib/physics` exists (its spec's "Step 2: wheels" is the brief). The Expanse has nothing yet and needs everything here, and it is where "the universes and worlds" are going.
9. **Constants are the infinite-worlds ones where they overlap**: `CELL = 64`, `ORIGIN_CELL = 50000`, the chunk grid's hysteresis `1`. New: `REGION = 1024`, `MAX_DEPTH = 3`, `STEP = 8`, mesh steps `1 | 2`, physics radius `1`.

## What this is not

- Not a change to any authored world, the universe map, the galaxy or Albuquerque. The galaxy's `terrain.js` imports its layers from a new place and that is all.
- Not the Expanse's sectors, names, lanes or map (infinite worlds Phase 4) nor the landing from orbit (its Phase 5's `landings.js` line). The route takes a seed; the landing hands one over later.
- Not weather, seasons, snow, rain, ice, splashes, flowers, a day cycle. The wind has a strength and an angle from the spec; the sun stands still.
- Not multiplayer on the surface. Poses can carry the seed later as they carry a sector.
- Not WebGPU or TSL. Every shader here is a GLSL rewrite with a pure test, as the house's are.

## Open assumptions, marked

- Rapier's heightfield row order and `-compat`'s behaviour in a Vite worker are checked in Phase 1's first task; if the compat build cannot load in the worker, physics stays on the main thread (it is there in this design anyway; only `makeCell` is in the worker).
- The river trace's constants (`STEP 8`, momentum `0.6`, width growth) are first numbers; the tests pin properties (never climbs, reaches the sea), not shapes. Screenshots at three seeds go in Phase 1's PR from a Node script that renders a cell's mask to PNG (`scripts/land-preview.mjs`), so the shapes are judged before a triangle is drawn.
- The shallows blur needs the opaque frame in a texture. If `rt.gfx.post` cannot give a pre-transparent copy cheaply, the water on `high` falls back to `mid`'s plain transparency and the design notes it.

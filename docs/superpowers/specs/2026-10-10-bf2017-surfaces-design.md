# The surfaces at the game's fidelity: how Frostbite makes the picture from these assets, and the half of it the web can take. The design

Date: 2026-10-10 (night). Status: design for Opus 5.5 to implement; the owner asked for it. Builds on `2026-10-10-galaxy-engine-design.md` (the stack under `src/lib/three/light/`, PR #827), `2026-10-10-bf2017-levels-lighting-sabers-design.md` (lanes L and G: the level packs and the derived light, #831 and #833), `2026-10-10-battlefront-fidelity-design.md` (PR #836, lanes S, V, X, C, N, U: shadows, volumetrics, particles, cameras, scatter, headroom; S, V, X and C running) and `2026-10-10-bf2017-cast-left-design.md` (PR #839; its lane D is the desktop's texture gap). Plans: `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-10-10-bf2017-surfaces-laneQ{1,2,3,4,5,6}-*.md`. Hand-off: `docs/superpowers/HANDOFF-bf2017-surfaces.md`.

## What the owner asked

"Look into the game files and understand how it renders such a beautiful game using Frosty. How is it able to use the assets to make a beautiful game, and how can I bring even half of that beauty into this repo?"

## The answer in one paragraph

The game's picture is six layers stacked, and the export in `C:\Users\tilak\Downloads\BF2_Extract` (and the bucket `bf2017-assets`) holds the data for five of them. The site today draws one and a half: the geometry with its flattened glTF maps, and the light's records (sun, sky, fog, probes) through lanes G and R. What the game adds on top, in the order the eye notices it, is: **the surface shader** over each material (a tiling detail normal at 20× over every prop, grunge, breakup and scorch overlays, paint and metal colours, emissive colour times intensity, parallax, wear), **the baked indirect light** (Enlighten's static irradiance per level, which is why the game's shade is never flat grey), **the ground's own layers** (tiling snow, rock and sand normals per paint layer, displaced), **weathering** (snow, dust or rain laid on the up-facing faces by sky visibility, and 13,984 placed decals), **the texture quality** (BC7 at 2048 where the site serves ETC1S at about 40 dB), and **the picture** (the record's HDR grading LUT, its five-Gaussian bloom with colours, its HBAO numbers, its painted 8192 × 2048 HDR sky panorama and fog gradient, its cloud-shadow texture). None of these six is on the site. Each is a lane below; four start now.

## How the game makes the picture, from the export

Every number here was read from the export on 2026-10-10 (`web/data.tsv`, `web/textures.jsonl`, `agents/textures/matdump/materials.jsonl`, the VisualEnvironment record `data/Levels/Lighting/Hoth/Sunny_01/VE_Sky_Arctic_Sunny_01.json`). Photogrammetry is the material of it (DICE scanned the real props and locations: the 2016 GDC talk "Photogrammetry and Star Wars Battlefront"); the layers below are how the engine shows the scans.

### 1. Packed PBR maps

17,511 textures: 7,104 BC7 sRGB colour, 7,037 BC7 linear, 470 BC5 normals, 270 BC6 HDR, 1,616 half-float; 5,683 at 2,048 px, 89 at 4,096, 52 at 8,192 (the sky panoramas). The channels are packed and named by suffix (`_cs` colour + smoothness, `_nam` normal + two masks, `_nma` normal + metal + AO, `_msr` metal + smoothness + reflectance, `_nts` normal + translucency + smoothness for vegetation, `_aosl` AO + detail-array slice, `_rsssao` reflectance + SSS mask + AO for heads: 36 suffixes in `research/research_textures.json`, confidence per row). The export unpacks each material to three glTF maps (colour, a rebuilt normal, an ORM) from the mesh's default variation. **On the site**: these three maps, as hybrid KTX2 (ETC1S colour at about 40 dB, UASTC q6 the rest, `tool/ktx2_encode.py`'s docstring), mips dropped per tier.

### 2. The surface shader

30,517 materials on 15,324 meshes name 2,870 `ShaderGraph`s: 52 presets under `Shaders/Presets/Walrus/` (`SS_PropsPreset`, `SS_VehiclePreset`, `SS_WeaponsPreset`, `SS_CharactersPreset_WalrusMetallic`, `SS_VegetationPreset`, `SS_GlassPreset`, `SS_CreaturePreset`…), the preset families under `Shaders/Presets/` (`SS_PropsMetallicPreset_1UV_TextureArray_01`, `_2UV_`, `SS_Panels_Detail_2UV_TextureArray_01`, `SS_PropsNonMetallicPreset_01_EmissiveAlphaTest`…) and per-world instances (`SS_Naboo_Concrete_01`, `SS_MC80WallsPreset_01`, `SS_Arctic_CorridorSnowPile_01`). The graph itself is opaque (every `ShaderGraph` record is 218 to 442 bytes: a name). **What is not opaque is every material's parameters**, dumped per mesh in `materials.jsonl` (uploaded to the bucket on 2026-10-10 as `web/materials.jsonl`, 14.1 MB): per material its shader, its texture slots by parameter name, its vectors, bools and conditionals. ### 3. The baked light

Lane L's hand-off said "no lightmaps exist (Enlighten)". **They exist and were exported.** Per level the export has `StaticEnlightenData` records naming five textures: `EN_Hoth_01_Static_Sunset_staticIrradianceTexture` (3,128 × 3,680, BC6U HDR), `staticDirectionTextureR`, `G`, `B` (directional irradiance, one direction map per colour channel, `DirectionalIrradianceRgbEnable: true`) and `staticSkyVisibilityData`; 200 irradiance atlases across the levels (Hoth 3, Endor 4, Naboo 2, Tatooine 3, Kashyyyk 3, Takodana 2, Yavin 2, Death Star 2…). They are Enlighten static generation 4 with Flux's baker (`EnlightenDataAsset`: `OutputFormat R9G9B9E5`, `SystemLightmapSize 128`, `MaxLightmapSize 4096`, `FluxAmbientOcclusionRays 5000`), and the `EnlightenComponentData` of every weather carries the bounce's numbers (Hoth Sunny: `TerrainColor 0.547, 0.594, 0.644`, `SkyBoxSkyColor 0.84, 1.58, 3.0`, `SkyBoxGroundColor 0.049`, `BounceScale 1.0`, `SunScale 0.5`). **What is missing** is the mapping: a static prop's lightmap chart is not in its mesh (the exporter writes `TEXCOORD_1` only where the mesh has a second UV set, and Hoth's props have one set; the vehicles' second set is their unique unwrap, not a chart); the charts and each instance's atlas placement live in the `DatabaseResource` the record names (`BB4CBCCB31F41CBB`), an Enlighten database the exporter never read. **On the site**: no indirect light but the probe's cube and the sky's hemisphere (G), and lane R's SSGI (unjudged). This is the single biggest difference between the site's Hoth and the game's: the game's shadowed snow is lit blue by the sky and warm by the sunlit snow beside it; the site's is flat.

### 4. The ground

The terrain is a heightfield (exported, on the site as lane L's `image` layer) painted with layers; each layer combination draws a stack of tiling surface textures with displacement (`maps/terrain_scatter/<terrain>.json`'s `surfaceShaders`: Hoth's combinations each name four or five of `T_ArcticBase_SnowRockyPacked_04_N`, `SnowRoughPacked_03_N`, `SnowRocky_02_N`, `SnowPacked_04_N`, `SnowChunkyWind_01_N`, over `T_ArcticBase_SnowSparkle_03_RGBM`; 181 terrain textures across the ten terrains, 173 not yet in the bucket), and the paint masks (where each layer is) are the streaming tree's undecoded section (`maps/README.md`). The colour map is undecoded too. **On the site**: the site's own `snow` detail over the game's heights (`sites/ice.js`: `ground.detail: 'snow'`), a flat blue-white plane in `docs/superpowers/evidence/bf2017-levels/hoth/field.jpg`.

### 5. Weathering and decals

Every weather's record carries `GlobalWeatheringParamsEntityData` with `WeatheringSkyVisibilityParam` (`SkyVisibilityRange`, `SkyVisibilityExponent`, `IndoorThreshold`, `DisableGlobalWeatheringIndoor`) and `AccumulateOverTimeOp` (`TargetValue`, `TimeToReachTarget`): the engine lays the world's weather (Hoth's snow, Jakku's sand, Kamino's rain) on each material's up-facing, sky-visible faces, gated by the material's `UseWeather`, `ESB_TopDirt`, `ESB_Sand`, `ESB_Rain` and its `WeatheringMask` channels. And the maps place 13,984 decals with transforms and textures (`maps/<level>.extras.json`'s `decals[]`, `shaderTextures`): none on Hoth, 582 on Geonosis_02, 563 on Naboo_03, 536 on Kamino_03, 512 on Kashyyyk_02, 119 on the Death Star. **On the site**: nothing of either; a Hoth crate stands on the snow with no snow on it.

### 6. The picture

The weather record's post: `TonemapComponentData` (`EV 10`, `ExposureCompensation 1.5`, `TonemapMethod_Linear` with the HDR LUT, `BloomMethod_GaussianSimple` as five Gaussians with `Gaussian3/4/5Weight 0.2, 0.35, 0.5` and colours `Gaussian5Color 0.41, 0.57, 0.81` on Hoth's day, `BloomScale 0.1`, `LensDirtTexture` and `FFTKernelTexture` null on Hoth), `ColorCorrectionComponentData` (`HdrColorGradingLut T_CC_Hoth_Sunny_01`; 63 `GradingLutAsset`s), `DynamicAOComponentData` (`HbaoRadius 1.5`, `HbaoAngleBias 15`, `HbaoAttenuation 0.7`, `HbaoContrast 0.65`, `HbaoPowerExponent 2`, `HbaoBlurRadius 8`, `HbaoBlurSharpness 4`, `AffectOutdoorLight true`, `TemporalFilterEnable true`), `SkyComponentData` (`PanoramicTexture T_Hoth_Sunny_02_C`, 8,192 × 2,048 BC6U HDR, in the bucket as KTX2; `SkyGradientTexture T_Hoth_Sunny_02_Fog_C`, 512 × 256 float; Rayleigh `5.8e-6, 1.35e-5, 3.31e-5`; `CloudLayer1/2Color`), `OutdoorLightComponentData` (`CloudShadowTexture T_Arctic_01_CloudShadow_RGBM`, `SunIntensity 128,000`, `SunColor 1, 0.956, 0.918`), `SubSurfaceScatteringComponentData` (seven profiles), `SunFlareComponentData`, `MotionBlurComponentData`. **On the site**: lane R's chain has the pass kinds (bloom, SSGI, AO, SSR, god rays, flare, LUT, TRAA) with the house's numbers; the LUT is unapplied ("Lane R's rest"); the sky is the analytic model without the panorama; the bloom is three's single bloom; lane S calibrates the exposure, lane V the fog, flares, motion blur and depth of field; lane C the cameras.

## The design

### Q1: the surface shader on the site (the recipe and the node material)

- **The rulebook.** `scripts/lib/bf2017-materials.mjs` (pure, tested on five fixture rows copied from `materials.jsonl`: a `SS_PropsPreset` prop, a `SS_VehiclePreset` with `ESB_VehicleIsWreck`, a `SS_CharactersPreset_WalrusMetallic` body with the detail array, a `SS_VegetationPreset` leaf, a `SS_PropsNonMetallicPreset_01_EmissiveAlphaTest`) turns one dump row into one recipe per material: `{ family, shader, maps: { color, normal, orm, detail, detailArray, detailSlice, grunge, breakup: { color, normal }, scorch, height, wear, weathering, emissive, idMap, tintSwatch }, params: { detail: { tiling, normal, smoothness, strength }, emissive: { color, intensity, blink }, paint, metal, grunge: { color, intensity, tiling }, scorch: { tiling, ember }, breakup: { tiling }, parallax: { on, scale }, reflectance: { up, down }, backface: { subsurface, smoothness }, alphaTest, doubleSided, terrainBlend, weather: { top, sand, rain, use }, uvSet }, _source }`. **The family is read from the shader's name** (`props`, `propsMetallic`, `propsNonMetallic`, `panels`, `vehicle`, `vehicleLarge`, `weapon`, `character`, `head`, `hair`, `creature`, `vegetation`, `glass`, `emissive`, `decal`, `instance`): a per-world instance (`SS_Naboo_Concrete_01`) falls to the family its texture slots match (`_CS` + `_NAM` is `props`), and a material with no texture slots in the dump (8,573 of them: the game bound them through its variation database, as the export's GLB already carries) keeps the GLB's three maps with no overlay, said in the recipe as `family: 'glb'`. Every parameter the dump lacks is a named default with a comment. The CLI `node scripts/bf2017-materials.mjs <mesh|--level hoth>` reads `web/materials.jsonl` from the bucket (`bf2017-fetch.mjs --raw materials.jsonl`) and writes `recipes.json` beside a level pack (`public/models/galaxy/bf2017/levels/<world>/recipes.json`, one entry per pack mesh per material index) and per crew pack (`crew/<name>.recipes.json`); it lists the maps the recipes want that the pack lacks, and phase 0's import fetches them as KTX2 by name (the desktop encodes 1,788 of them tonight: `web_opt/_surfaces_list.tsv`, log `logs/surfaces_textures.log`; a map not yet there prints `missing:` and the recipe goes without it).
- **The material.** `src/lib/three/surface/gameMaterial.js`: `createGameMaterial(recipe, maps, { tier, overlays = [] }) → MeshPhysicalNodeMaterial` (dynamic `three/webgpu` + `three/tsl` through the stack's `loadThree()`), built as TSL: the base from the three maps; **the detail normal** sampled at `uv × tiling` (the array's slice from `AOSlice.b × slices` on characters, `ESS_SliceArrayTexCoord_01` choosing the UV set), blended into the base normal (UDN blend: `n.xy + d.xy × strength, n.z`), its smoothness term added at `SmoothnessDetail_Intensity`; **overlays** in order: breakup (colour and normal at `BreakupMaskTiling`), grunge (`GrungeColour × GrungeIntensity × mask`), scorch (`ScorchMask` to black and `ScorchEmberIntensity × mask` to emissive when the wreck flag is on), wear (`__WearMap` lifting metal and roughness), AO dirt (`AODirtColor` in the ORM's cavities); **colours** (`PaintColour` on the paint mask, `MetalColour` on the metal mask, `ColorTint`); **emissive** `texture × EmissiveColor × EmissiveIntensity`, the `OneChannelMask` and `FromBaseColor` modes, `BlinkLength` as a time square wave; **parallax** as TSL parallax occlusion over `ParallaxOcclusionHeightmap` (8 steps on high, 16 on ultra, off below); **reflectance** `ReflectanceUp/Down` by the world normal's y into `specularIntensity`; **vegetation** `SubsurfaceBackfaceScale` as `MeshPhysicalNodeMaterial`'s transmission-free translucency (the `_nts` map's translucency channel times the wrapped sun), `AlphaOnOff` as alpha test; **terrain blend** as a hook `overlays` can fill (Q2 provides `terrainBlend(groundColorNode)`); **weathering** as the same hook (Q4 provides `snowOverlay`, `sandOverlay`, `wetOverlay`). Each `overlays[i](ctx) → { color?, roughness?, normal?, metalness?, emissive? }` with `ctx = { uv, worldNormal, worldPosition, skyVisibility, params }`, applied in order; the pure composition (`composeOverlays(base, parts)`) is tested in Node without three. Tiers: ultra every feature; high no parallax over 8 steps and the detail at half tiling resolution; mid detail and emissive only; low the three maps (the material then equals the GLB's).
- **Hair and heads**: `hairMaterial(recipe, maps)` (Kajiya-Kay two-lobe anisotropic specular along the strand tangent from `HairStrandTexture`, `MelaninXY` to the base colour by the Marschner melanin curve approximation, `TintTipColor` toward the tips by `v`), `headMaterial` (the `_rsssao` map's SSS mask into the post's SSS pass when V lands it; until then a wrapped diffuse by the profile's `RadiusR/G/B`).
- **Wiring**: `surface/level/levelGltf.js` takes an optional `recipes` map and a `materialFor(recipe, maps)`; on a `'nodes'` world (lane T's flip, or the Battlefront world) `createLevelLoader` builds the game material instead of patching the GLB's; the classic renderer keeps the GLB's. Crew packs the same through `crew.js`'s material pass. `scripts/light-fixture.mjs --materials` draws the fixture's cubes under five recipes (the five fixture rows) with their maps fetched from the bucket, both backend kinds; the PR shows a 2 m wall before and after.
- **Proof**: `docs/superpowers/evidence/bf2017-surfaces/Q1/`: the fixture; Hoth's hangar wall and a crate at 2 m on `/galaxy/hoth/surface?gpu=webgpu` (the owner's laptop) and `?gpu=webgl`; `galaxy-check surface hoth` under `BUDGET=1` per tier; `renderer.info.memory.textures` before and after (the detail maps are shared per name: 384 distinct detail maps across the dump, one upload each).

### Q2: the ground's own layers

- `scripts/bf2017-ground.mjs <world>` reads the terrain's `surfaceShaders` and the level's `maps/terrain_scatter/<terrain>.json`, fetches the layer textures (the 173 encode tonight), and writes `ground.json` beside the pack: per layer combination its normal stack (name, tiling in metres from the texture's size at the game's 0.5 m/px detail, said as a constant per world until the layer shader's tiling is read), the sparkle map, and **the derived mask** per layer (the masks are not exported: slope from the heightmap, height bands, the game's own placed-mesh density; `mask: 'derived'` in the JSON, replaced by the real masks when the desktop decodes section 1 of the streaming tree; lane N's scatter reads the same masks, so the two agree). The derivation is pure (`scripts/lib/bf2017-ground.mjs`, tested on a 64 × 64 synthetic heightmap: a ridge takes the rocky stack, the flat the packed one).
- `src/lib/three/ground/layeredGround.js`: a TSL ground material for lane L's `image` layer on a `'nodes'` world: the heightfield's normal from the near map; per layer a tiling detail normal (triplanar on slopes over 35°) and roughness; the layers blended by the derived mask with height-based blending (the stack's displacement channel where the `_N` map carries one in blue, else the mask alone); the sparkle map (`T_ArcticBase_SnowSparkle_03_RGBM`) as a view-dependent glint on snow worlds (`sparkle: true` in `ground.json`); the far ground fades to the macro colour (the heightmap's shade until the colour map is decoded) past 300 m. The classic worlds keep `groundLook.js`.
- Tiers: four layers on ultra and high, two on mid, the macro only on low. Proof: Hoth's field and the ridge at 2 m and 50 m, before and after; the frame table from the perf probe.

### Q3: the bounce

- **Now, from the record**: `src/lib/three/light/bounce.js` (pure + a node): the weather's `EnlightenComponentData` into the stack: the ground bounce as a hemisphere term coloured `TerrainColor × sun × BounceScale × SunScale` under the horizon, the sky's `SkyBoxSkyColor` as the zenith of the ambient, `SkyBoxGroundColor` the nadir; lane R's `probes.js` scales its cube by `BounceScale`; lane R's SSGI radius and strength from the record's `CullRadius`; `applyGameLight` takes it when `entry.record` has the component. Proof: the fixture's shadowed cube reads blue-grey under Hoth's day, not grey.
- **The atlases, a spike on the desktop** (`tool/EnlightenResProbe.cs`, the pattern of `TerrainResProbe.cs`): read the `DatabaseResource` of `EN_Hoth_01_Static_Sunset`, look for the chart table (per instance: a mesh id, an atlas rect and a UV scale/offset; Enlighten's "system" charts hold per-vertex or per-face UVs), and report: the resource's size, whether float pairs in texture range appear per instance, and whether instance ids match the map's physics-part indices. If they do, the follow-up is `bf2export lightmapuv`: one `TEXCOORD_1` per instance (not per mesh) written into the pack as a per-instance `uvRect` plus a per-mesh chart UV, and `gameMaterial.js` adds `lightmap: { irradiance, dirR, dirG, dirB, sky }` sampled at `uv1 × rect.zw + rect.xy`, the directional term `irradiance × max(0, dot(n, dir))` per channel (Enlighten's directional irradiance RGB), the sky visibility handed to Q4's overlays. If they do not, the spike says so and the bounce stays the record's.

### Q4: weathering and decals

- **Weathering** (`src/lib/three/surface/weather.js`, pure parts tested): three overlays for Q1's hook: `snowOverlay`, `sandOverlay`, `wetOverlay`, each `(ctx) → { color, roughness, normal, metalness }` from the world normal's up-facing term (`smoothstep(0.4, 0.9, n.y)`, the game's `WeatheringParam` where the record has an exponent), the sky visibility (the record's `SkyVisibilityRange` and `Exponent`; until Q3's atlases, the sky visibility is 1 outdoors and `IndoorThreshold` in a site zone that is a room), the material's gate (`weather.top`, `weather.sand`, `weather.rain`, `weather.use`, the `WeatheringMask` channel), and the weather's `AccumulateOverTimeOp` (`TargetValue` reached over `TimeToReachTarget` from the weather's start: a blizzard whitens the crates over the record's seconds). Snow: white with the sparkle map's roughness 0.35; sand: the desert's colour at roughness 0.9; wet: darkened albedo, roughness 0.08, `RainFlipNormal` as the record says. The world's weather kind comes from the entry (`snow` on Hoth and Crait, `sand` on Jakku and Tatooine, `wet` on Kamino's storm and Kashyyyk's rain).
- **Decals** (`scripts/bf2017-decals.mjs <world>` and `src/lib/three/decals/`): the map's `decals[]` with their transforms and textures into `decals.json` beside the pack per cell; projected decals as `DecalGeometry` over the cell's static instances at load (one merged mesh per cell per texture, `polygonOffset`), volume decals as a box pass on the node renderer (a `VolumeNodeMaterial` box that samples the depth buffer and blends its colour and normal onto what it meets: `webgpu_volume_*`'s shape); 2,048 textures max per world through phase 0's import. First worlds: Geonosis_02 and Naboo_03 are not packed; the decals land on the first packed world with them (Endor_01 has 23, Kamino_01 51 when lane L packs them); Hoth has none, so the proof is the fixture with ten of Naboo's decals on its wall.

### Q5: the texture quality (the desktop)

- Measure first: `qa/ktx_cmp` on twenty of Hoth's colour maps, ETC1S (as served) against UASTC q10 against the BC7 master: PSNR and a 4× crop of the hangar's floor plate.
- If UASTC colour is over 3 dB better on the photogrammetry maps (the encoder's own note says about 40 against 45): a second colour class for ultra, `ultra/` beside each colour KTX2 (UASTC q8, the same mips), uploaded by name through its own `--root`; the level pack's `tex` gains an `ultra` size row that names it; `levelPack.js`'s `tierTexture` picks it on ultra. Bytes: 3.4× the colour maps on ultra only. GPU memory the same.
- Anisotropy 16 on every KTX2 the pack binds (`texture.anisotropy = renderer.capabilities.getMaxAnisotropy()`): a one-line change in `levelGltf.js` that this lane measures on the hangar floor at a grazing angle before Q5 touches an encoder.

### Q6: the picture

- `src/lib/three/light/grade.js`: the record's `HdrColorGradingLut` through lane G's `gameLut.js` into the chain's `lut` (lane R's "rest"), after tonemap in the game's order (`TonemapMethod_Linear` + the LUT is the look); the bloom as the record's five Gaussians: `BloomNode` at five radii (the pyramid's levels) weighted `Gaussian1..5Weight` and tinted `Gaussian1..5Color`, `BloomScale` as the strength, `LensDirtTexture` multiplied in where the record has one; `DynamicAOComponentData` into the GTAO pass (`HbaoRadius`, `HbaoPowerExponent` as the exponent, `HbaoContrast`, `HbaoBlurRadius`); the sky's `PanoramicTexture` (the KTX2 HDR panorama, `T_Hoth_Sunny_02_C`) behind the scattering as lane R's `sky.js` blend (`panorama: 'behind'`; the SA planets are painted domes, the space maps the same), the `SkyGradientTexture` as the fog's colour by view elevation; `CloudShadowTexture` into lane S's `clouds.js` in place of the noise where the record names one. All additive on `apply.js`, behind the entry's fields; nothing changes for a world without the record.
- Waits for S (`calibrate.js`, `sun.js`, `clouds.js`) and V (`fog.js`, `post.js`, `passes.js`) to merge, since all three touch `post.js`; reads their branches meanwhile.

### Quality and rules

- Ultra on a laptop or desktop with a graphics chip restricts nothing; each lane's budget constants take the record's own tier where it has one.
- Every number from the records (`_source` on every leaf); a constant the records lack is named with a comment.
- The stack is `src/lib/**` (`surface/`, `ground/`, `decals/`, `light/bounce.js`, `light/grade.js`): pure where it can be, three where it must, tests beside, files under 800 lines, every addon import dynamic through the stack's `loadThree()`.
- A lane proves itself on the lit fixture on both backend kinds before it touches a world; the galaxy's surfaces take it behind `site.level` on the node renderer (lane T's flip), the classic renderer keeps what it has; the Battlefront world takes all of it.
- Compare on the shot: before and after in `docs/superpowers/evidence/bf2017-surfaces/<lane>/`.
- One lane per PR; `origin/main` merged before and after; both sides kept in any shared file.

### What this design does not do

- No particles, volumetrics, cameras, shadows, scatter or upscaling (#836's lanes own them); no hit decals (P4); no sound (lane D2).
- No `EmitterGraph`, no cloth, no Enlighten runtime (the dynamic radiosity): the atlases are static.
- No re-export of the meshes for a second UV set until Q3's spike says the charts are readable.
- No change to the classic (`'glsl'`) worlds' materials: the node material is the point of the engine decision.

## Open assumptions, marked

- **C1.** The family-by-name recipe reproduces the presets' look for the five families the fixture pins (props, vehicle, character, vegetation, emissive alpha-test); a family the names don't settle ships as `glb` and the PR lists them by count.
- **C2.** The detail arrays (`NormalDetailTextureArray`) were exported as slices (`textures.jsonl` rows with `type: 'array'`, 65 of them; `slices` 2 to 10); the slice index is `AOSlice.b × (slices − 1)` rounded (the research's "discrete k/10 levels"); if a character's detail reads wrong on the fixture, the slice goes to the nearest by name and the PR says so.
- **C3.** The derived ground masks read right on Hoth (packed snow on the flat, rocky on the ridges, chunky in the trenches) without the real masks; a wrong world ships the macro only and says so.
- **C4.** The Enlighten database is readable enough to find per-instance chart rects; if not, the bounce stays the record's and the design's §3 says the atlases wait.
- **C5.** The 1,788 maps encode and upload tonight (the encoder's log says `ok=` per pass; the uploader's "to upload now" line is checked by hand); a map not yet there is `missing:` in the import and the recipe goes without it, and the lane re-imports when it lands.

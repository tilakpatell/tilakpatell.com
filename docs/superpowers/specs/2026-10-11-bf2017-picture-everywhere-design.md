# The game’s picture on every world: the node renderer as the only renderer above low, every layer on, every gate green. The design

Date: 2026-10-11 (early). Status: design for Opus 5.5 to implement; the owner asked for it. Builds on `2026-10-10-bf2017-surfaces-design.md` (the six layers, lanes Q1 to Q6), `2026-10-10-battlefront-fidelity-design.md` (lanes S, V, X, C, N, U), `2026-10-10-bf2017-every-asset-design.md` (lane E0, the level factory, PR #888), `2026-10-10-bf2017-accuracy-design.md` (colour, bots, screens, fighters, maps, picture) and the decision `docs/decisions/2026-10-10-the-engine-in-place.md`. Written from four readings of `origin/main` at `00843045b` (#890) on 2026-10-11, the game’s own frames (its Steam set, the Endor village at night and the space battle; the Crait and outpost frames are sequel-era and set aside) and the Frostbite papers (Lagarde and de Rousiers 2014, “Moving Frostbite to PBR”; Hillaire 2015, “Physically based and unified volumetric rendering”; Enlighten’s radiosity in Frostbite 3).

## What the owner asked

“Make sure all of the assets and stuff you use looks like the Battlefront game and how Frostbite renders it and makes it high fidelity with details. Or at least use an engine library that is robust so all the worlds’ assets are robustly made.” Then: “Is there no similar game engine for web technologies? Develop it so you can run these games with high fidelity and make it performant.” Then: “Make sure they pass everything and all the worlds run on the renderer and everything that can show all the details.” Then: “The only renderer should be the best-looking one above medium settings, and check we can change it. Make sure it runs so we have all the layers and can see the beauty of it, and it looks like 2017 Battlefront II: look at pictures online, and mods.”

## The answer in one paragraph

There is no Frostbite for the web and no library that renders these assets as DICE does; Babylon, PlayCanvas, Unity’s and Godot’s web exports are editors with their own pipelines, and each would mean re-implementing the same surface-shader families on top of a rewrite of a hundred worlds (the three-over-Babylon decision stands). The engine this site needs is the one it has been writing: three r186’s node renderer (WebGPU, with WebGL 2 as the `nodes-webgl` backend), TSL, Rapier, and the stack under `src/lib/three/light/`. **That stack is built and merged, and it is switched off where it matters.** The seventeen Star Wars worlds (`galaxy-surface`) still run the classic `WebGLRenderer` with a GLSL composer: the module never says `shading: 'nodes'` (`src/components/galaxy/surface/module.js`, `src/runtime/backend.js:20`), so the calibrated sun with its four PCSS cascades, the cloud and contact shadows, the Rayleigh and Mie sky with the record’s painted panorama, the height and volumetric fog, the clustered placed lights, the crossfading probes, SSGI, GTAO, SSR, god rays, the lens flare, motion blur, the record’s linear tonemap followed by its LUT, the five-Gaussian bloom, TRAA and the upscaler, the game material with its recipes and variations, the layered ground, the placed decals, the particles and lane U’s batching and occlusion all draw only on the separate `battlefront` world and the `litWorld` fixture. The galaxy surface draws one sun with a PCF shadow that `groundWorld` usually turns off, three pooled point lights, an analytic gradient sky, exponential fog, Unreal bloom, a hand-rolled tone shoulder and the LUT over the encoded picture. This design is the switch, and what has to be true for it to hold: every world on the node renderer from the mid tier up, every layer passed what it needs, the quality switch checked, and every gate green, with the game’s frames as the measure.

## 1. What the four readings found

**The renderer** (`src/runtime/{backend,webgl,webgpu,quality}.js`, `src/lib/three/renderer.js`, `src/components/universe/post.js`, `src/components/galaxy/surface/scene.js`): the surface is `'glsl'`; its tone mapping is the composer’s final pass (exposure, a Neutral shoulder without its toe, sRGB by hand, saturation 1.06, contrast 0.07, vignette, dither, the LUT after the encode on high and ultra); its sun shadow is a 2,048² PCF map that `groundWorld` replaces with a baked floor mask on any GPU with float targets; its sky is a gradient dome with noise clouds; its fog is `FogExp2` tinted by the sky; its placed lights are a pool of three; AO, SSR, volumetrics, TAA and the upscaler do not exist on it. The surface’s node twins exist and nothing imports them: `src/components/galaxy/surface/engine.js` (`postFor`), `surface/nodes/{post,sky,skyfog,water}.js`. The node chain’s order is `render → ssgi → denoise → ao → ssr → volumes → fog → bloom → godrays → lensflare → dof → motionBlur → tonemap → lut → traa|smaa → upscale → output` (`src/lib/three/light/post.js:85`), per tier (ultra everything with TRAA; high without god rays and the flare, with SMAA; mid AO, bloom, SMAA; low bloom). GTAO stands in for HBAO with the record’s radius, exponent, attenuation and blur; its angle bias, contrast and sharpness have no node. The TSL bloom glows about a third of Unreal’s for the same numbers (`surface/nodes/post.js`, `UNREAL = 3`), noted and unsettled.

**The material** (`src/lib/three/surface/{gameMaterial,families,hair,compose,weather,variation}.js`, `scripts/lib/bf2017-recipes.mjs`, `level/levelGltf.js`, `level/levelVariations.js`): a real TSL `MeshStandardNodeMaterial` (physical for reflectance and heads) driven by the game’s per-material parameters with `_source` on every number; UDN detail normals at the game’s tiling, the AOSlice array on the crew (levels draw slice 0 only), POM at 8 and 16 steps, grunge, scorch with embers, breakup, wear, AO dirt, paint and metal and tint, emissive in nits through the exposure constant, alpha test, two-sided foliage, hair with melanin and Kajiya-Kay, a head scatter approximation. It draws **only on the node renderer above low** (`level/index.js:187-200`), and on Hoth it changes 6 of 123 meshes (Q1’s evidence). Not reproduced: per-instance variations (per mesh only; the bins’ `Uint32 variation[n]` column is unread, so 24 mixed Hoth meshes draw their default and the 95 % bound gate is missed at 93.1 %), world weathering (`level/index.js:200` passes no overlays), terrain blend, placed decals in levels (`decalScene.js` is fixture-only), a glass shader, level detail slices, detail smoothness, `idMap`, `TintSwatchTexture`, `F90ColorTexture`, the `_nts` translucency, real SSS, POM self-shadowing, anisotropy (pack maps stay at 1; Q5 undone).

**The pipeline** (`scripts/bf2017-level.mjs`, `bf2017-import.mjs`, `assets-publish.mjs`, `galaxy-check.mjs`, `galaxy-budget.mjs`, `src/lib/level/{lod,fit}.js`): the game’s own LOD chains with no simplifier, the game’s KTX2 with mips dropped and nothing re-encoded, cell-instanced draws with far and horizon lists, Havok collision, hash-addressed publishing with a deploy check, `_source` on every number, the coverage and maps ledgers in CI. Its losses: colour maps capped at 1,024 on mid and high and 128 to 512 on low, normal and ORM a step under, **no pack built with `--ultra`** (no 2,048 maps, no LOD0 over 12,000 triangles anywhere); the budget cull drops 169 to 382 meshes a tier on Endor (decals, cables, leaves, torches, roots, rocks); every skinned actor dropped (Endor’s 425 shrubs); `decals.json` empty; the 0.5 m terrain detail resampled to 1 m and the far ground to 4 m; ground and scatter masks derived, not the game’s; the probes 64² with the sun clipped; the panorama 2,048 × 512 LDR clamped where the game has 8,192 × 2,048 HDR; the LUT 17³ of 33³; no Enlighten bounce (the 200 atlases never uploaded); the `models MB` gate counts GLBs only, not the packs’ KTX2 and bins; the four space packs still in git.

**The lanes** (`docs/superpowers/HANDOFF-bf2017*.md`, `HANDOFF-galaxy-engine.md`): Q1, Q2, Q4, Q6, S, V, X, C, N, U, R, G, L, colour, bots, screens, fighters, maps, Z, O, Q, M, A merged; T’s flip of the galaxy surface to `'nodes'` **pending**; Q3 (Enlighten) and Q5 (UASTC ultra colour, anisotropy) **unstarted**; the picture lane (the whole stack under the Battlefront world, the gallery at ultra) **unstarted**; E0 open (#888), E1 to E4 in session; no world passes `picture`; U’s batch, bundle, occlude and upscale options **all off** on main.

## 2. The game’s frame, and ours

Frostbite’s frame, as the papers and the records say it: a physically based camera (the VE record’s EV and compensation: Hoth day EV 10 with +1.5), Lagarde’s GGX with `f0 = 0.16 r²` and specular occlusion, Enlighten’s baked radiosity under a dynamic sun, four PCSS cascades (HFTS on Nvidia), HBAO, Hillaire’s unified volumetrics (the fog and the dust in every outdoor frame), a painted HDR sky dome with its sun, the five-Gaussian bloom confined to emitters, a linear tonemap and the 33³ grade, TAA, motion blur and depth of field in the hero shots, and photogrammetry-grade surface detail that holds at distance because the detail arrays tile under the base maps. The Steam frames show it: hard, high-contrast sun shadows grounded by contact AO; haze and lit dust; bloom only on the sabers, lamps and fire; a filmic, saturated grade; detail at every distance.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| exposure and tone | EV + compensation, linear tonemap, 33³ LUT after it | site scalar, hand shoulder, 17³ LUT over the encoded picture | the record’s EV, linear tonemap, LUT after (Q6) |
| sun and shadow | 4 PCSS cascades, contact shadows, cloud shadows | 1 PCF map, usually off for a baked floor mask | 4 PCSS cascades, contact and cloud shadows (S) |
| sky | 8,192 × 2,048 HDR panorama with the sun | gradient dome, noise clouds | Rayleigh and Mie plus the panorama, 2,048 × 512 LDR (Q6) |
| fog and air | unified volumetrics, height fog, god rays | FogExp2 tinted by the sky | height and volumetric fog, cones, god rays, flares (V) |
| bounce | Enlighten radiosity, probes with the sun | hemisphere light, PMREM of the dome, one probe at a time | SSGI, crossfading probes, the ultra probe grid; no Enlighten (Q3) |

| AO | HBAO | none | GTAO with the record’s numbers |

## 3. The decision

1. **The node renderer is the only renderer from the mid tier up on every Star Wars world; the classic renderer is the low tier’s.** `galaxy-surface` says `shading: 'nodes'`, and `pickBackend` gives it `webgpu` where the browser has WebGPU and `nodes-webgl` elsewhere (`src/runtime/backend.js`). On the mid tier a device without WebGPU takes the classic renderer (lane U’s laptop tables: the node chain over WebGL 2 costs about twice the frame at 1,600 × 900, and mid without WebGPU is the phones), on high and ultra the node renderer always. The budget rows (`src/lib/budgets.js`) and the pace stay the throttle; `headroomAt` (`src/runtime/quality.js:52`) gives the resolution steps.
2. **The quality switch is the owner’s, and it is checked.** `tp-quality` (`src/components/settings/settings.js`, `src/lib/device.js` `setQuality`) already picks low, mid, high, ultra or auto; the settings panel gains a line that says which renderer and backend the world runs on (WebGPU, WebGL 2 through the node renderer, or classic) and the `?gpu=` override it honours, with a test that a change of quality re-creates the world on the right renderer.
3. **Every layer on, as the record says.** On the node renderer each world passes `picture` (its VE record’s panorama, fog gradient, cloud shadow, LUT and Gaussians through `applyGameLight`), its placed lights, probes, decals, scatter, particles, volumetrics and weather, with the per-tier chains of `light/post.js`. Nothing is hand-tuned where the record has a number; where it has none, the site’s constant keeps its `_source`.
4. **Every detail the assets carry, drawn.** Every pack gains its ultra cut (LOD0 and 2,048 maps), the caps rise a step on mid and high, anisotropy 16 on every pack map, the decals filled, the dropped meshes restored where the row allows, per-instance variations read from the bins. What only the desktop can make (the HDR panoramas, the Enlighten atlases, the UASTC colour row, the 4,149 textures) is a listed ask, not silently approximated.
5. **The gates are the owner’s “pass everything”:** `npm run lint`, `npm test`, `npm run build`, `node scripts/health.mjs --check --skip build`, `npm run coverage:bf2017`, `npm run maps:bf2017`, `node scripts/bf2017-colour-check.mjs --check` (#881), and `BUDGET=1 QUALITY=high SCANS=game node scripts/galaxy-check.mjs surface <every world>` through the dev server in software GL with the node renderer, every world within its rows and with no cc0 fetch; on the owner’s laptop at ultra, every world under 16.7 ms p95 (`scripts/galaxy-budget.mjs`) with its shot in the gallery beside the game’s frame. A gate that cannot run in the cloud (ultra frame time) is a desktop workflow, never a skipped row.

## 6. Risks

- The node renderer over WebGL 2 is slower than the classic renderer at the same picture: the mid rule and the pace carry it; the low tier keeps the classic renderer whole.
- Seventeen worlds at high in software GL in CI is slow (Hoth loads in 53 s under the bucket): T2 runs them in CI in two shards and keeps `galaxy-check`’s `PHASE_WAIT`.
- Memory on the ultra packs at 2,048 maps: the rows and `fitTextures` cap the device; D2 records the numbers.
- The TSL bloom’s third: settled by measurement in U2, not by a constant.
- What the desktop owes (HDR panoramas, Enlighten) is the difference between “the record’s sky with its sun” and ours; the lanes draw the LDR copies and say so in the gallery.

## 7. AAA, the compute, the desktop and the stream (added 2026-10-11, after the owner’s second ask)

The owner asked for AAA quality, “depending on compute”, with Supabase to accelerate it, GeForce Now’s way of using a GPU and streaming considered for the local desktop, Frosty and the desktop connected where needed, and a blueprint (WebGPU, AgX or ACES tone mapping, SSAO, bloom, SSR or probe GI, HDR IBL, 2,048 to 4,096 PCF shadow maps) weighed, with a note on WebAssembly ports of engines. Point by point against this repository:

| the blueprint says | what is true here |
| --- | --- |
| use `WebGPURenderer`, the node pipeline | it is the engine (three r186, `src/runtime/webgpu.js`); the Star Wars worlds do not ask for it: lane T2 |
| ACES or AgX tone mapping, linear working space | the working space is linear already; **the game’s tone map is not ACES**: the VE record says `TonemapMethod_Linear` followed by its own 33³ LUT, and lane Q6 built that order. ACES or AgX would un-match the game. AgX is the site’s fallback only where a world has no record |
| SSAO | GTAO with the record’s HBAO numbers, built (`light/passes.js`); on when T2 lands |
| cinematic bloom | the record’s five-Gaussian bloom, built (Q6); the TSL-against-Unreal ratio is U2’s measurement |
| SSR or probe GI | SSR, SSGI, crossfading probes and the ultra probe grid, built; the game’s baked Enlighten bounce is the desktop’s (the 200 atlases, §4) |
| HDR environment maps | the game’s own probes, 64² with the sun clipped (D2 restores 128² with the sun); the HDR sky panoramas are the desktop’s re-encode |
| 2,048 to 4,096 PCF shadows | four PCSS cascades with cloud and contact shadows (lane S), 4,096 on ultra; on when T2 lands |

So the blueprint’s passes exist; the design’s lanes are what switch them on and feed them the record’s numbers. What the blueprint does not cover, and the owner’s ask adds:

**Compute, and what Supabase is for.** Supabase holds no GPU. It accelerates the picture in two ways: as the CDN for everything precomputed (the packs, the ultra cuts, the probes, the atlases, the panoramas, the hashed and immutable `site-assets`), and as the signalling store for a stream (below). The heavy work happens once, on the owner’s desktop, and ships as data. **Lane W** (the desktop) makes that a workflow on the self-hosted runner `[self-hosted, gpu]` (as `desktop-doctor.yml` and `gen3d.yml` run), dispatched from the Actions tab, with a job per ask: the HDR panoramas (BC6 → RGBA16F KTX2 through the exporter’s `ktx2_encode.py`), the 200 Enlighten atlases and the 4,149 textures uploaded, the UASTC colour row (Q5), the depot probe, the variation hashes already in the bins, and the frame-time gate at ultra under ANGLE d3d11 (U2’s 16.7 ms p95 for every world, the laptop tables’ way), each writing its evidence and opening a PR as the gen3d jobs do.

**GeForce Now’s way: the stream.** A cloud game streams a frame rendered on a server GPU as video over WebRTC, and sends the input back. The same works from the owner’s desktop: a headless Chromium with WebGPU draws the site’s own ultra picture (every layer on, the HDR panoramas and the atlases the desktop has, no budget rows), the frame is captured and encoded (NVENC through Chromium’s `getDisplayMedia` and `RTCPeerConnection`, or OBS’s WebRTC output), and the visitor’s page shows the video with the input sent back on a data channel. It is the one route to the game’s full frame on a phone. Its limits are the desktop’s (one stream per encoder session, the desktop awake, the owner’s upload), so it is a mode (“watch at ultra, streamed from the desktop”) beside the local renderer, never the site. **Lane R2** is its spike (and, added after the owner’s third ask, the game itself on the owner’s desktop as a second source of the same stream, the one way the real game reaches a browser): signalling through Supabase Realtime (the stack page `docs/stack/supabase.md`; the multiplayer stays on Nostr, `docs/stack/multiplayer-nostr.md`), a desktop sender script, a page receiver, the input channel, and the numbers that decide it (end-to-end latency, bitrate, the frame on the desktop, the owner’s upload), measured on the owner’s desktop through lane W’s workflow; a product decision follows the numbers, not before.

**WebAssembly.** WebAssembly is already where it pays here: Rapier (the physics), the Basis transcoder (every KTX2), the meshopt decoder (every GLB). It does not give a Frostbite: there is no Frostbite source to compile, and a Unity or Unreal port would be a different engine with its own pipeline, not these assets rendered as DICE renders them. The claims about AI-decompiled engine ports are not a path this design takes; the game’s records, maps and materials are read directly instead, which is the accuracy design’s whole method.

**Frosty and the desktop.** The exporter (`tool/bf2export.csproj`, on Frosty’s SDK) runs on the desktop and already wrote the drop; lane W gives it a workflow, so an ask from the cloud (a texture, a record type, a bake) is a dispatch, not a message. The owner connects the desktop by waking the runner (`scripts/desktop/setup-runner.ps1`, `desktop-doctor.yml`).

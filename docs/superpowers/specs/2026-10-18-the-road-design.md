# The Road: Middle-earth as a journey, the games at its end

Date: 2026-10-18. Lane: Middle-earth (`src/components/middleearth/`), a new world under it, and the map it all stands on. Rests on `2026-10-09-middle-earth-landing-design.md` (the desk, the candle and the sheet), the chapters’ specs (`2026-10-04-shire-world-design.md` through `2026-10-05-mount-doom-design.md`), `2026-10-07-living-characters-design.md` (the toy look, the Meshy cast on one skeleton, the creatures on `gait.js`), `2026-10-15-engine-floor-design.md` (one renderer, every new world a `'nodes'` module on the runtime) and `docs/health/RULES.md`. Written from `origin/main` at `ba818787` (#1021), with #1023 (FL2 Middle-earth: the chapters onto the runtime) open and merging. Architected here; built by Opus 5.5 sessions a task at a time from the plan beside it.

## What the owner asked

“Architect a way to make the LOTR world amazing and better. Use inspiration from the lore and everything, making cooler features and different stuff. Something really cool from the movies, going through the world. Keep the entire game, but I want something other than the game; the game should be at the end. A 3D experience with three.js through the world of Middle-earth, whatever is best. Detailed, amazing. The current map is also not accurate: make sure it’s accurate to Tolkien.”

## The answer in one paragraph

Middle-earth on the site is a map on a desk and twelve places you walk, each a town with quests, a kitchen and a side game. It is a collection of games; nothing on it is the story. This design adds **the Road**: one continuous three.js journey from Bag End to the Cracks of Doom and on to the Grey Havens, the way the films and the book go, with the visitor as the unseen tenth walker. The whole of it is a function of one number, how far along the road you are: the sun, the season, the weather, who walks with you, what the world is doing and which shot the camera takes all follow it, so the ride is deterministic, scrubbable, resumable, deep-linkable and testable without a canvas. Sixteen stages (Hobbiton, the Shire’s edge, Bree, Weathertop, the Ford, Rivendell, Caradhras and the Doors, Moria, Lothlórien, the Anduin, the Marshes and the Black Gate, Ithilien and the Morgul Vale, Cirith Ungol, Gorgoroth and the Mountain, the White City, the Havens), each a built world of its own streamed in as you come, each with the film’s set pieces performed round you by the cast the site already owns (twenty-nine rigged Meshy figures, 139 clips, 900-odd voiced lines) and the creatures it does not yet have (the Nazgûl and their horses, the Watcher, the Balrog, the crebain, the fell beasts, Shelob, the mûmakil, the Eagles, the Eye). The visitor walks, is carried on a horse, climbs a mallorn, paddles the Anduin, is carried by Sam and flown out by Gwaihir. The games are not touched: they wait at the end, where the Road closes on the desk and the map unrolls with every place on it and the words “Now walk it yourself”. A first visitor is shown the Road before the map; a returning one has it one button away. Under all of it, the sheet of the map is remade from geography rather than guesswork: every place, river, range, forest and coast in miles from Hobbiton, from the published distances and Tolkien’s own statements, projected onto the sheet by one tested function that the hub, the diorama, the pins, the flight and the Road all read. The Road is a `'nodes'` module on the runtime from its first commit, so it adds nothing to the floor the engine-floor lanes are taking down.

## 2. Goals, non-goals, anti-goals

### Goals, measured

On `high` at 1440 × 900 and on the phone size (390 × 844), in the container on `nodes-webgl` (the `webgpu` rows are the owner’s machines’, left empty and said so), by `scripts/perf-probe.mjs`’s new `road` journey, `scripts/autopilot-check.mjs --routes` with `--shots` and `--phone`, and the lane’s own `lab/road/measure.mjs`:

| what | target |
| --- | --- |
| the ride, start to end on auto-walk, no input | 20 to 30 minutes (binding); a stage 60 to 150 s; the stage briefs of §4.3 are kept whole and the road lengths and holds are what the builder tunes to fit (a beat is never cut to make the time) |
| a stage reachable by its address | `#/middle-earth/road?at=<stage>` opens on that stage’s first beat within 4 s in the container’s software renderer, 1.5 s on the owner’s desktop, the stage’s ground and cast in the first drawn frame (no pop-in of the near scene) |
| draws at any stage, `high` / `low` | ≤ 350 / ≤ 200 |
| triangles at any stage, `high` / `low` | ≤ 1,200,000 / ≤ 400,000 |
| main thread, median, `high` in the container | no worse than `/fly`’s `high` row at `ba818787` |
| download: the boot slice (the first stage, the four hobbits, Gandalf, the nature and farm packs) | ≤ 8 MB |
| download: the whole Road, ridden end to end | ≤ 45 MB; `WORLD_MB['/middle-earth/road']` the measured number |
| memory over a whole ride (`rt.assets.stats()` and `renderer.info.memory` at the end against the second stage) | within 10 %: stages disposed, figures retained |
| the share of the viewport under the Road’s UI while walking (ribbon, prompt, lore card), desktop / phone | ≤ 14 % / ≤ 22 %; during a beat (the card hidden) ≤ 8 % / ≤ 12 % |
| the sheet’s geography | every relation in the research note’s “what is wrong today” list holds on the new sheet; `sheet.test.js` pins each |
| pure rules without a test | 0 |
| GLSL sites in the Road’s closure | 0 (`src/runtime/shading.test.js`) |

And by eye, in each pull request: the stage’s shots, desktop and phone, beside the film’s moment they stand for (named, not pasted).

### Non-goals

- The chapters, their quests, side games, kitchens, hidden places, ghosts and HUDs: untouched. Nothing in `shire/`, `towns/`, `rush/` changes, and #1023’s files (`module.js`, `stage.js`, `tick.js`, the towns’ `*World.jsx` and `scene.js`, `pages/MiddleEarth.jsx`) are not this lane’s to edit beyond the one route the page gains.
- Porting the chapters to `'nodes'` (lane FL3’s, by #1023’s session).
- New figures from Meshy, new voices, a score: the Road ships on what is committed; §4.7 and §10 say what the owner may add later and where it drops in.
- The universe map’s Middle-earth planet and the landing on it.
- WebGPU-only features: everything draws on `nodes-webgl` too, the way every `'nodes'` world must (`backend.test.js`).
- A free-roam open world: the Road is a road. You may stop, look about, step back and run ahead; you may not leave it.

### Anti-goals (a failure even if it works)

- A cutscene you watch with your hands in your lap: the visitor must always be the one moving, and able to stop and look.
- A HUD with numbers, scores, timers or objectives on the Road. It is not a game.
- Copied dialogue: the site’s own words, except the short famous lines the clips already carry (the autopilot’s standing rule).
- Any pixel of the Tolkien Estate’s maps copied: the sheet is drawn from numbers.
- A second art: the Road wears the toy look the cast was made for (big heads, flat colours on the house look, ink lines), not a new one.
- The hub getting worse: a returning visitor who wants the games must be one click from them.

## 4. The shape

### 4.1 One road, one number

The Road is a polyline through sixteen stages, each built in its own frame (metres, +z along the road at its start, the floating origin reset to the stage’s origin on entry), chained end to start with an 80 m overlap so a seam is never in view. `road/line.js` (pure, tested) holds the whole: `STAGES` in order with each stage’s `length` (metres of road), `total`, `at(s) → { stage, local, metres, heading }` for a progress `s` in `[0, 1]`, `sOf(stageId, local)`, `nearest(stageId, metres)`; a stage’s own `ROAD` (its control points, a Catmull-Rom through them sampled at 1 m into a table at build) gives `pointAt(metres) → { x, y, z, tangent }` and `heightAt(x, z)` reads the stage’s ground.

Everything is a function of `s` and a local clock:

- **The clock** (`road/clock.js`, pure, tested): `step(state, dt, input) → state`. Walking forward at 2.5 m/s (brisk, the camera leading the walkers a little; a film’s walk, not a hike), running at 6.0 m/s while Shift is held or the touch button is pressed, backward at 1.5 m/s on S. **Auto-walk** (on by default; Space toggles; any held input pauses it for 6 s then resumes) walks at the same pace and **holds at a beat**: a beat has a `hold` (seconds the walkers stop while the scene plays, 6 to 25 s; most beats are performed as you pass and hold nothing); auto-walk waits it out, a visitor walking on through it does not wait (the scene keeps playing behind them, as a scene would). Reduced motion: no auto-walk; the clock steps between beats on Next (Enter, or the button), each beat a still with its scene at its end state. The state is `{ s, mode: 'walk' | 'run' | 'back' | 'hold' | 'still', held, auto, speed }`; `clock.test.js` holds that a whole ride on auto takes 20 to 30 minutes from `STAGES`’ lengths and holds, and each stage 60 to 150 s (a stage’s road is 150 to 300 m; Hobbiton’s lane, say, 250 m with 40 s of holds). The briefs are binding and the lengths give; the test is the ledger.
- **Frames**: a stage is built about its own origin (0, 0, 0), its road starting at +z 0, and never grows past 1,200 m in any axis. The world keeps the stage you are in at the scene’s origin; the next stage’s group stands at the current stage’s end transform (`line.js`’s `endOf(stage) → { at, heading }`, so the overlap lines up); on crossing into it the world re-roots in one frame (the old group to the negative offset, the new at 0, the camera and the walkers carried with it), as Moria’s zones are offset today but moved instead of left. `rt.origin` is not used (it shifts in 50 km cells and `reset()` zeroes it; `anchor()` is still answered for the runtime’s bookkeeping). `at(s)` answers in a stage’s own frame (`{ stage, local, point, tangent }`), and nothing asks for a point two stages away.
- **The travel mode** (per stage, data): `walk` (Hobbiton to the Ford), `horse` (the Ford: you are on Asfaloth behind Arwen, the camera low and bounced by the gallop from `creatures.js`’s `GALLOP`), `climb` (Caras Galadhon’s stair, Cirith Ungol’s stair: the road rises in a spiral or zig-zag; the pace halves), `boat` (the Anduin: the camera at the gunwale, the boat on the water’s own height, the paddle’s rhythm), `carried` (Sam carries Frodo on the Mountain: the camera at Sam’s shoulder, swaying), `eagle` (out of Mordor: the road is in the air, 400 m up, Gwaihir’s wingbeat the sway, the pace 40 m/s), `ship` (the Havens: the last 200 m are on the water, slow, into the light). The mode changes what the clock’s speeds are and which camera rig the director uses; it changes no control.
- **The fellowship** (`road/fellowship.js`, pure, tested): `walkers(s) → [{ id, order, state }]`: who walks with you and in what state. Frodo alone leaving Bag End; Sam from the field’s edge; Merry and Pippin from Maggot’s crop; Strider from Bree; Glorfindel’s part goes to Arwen at the Ford (the films’; a lore card says the book has Glorfindel); nine from Rivendell in the film’s order (Gandalf, Legolas, Gimli, Aragorn, Boromir, Merry, Pippin, Sam, Frodo); eight from the Bridge, `grieving` to Lórien; Frodo and Sam alone from Amon Hen; Gollum `leading` from Emyn Muil; Sam `carrying` on the Mountain; Frodo and Sam `lying` at the end of the Mountain; the four hobbits at Cormallen; Frodo, Bilbo, Gandalf, Elrond, Galadriel at the Havens, Sam, Merry and Pippin `staying`. The test pins each count and each change at its stage. A walker’s state picks its clip (`walk`, `run`, `walk.formal` for the elves, `tired`, `crawl` for Gollum, `lifted` for the carried, `lie.down`) and its place in the line; their speed is the clock’s, their feet paced by `locomotion.js` so nothing skates.
- **The beats** (per stage, data, in `stages/<id>/beats.js`): `[{ id, at (metres into the stage), hold (s), kind, shot, lines, fx }]`, sorted, pure, tested (`beats.test.js` walks every stage: sorted, within the length, holds sum under the goal, every `shot` a known rig, every voiced line in `voicelines.js`). A beat is **entered** when `metres` crosses `at` forward and **left** when it crosses back; its scene is a function of `(metres − at, time since entered)`, so walking back past it puts the world back where it was before (Gandalf on the bridge again), and nothing one-shot (a burst of particles, a sound) fires twice without leaving and coming back.

The address keeps you: `?at=<stage>` and, finer, `?at=<stage>:<metres>`; `rt.saves` keeps `tp-road` `{ v: 1, s, ridden, at }` (registered with a version; the runtime’s saves migrate), so the hub can say “Carry on from Moria”.

### 4.2 The film grammar

`road/director.js` (pure, tested): `pose(ctx) → { at, lookAt, fov, roll, dof }` for the camera each frame from `{ stage, metres, beat, mode, walkers, look: { yaw, pitch }, reduced }`. The rigs, as data on a beat or a stage, each a function of the walker you follow (Frodo, or the one the beat names) and the road’s tangent:

| rig | what |
| --- | --- |
| `follow` | behind and above the lead walker, 3.2 m back, 1.7 m up, looking a little past them along the road; the default while walking |
| `lead` | ahead of the line, walking backward, the Fellowship coming at you (Rivendell’s setting out, the ridge in Hollin) |
| `side` | tracking beside the line at their height (the Shire’s lanes, the Marshes) |
| `low` | 0.5 m off the ground, looking up (the Argonath, the Doors of Durin, the Morannon) |
| `crane` | rising from `follow` to 30 m over 8 s, the reveal (Rivendell’s valley, Dwarrowdelf, Caras Galadhon, Minas Morgul, the Mountain) |
| `wide` | far and still, the walkers small in a big frame (Caradhras, Gorgoroth, the Pelennor) |
| `seat` | fixed where the beat puts it, the scene in front (the Council, the Mirror, the Cracks of Doom) |
| `ride` | on the mount or the vessel (`horse`, `boat`, `eagle`, `ship`): offset in its frame, sway from its gait or its wave |

The visitor’s look is added on top, never instead: the pointer, a drag or a touch drag turns the camera up to ±55° of yaw and ±25° of pitch about the rig’s framing (`runtime/look.js`’s controller in `drag` mode on a fine pointer, `touch` on a coarse one; no pointer lock, this is not a shooter), eased back to centre over 2 s once let go; during a `seat` the look is ±20°. Between rigs the director eases over the beat’s `cut` (0: a hard cut on the beat’s first frame, the film’s grammar for the Balrog; 1.5 s otherwise). `director.test.js` holds the offsets, the clamps, the ease, and that no pose ever puts the camera under the ground the stage’s `heightAt` reports (the pose takes `heightAt` and keeps 0.4 m over it).

The lens: `fov` 50° walking, 38° in a `seat`, 62° on the eagle; the depth of field (`lib/three/camera/cinematic.js`’s `dofFor`) on `high` and `ultra` only, focused on the followed walker, in `seat` and `low` rigs only (elsewhere it costs more than it gives). **Letterbox**: while a beat holds, two bars of `--hud-bar` (7 % of the height each) come in over 0.6 s and go as the hold ends; off on a phone in portrait, off under reduced motion, off from the Menu. The kit’s `Film` is a muted video with captions, not this; the bars already exist once, as the Death Star inside’s `.ds-letterbox` (`deathstar/inside/ui/Hud.jsx`, `inside.css`), so they are lifted into the HUD kit (`runtime/hud/Letterbox.jsx`, its rule in `hud.js` tested: when it shows, how tall, never over the prompt), the Death Star’s page taking the kit part with no pixel changed (the repair recipe), the Road the second user (one drawing per concept). **Title cards**: each stage opens with its name and its date in the Tale of Years on glass at the bottom left (`--fs-display-3` Cinzel for the name, `--fs-xs` muted for the date), in for 4 s; the first stage opens with the Road’s own card (“The Road”, “Bag End to the fire”, “Hold W to walk · drag to look · Space for the road to walk itself”).

### 4.4 The world under it

Each stage is a built scene of its own, made when the walker is one stage away and disposed when two behind, through `rt.assets` (owner `road:<stage>`), so at most three stages are built and at most two are drawn (the one you are in and, through the overlap, the next).

- **The ground** (`road/ground.js`, drawing; `road/terrain.js`, pure, tested): a stage is a heightfield of 1,024 × 512 m at 2 m (`high`, `mid`) or 4 m (`low`), summed from `lib/land/layers.js`’s layers under the stage’s spec (swells, hills, a ridge, mountains round the horizon) and cut by the stage’s own features: the **road flat** (a new pure `lib/land/paths.js`: a polyline levelled to its own height with an eased band either side, as `flats.js` levels a pad; the galaxy’s and the flight’s land may use it later), rivers as `lib/land/rivers.js` carves them or, where a stage names one (the Brandywine, the Bruinen, the Anduin, the Morgulduin), as a channel along a given line, lakes as flats under the water. Built on the main thread in two frames under 8 ms each, else in `rt.workers` (the builder measures and says). Drawn as a grid fine near the road (1 m) and coarse beyond (8 m), one mesh, through `groundmapNodes` (the stage paints its ground colours, grass, soil, snow, ash, into the picture the shader reads) under `houseNodes`’ house, with `groundworkNodes`’ baked floor light on `high` where a stage has props worth grounding (Bree, Rivendell, Moria, the Havens). `heightAt` reads the grid as it is drawn.
- **The scatter** (`road/scatter.js`): `kitNodes`’ pools over the nature and farm packs, placed by the stage’s table (which models, how thick, on which ground, clumped how: the shape of `lib/land/flora.js`), instanced, culled by distance in bands, never on the road flat; `foliageNodes` and `windNodes` on every crown; `recolourNodes` for the season (Rivendell’s gold, Lórien’s gold and silver, Mordor’s grey on dead trees, Caradhras’ white). On `low` half the counts and no grass; on `mid` the grass thinned.
- **The water** (`road/water.js`, TSL, under 200 lines): one plane material: a slow Gerstner ripple on two octaves, the sky’s colour by Fresnel, foam at an edge from the ground’s height under it, a flow direction; the Brandywine, the Bruinen (with its rising ridge driven by a uniform), the Anduin (with the boats on its height), the Marshes’ pools (still, dark, a second layer for the faces), Mirrormere (still, with stars drawn in it by day), the Mirror, the Gulf of Lune. `nodes-webgl` draws it as WebGPU does (no storage buffers).
- **The sky** (`road/sky.js`, TSL dome, under 200 lines): a gradient by sun height, a sun disc and halo, a moon by phase, stars by darkness, two layers of cloud as scrolling noise, a **pall** (Mordor’s: a dark lid from a direction, fed by the stage), driven by `road/daylight.js` (pure, tested): `light(s) → { sun: { az, el, colour }, ambient, fog: { colour, density }, moon, stars, pall, exposure }`, piecewise over the stages (each stage names its start and end light; the function eases between; the test pins each stage’s start). The house’s fog hook takes the sky’s colour.
- **The weather** (`road/weather.js`): the particle layer under the Battlefront effects, not the effects themselves: `lib/three/particles/emitter.js` (the CPU step, the path the node renderer takes on WebGL 2), `gpu.js` (the same on WebGPU) and `sprites.js` (one instanced quad a pool), driven by the Road’s own small records in `emitter.js`’s record shape (`src/data/middleearth/fx/*.json`, eight: rain, snow, ash, embers, motes, spray, smoke, fire; each a few curves, not a Frostbite graph), one pool a kind, spawned by the stage and the beats; on `low` the counts quartered and no collision. `effects.js` and `sheets.js` are not touched (they read the game’s records and the galaxy’s sheet folder); if `sprites.js` resolves a sheet only under the galaxy’s folder, it gains a `dir` option, an addition. Sheets: none of the game’s on disk fit (the committed ones are blast, glow, a blackbody ramp and a scorch), so the lane paints its own 4 × 1 sheets with `scripts/road-sheets.mjs` (a flake, a drop, a flame, a mote, a leaf, a petal, a smoke puff, an ash flake; 512², WebP, under `public/models/middleearth/fx/`, in `pack.js`).
- **Light**: one directional sun with a shadow map on `high` (2,048, following the walkers), none on `low`; the stage’s point lights from the kit (`lib/three/light/placed.js`’s pool if its import is clean, else the stage’s own, at most 8 lit at once); Gandalf’s staff, the Phial, the torches as lights the cast carries.
- **The origin**: as §4.1’s frames: each stage about its own origin, the world re-rooted at a stage’s edge; `rt.origin` unused.

### 4.5 The cast and the creatures

- **Figures**: the Meshy cast through `lib/three/rig.js` (`loadFigure`, `figure`, `pose`), `animator.js` (the base states, the layers, the look), `locomotion.js` (feet paced to ground) and `clipLibrary.js`, the way `cast3d.js` wires them for the towns, in the Road’s own wrapper `road/cast.js` (about 250 lines): `loadCast(names, rt) → { ready }` (through `rt.assets.gltf`, retained for the whole ride, fetched one stage ahead), `walker(id) → { group, set({ base, full, upper, look }), play(clip), tick(dt, camera) }`. `cast3d.js` itself is not used: it loads through `lib/three/gltfCache` (so the memory goal could not be read off `rt.assets`), imports `towns/watchers.js` (a town), and gates on `window` (no Node test). One skeleton, one clip library, no new rig. Gandalf’s staff, Sting, the Phial, Aragorn’s torches are the kit’s `grip.js` hand poses with a built prop. On `low` the far walkers of the line (past the fourth) are drawn as `figureRows`’ rows from the same clips (one draw a part), as Battlefront’s far soldiers are; a crowd is rows on every tier.
- **The toy look**: `inkNodes`’ hull round every figure (the Middle-earth cast’s ink line, as the towns draw them), the house’s flat shade; no scan on a body.
- **Gollum**: the hobbit figure (`cast/hobbit.glb`) recoloured pale grey-green by `recolourNodes` with its hair hidden, scaled to 0.85, on `crawl`, `crawl.idle`, `crouch`, `crouch.walk` and `sit.ground`, his head turned by the animator’s look to Frodo always; the toy look forgives the rest. If it reads wrong in the shots, the owner makes a Gollum with Meshy as the cast was made (the credit track) and drops `cast/gollum{,-idle,-walk,-run}.glb` in; `road/cast.js` prefers it when it exists.
- **Creatures** (`road/creatures/`, code-built on `gait.js` and `creatures.js`’s leg rigs, each under 300 lines, each with a pure `*.rules.js` where it has rules):
  - **Horses** (`horse.js`): the farm pack’s horse (`public/kit/farm/horse.glb`, rigged, with its own Idle, Walk, WalkSlow, Run and Jump clips: `2026-10-08-kit-worlds-design.md` §29; no world uses it yet), its clips through `animator.js` and its feet paced by `locomotion.js`, recoloured black for the Nazgûl’s, white for Asfaloth, bay for Gandalf’s cart horse, and at 0.7 for the hobbits’ ponies; a rider is a figure in `sit.ground` parented to its back (there is no riding clip; the sit and the horse’s bounce read as riding in the toy look). `GALLOP` from `creatures.js` drives only the camera’s sway.
  - **The Nazgûl** (`nazgul.js`): a rider in a cloak (a cone of cloth over a hobbit figure at ×1.3, the cloak swayed by `windNodes`, the face a void), on foot or mounted; in the Ring vision a second body under the cloak, a pale king in a crown, emissive white, shown by the vision’s uniform. One instance per rider, at most nine.
  - **The crebain** (`crebain.js`): 300 points on a boids rule (`crebain.rules.js`, pure: cohesion, alignment, separation, a goal that moves), drawn as one instanced wing-pair with a flap by phase.
  - **The Watcher** (`watcher.js`): twelve tentacles, each a tube on a sine of its own phase, out of the lake’s plane, one of them taking Frodo.
  - **The Balrog** (`balrog.js`): a dark giant, 9 m: a body of black rock (the orc figure at ×6 under the matcap in black with cracks of emissive fire, `recolourNodes` with an emissive mask), wings of smoke (the puffs in `SMOKE` and `FIRE` ramps as two sheets that beat), a mane of fire (the particles), a whip of fire (a ribbon of quads along a Catmull-Rom that cracks), and the light it casts (one point light, red). ## 9. Decisions

- **A journey over more game.** The owner’s word: something other than the game, the game at the end. The Road is not a game; nothing in it scores.
- **One number.** Everything a function of `s`: it makes the Road testable in Node, scrubbable, resumable and deep-linkable, and it keeps the walkers honest (walk back and the world is as it was).
- **The visitor moves, the world performs.** Auto-walk is a convenience, not the default experience; it pauses the moment a hand is on the keys.
- **Toy, not epic.** The Road wears the cast’s toy look. The epic is in the scale of the land and the grammar of the camera, not in a new art the site cannot afford.
- **Sixteen stages, not ten.** The chapters are the places you can play; the Road is the journey, and the journey has the Ford, the Doors, the Anduin, the Vale, the City and the Havens that the chapters do not.
- **Own words, the clips kept.** The standing rule; the film’s own voice where the site already has the clip, the site’s voice everywhere else.
- **`'nodes'` from the first commit.** The engine floor is taking the classic renderer down; a new `'glsl'` world would be a step back it would then have to port.
- **Nothing from the towns.** Their builders carry GLSL in their closure; the Road builds its own on the nodes stack and the kit. The chapters keep theirs; FL3 ports them in their own time.
- **The sheet from numbers.** Accurate because it is data, and drawn by the site, so it is the site’s own picture.
- **A first visit rides.** The owner said the game should be at the end; a new visitor sees the Road, then the map. One line turns it off.
- **No score, no new figures, no new voices in this lane.** The Road ships on what is committed; the owner adds on the credit track where §10 says.

Settled after the builder check (a reviewer who read the design cold and said where a wrong guess would hurt), each the architect’s call:

- **The time**: the whole ride 20 to 30 minutes is binding, the stage briefs are kept whole, the road lengths and holds give (§2, §4.1); the pace is 2.5 m/s.
- **The order against #1023**: the spine touches only new files, `App.jsx`’s route and the route’s rows; the hub’s and the page’s changes and the sheet wait for #1023 to merge (§8).
- **The weather**: the emitter, the GPU twin and the sprites directly, the Road’s own records and painted sheets; `effects.js` and `sheets.js` untouched (§4.4).
- **The cast**: `road/cast.js` on the lib, through `rt.assets`; `cast3d.js` copied in shape, not imported (§4.5).
- **The frames**: a stage about its own origin, the world re-rooted at a stage’s edge, `rt.origin` unused (§4.1).
- **The post chain**: the Road’s own description; `grade` is the Road’s TSL node; no `fog` pass; traa falls to smaa on `nodes-webgl` as the chain does (§4.6).
- **The letterbox**: the Death Star’s bars lifted into the kit, not drawn a second time (§4.2).

## 10. Left to the builder, and to the owner

The builder decides, within this design: the stages’ exact lengths and holds (the clock’s test is the ledger); each stage’s layers and scatter tables; the shapes of the built landmarks; which existing voiced lines fit which beats; the fx records’ numbers; whether `cast3d.js` is used as is; whether the ground is built in a worker; `BatchedMesh` against merging per stage; the inset’s size; what `low` draws for each creature.

The owner, on the credit track, after the first pull requests: a Gollum figure from Meshy (`cast/gollum*.glb`), horses with turnable legs if the farm pack’s will not do, the new voiced lines run through `scripts/voices` (the Road’s `voicelines.js` lists them; until then they show as words), and a score if ever licensed. Each drops into a path this design names; nothing else changes.

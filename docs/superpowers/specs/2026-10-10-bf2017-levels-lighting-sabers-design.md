And three things that are *not* there and will not be unless the desktop exporter grows:

- **Audio.** `GUIDE.md`: 17,509 sound assets, "not exported". The first design's lane F waits on an upload that is not coming; it waits on an exporter change (Frosty can write `.wav` from a `SoundWaveAsset`; a `bf2export sound` pass is the desktop side's task, not the site's).

- **Placed lights and effects.** The map builder counts them (Hoth: 1,234 lights, 648 effects) and writes none. `bf2export lights` on the desktop would give lane G the hangar's lamps and lane F the effect spawn points. Asked for in the hand-off; until then the site's own lamps stand.

## The review of #802: what stands, what is corrected

The pipeline design's heart stands: fetch ahead, import to the site's cuts, credit, the rig whole, the game's clips on it, people first. These are corrected:

1. **Lane S duplicates what shipped.** `main` already has the asset host: `src/lib/assetBase.js` (bucket first, local after one failure, `VITE_ASSET_BASE`), `src/lib/assetPath.js` (`<base>/<hash12>/<path>`), `scripts/assets-upload.mjs` (by content hash, a year's cache, never overwritten, `--prune`), `src/data/assets-manifest.json`, the packs and the service worker carrying bucket URLs (planet flight lane I, `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-10-09-asset-hosting.md`; the decision `docs/decisions/2026-10-09-heavy-assets-mirrored-on-supabase-storage.md`). Lane S's `assetUrl.js`, `galaxyAssets.json`, `assets-publish.mjs`, `assets-ignore.mjs` and a second bucket `site-assets` would be a second copy of all of that. **Lane S is cut to what is missing**: the fetch pool with retry, timeout, abort and progress (`src/lib/net/assetFetch.js`, called from `assetBase.js`'s `withFallback` path so every loader gets it), the short-body check, the small-cut-first swap (`progressive.js`), `scripts/lib/pool.mjs` for the scripts, and `stream-check.mjs`. `REMOTE` in `assets-upload.mjs` gains `models/galaxy/bf2017` and `textures/galaxy/bf2017`; one manifest, one bucket, one env name. Its plan is amended in the hand-off, not rewritten.
2. **Lane W places by hand what the game already placed.** W's kits-merged-by-material, grid snapping and "where the built ones stood" are made unnecessary by the maps: the game's own layout of Echo Base, the bunker, Mos Eisley, the temple grounds, Theed, Tipoca, Kachirho, Cloud City and Scarif is in the `.bin`. **Lane L replaces lane W.** The kit import (`--kit`) survives as the level pack's mesh step. W's task 1 (the sky) moves to lane G, which reads the VisualEnvironment records instead of picking a probe by eye.
3. **Lighting is data, not a guess.** Each level's `sky[]` names VisualEnvironment blueprints (`data/Levels/Lighting/<World>/<Weather>/VE_*.json.gz`, 192 records). A record carries `OutdoorLightComponentData` (sun colour, final sun illuminance, sky and ground colour, the cloud-shadow texture), `SkyComponentData` (Rayleigh and Mie coefficients, polarisation, the cloud layers' colours), `FogComponentData` (colour, start, end, the curves), `TonemapComponentData` (EV, exposure compensation, bloom scale), `ColorCorrectionComponentData` (brightness, contrast, saturation, the grading LUT `T_CC_<World>_<Weather>`), `WindComponentData`, `EnlightenComponentData` (bounce and sky colours), `DynamicAOComponentData`. The site's `site.sky`, `site.light`, fog, exposure, bloom and wind can be *derived* from these for every world and weather the game has, calibrated once. That is lane G.
4. **Level textures stay KTX2.** The first design re-encodes every texture to WebP below ultra, on a bytes argument measured on one hero. For a whole level (Hoth: 602 meshes, hundreds of materials) the binding constraint is GPU memory, not bytes: a 1024² WebP decodes to 4 MB of RGBA8 on the GPU; the uploader's ETC1S colour map is 0.5 MB and stays compressed on every GPU; its UASTC normal 1 MB. The uploader already encodes hybrid (ETC1S for maps bound as colour, UASTC q6 for the rest, `ktx2_color.txt`), so the level pack takes the bucket's KTX2 as it is, downsized per tier by the import, and the loader's KTX2 path (`lib/three/gltf.js`, lazy transcoder) reads it. People keep the first design's WebP mix (few maps, faces). The "60 textures a world" contract becomes a GPU-bytes contract: 256 MB desktop, 128 MB phone, read from `renderer.info.memory.textures` and the formats, written in the evidence.
5. **Performance is an architecture, not a budget row.** The first design holds worlds to the rows but says nothing about how a 24,000-instance level draws inside them. Section "How a level draws" below is the answer: cells, instancing, LOD bands, a far ring, progressive load, abort on leave.
6. **The planet skins are nobody's.** The first design's section 5 notes "planets for the space layer" and no phase takes them. Lane K does.
7. **The saber lane's inputs.** The first design (section 7) says the game's strokes "are here" and leaves their *use* to the saber-forms lane's mirrored-clip design. The clip set is richer than mirroring: six strikes and a second six, their returns, four directional blocks, six blocked reactions, three staggers each way, four dodges, the dash, the jump attack. Lane X turns the set into stroke tables and feeds the combat rules from them; the forms lane then has no mirroring to do.

What the first design said about audio ("when it lands") and clips ("the shape is not known") is answered above: the clips are glTF on the game's skeletons (10,106 of 10,270 up, the rest renamed and queued), and audio needs an exporter.

## The approaches weighed

**A. Keep lane W: hand-build each world from the game's pieces.** Rejected. It rebuilds, by eye and by a grid, what 873,000 transforms already state; a session would spend its days placing hangar floors and get Echo Base wrong.

**B. Load the game's level files at run time, as the desktop's `cloud_viewer.html` does.** Rejected as the shipped path: the raw `.bin` names every sub-level and every mesh at LOD0; the browser would fetch 600 GLBs and their 2048² maps to draw Hoth. Kept as the *tool*: the level pack is built from it.

**C. A level pack per world, cut and cell-streamed, under the site's rules (recommended).** A script reads the map, the terrain and the physics, picks the arena (the sub-levels that are the playable map, not the lobby, the cinematics or the outro), rebases it on the site's landing spot, cuts it into cells, picks each mesh's cut per tier from the LOD chain, downsizes its KTX2 maps per tier, writes one `level.json`, one `.bin` per cell and one GLB per mesh cut under `public/models/galaxy/bf2017/levels/<world>/`, and the bucket serves them by hash through `assetBase.js`. The world's site keeps its people, missions, zones and weather; its ground becomes the game's heightmap plus the site's flats; its props become the pack. The rest of this page is C.

## The design

### How a level draws (lane L)

**The pack.** `scripts/bf2017-level.mjs <map> --world hoth --spot <x> <z> [--subs Hoth_01,Hoth_012,Content] [--arena 1536]` writes `public/models/galaxy/bf2017/levels/<world>/`:

- `level.json`: `{ world, map, origin: [x, y, z] (the game's point that is the site's 0, 0, 0), yaw, cell: 128, arena: half-size in metres, cells: { "cx,cz": { bin, bytes, bounds, count, draws: [{ mesh, lod: { low, mid, high, ultra }, offset, count }] } }, far: { bin, bytes, draws } (one list for the whole arena at the last LOD, for beyond the mid band and for the first frame), horizon: { bin, draws } (the backdrop pieces outside the arena: ridges, far buildings, at the last LOD), meshes: [{ name, glb: { far, lod1, plain, ultra }, tris, bounds, mats }], terrain: { near: { png, metresPerPixel: 1, min, size }, far: { png, metresPerPixel: 2, min, size }, scale, offset }, shadowCache: { png, bounds } | null, physics: { per mesh: convex shapes or 'trimesh' } }`.
- `cells/<cx>_<cz>.bin`: the instances of that cell as the map packs them (Float32 position × 3, Int16 quaternion × 4, Float32 scale × 3), rebased to the site's frame, sorted by mesh so a draw is one contiguous range.
- `meshes/<name>.<cut>.glb`: the game's LOD for that cut (the chain picked as phase 0's `cutsFor`: `far` ≤ 700 triangles, `lod1` ≤ 2,500, `plain` ≤ 12,000, `ultra` LOD0), meshopt, materials pointing at the pack's shared textures.
- `tex/<map>.<size>.ktx2`: the bucket's derived KTX2 (colour, `__normal`, `__orm`) downsized by dropping mip levels (`scripts/ktx2.mjs` gains `--drop-mips`: a KTX2's levels are already there; no re-encode) to 512 (low), 1024 (mid, high), 2048 (ultra). Shared across cells and meshes; one material per game material.
- `terrain/near.png` (16-bit, 1 m a pixel, the arena plus 256 m), `terrain/far.png` (2 m a pixel, the whole 8 km), with the record's scale and offset so `lib/land/layers.js` reads heights in metres.

**The ground.** `lib/land/layers.js` gains an `image` layer: `{ type: 'image', near, far, scale, offset }` that `terrain.js`'s `heightAt` samples bilinearly (near where it covers, far beyond), so the game's ground is one layer among the site's and the site's flats (`lib/land/flats.js`) still level the landing pad and the built places over it. `ground.js` draws it as it draws any grid; `ground.detail` and the splat stay the site's. The flight (`/fly/hoth`, `lib/land/cell.js`) reads the same layer, so the game's mountains stand under the ship too.

**The cells.** `src/components/galaxy/surface/level/` (new, lane-owned): `levelPack.js` (pure: which cells are near, mid, far for a position; which draws a cell has per tier; the instance ranges), `levelScene.js` (three: one `InstancedMesh` per draw, matrices from the `.bin`, the material shared, bounds per cell for frustum culling, the near band's instances casting shadows within `SHADOW.extent`, nothing else), `levelStream.js` (the fetch order: `level.json`, then the far list and the near terrain (walkable in under 4 MB), then the 3 × 3 near cells nearest first, then the ring beyond; every fetch through `assetBase.js` and lane S's pool with a priority; one `AbortController` per world, aborted at dispose; a cell left behind is disposed when more than two cells away). Bands are the budget rows' `near` and `mid`: within `near` the `plain` cut (ultra: `ultra`), to `mid` the `lod1`, beyond the `far` list, nothing past the horizon's reach. Low and mid tiers skip the near cut entirely (`lod1` near, `far` beyond) and halve the horizon.

**The budget.** The rows do not move. Per tier the pack writes a `weight` per draw (instances × bounding volume); `levelPack.js`'s `fitTo(row)` drops the lightest draws (snow debris, cables, small clutter) until the cell's triangles and calls fit the row's share (a 3 × 3 of near cells takes 70% of the row; the far list 20%; the people and effects the rest), and the PR's table says what each tier dropped. `galaxy-check.mjs surface <world>` under `BUDGET=1` at every tier is the gate.

**Collision.** The walk layer's solids come from the mesh's collision GLB bounds and, for the pieces you walk on (floors, ramps, stairs), its collision triangles through `walker.js`'s height-map solids (`docs/superpowers/HANDOFF-galaxy-roam.md`'s rule: solids do not step up, stairs are floors). Where the Rapier body lane (#781) is on `main`, the near cells' Havok shapes (`web/physics/`, convex polytopes) become fixed colliders through `lib/physics/fromModel.js`'s pattern, and the heightmap through `lib/physics/heightfield.js` (64 m cells, already there). The pack writes both; the scene uses what the world has.

**What the site keeps.** The world's `sites/<file>.js` entry gains `level: 'hoth'` and loses its built kit list for that world; its `life`, `missions`, `zones`, `weather`, `look` stay; its `ground` becomes `{ layers: [image], flats }`. `echoLayout.js` and the Hoth props it built go when Hoth's pack draws the hangar; the deathstar interior keeps its own lane (its room layout is gameplay).

**Order.** Hoth (`levels/mp/hoth_01`: 24,532 instances, 602 meshes, terrain world + detail), Endor (`endor_01`), Tatooine (`tatooine_01`), Yavin (`yavin_01`), Kashyyyk (`kashyyyk_01`), Naboo (`naboo_01`, Theed; `naboo_02` the palace), Kamino (`kamino_01`, no terrain: platforms), Geonosis (`s5_1`), Jabba's palace (`s2_2`), Scarif (`s9_3/scarif`), Bespin (`s2/cloudcity_01`), Felucia (`s8`), Kessel (`s3`), the Death Star II (`deathstar02_01`). Never the sequel levels (jakku, starkiller, takodana, crait, the space maps of the First Order).

### The light (lane G)

**From the records.** `scripts/bf2017-light.mjs <world> [--weather Sunny_01]` reads the level's `sky[]` names from the map, fetches the VisualEnvironment records (`data/…/VE_*.json.gz`) and writes `src/data/bf2017/light/<world>.json`: one entry per weather the level has (Hoth: Sunny, Sunset, Cloudy, Blizzard, Interior). A pure, tested `src/lib/three/gameLight.js` turns an entry into the site's shapes: `siteLightFrom(entry) → { sky: { zenith, horizon, haze, hazeColor, suns: [{ az, el, color }] }, light: { sun, second, sky, ground, ambient }, fog: { color, density }, exposure, bloom, wind: { dir, strength } }`. The sun's direction: the record's `OutdoorLight` has colour and illuminance; the direction is read from the level's sun entity when the record has one, else measured from the outdoor probe's brightest texel (the fallback the first design proposed as the only path). Calibration: the game's illuminance (lux) and EV to the site's intensities by one factor, `GAME_TO_SITE`, set once on Hoth so the before/after shots match in exposure, then held for every world (the PR says the factor and shows the two shots).

**The probes.** `scripts/bf2017-probe.mjs` packs a reflection volume's six faces into one `.hdr` cube cross (or six files) under the level pack; `levelScene.js` loads the outdoor one as `scene.environment` through PMREM (as `scene.js:279` does for the dome) and an indoor one per zone the site marks indoors (the hangar), swapping at the zone change `scene.js:1527` already makes. 128² is enough for reflections and ambient; nothing bigger is shipped.

**The far shadow.** The distant shadow cache (2048², 16-bit, the whole level's sun shadow) is sampled by the ground shader (`lib/three/groundLook.js`) and by the far instances' material beyond the live shadow map's extent, so the ridges shade the snow and the forest floor is dark under the redwoods at every distance. Its world bounds are measured against the heightmap once (task 3 of lane G says how) and written in the pack.

**Grading and tone.** `universe/post.js`'s surface pass takes `exposure` and `bloom` from the entry; the colour-grading LUT (`T_CC_<World>_<Weather>`, if its texture is a 32³ strip) goes through a LUT pass on high and ultra; brightness, contrast and saturation otherwise. The site's own look rules (`look.js`, `lookOf`) read the derived sky, so the house's fog and shade follow.

**Weather.** The site's `weather.js` states map to the level's weathers (clear → Sunny, dusk → Sunset, overcast → Cloudy, storm → Blizzard), crossfaded over 20 s as the site crossfades today; a world with one VE keeps it.

**Not yet.** The placed lights (the hangar's lamps, Theed's lanterns) wait on `bf2export lights`; the site's lamps stay.

### The planet skins (lane K)

`bodies.js` paints every planet by shader from a palette. For each world the game has a skin for, the look gains `skin: { color, normal?, clouds?, atmo?, rings? }` (site paths under `public/textures/galaxy/planets/`, mirrored by the asset host) and `buildBody` samples them: the skin's colour over the procedural ground by altitude (`uSkinMix` 1 from orbit, easing to the procedural relief and the scans within the atmosphere, so the close ground stays as good as it is), the normal map tilting the limb's light, the clouds texture as the cloud layer with its own drift, the atmosphere gradient's colour feeding `createAtmosphere`. Sizes: 1024 (mid), 2048 WebP (high), 4096 KTX2 (ultra). Mapped from the game's names: Endor's gas giant and forest moon, Naboo and its moon, Bespin, Kamino, Sullust, Hoth, Tatooine, Kashyyyk, Yavin's gas giant and Yavin 4, Geonosis with its rings, Scarif, Kessel, Felucia, the Death Star II, Ryloth, Fondor, Athulla, Pillio, Vardos. Sequel worlds excluded by the standing rule.

### The sabers (lane X)

**Stroke tables from the game's clips.** `scripts/bf2017-strokes.mjs <hero>` reads `web/anims.jsonl` and the hero's clips and writes `src/data/bf2017/strokes/<hero>.json`: for each strike (`A_<Hero>_AttackLoop_Strike1..6`, `_V2`, with its `_BackToIdle`), the duration, the contact window measured from the blade tip's speed on `Wep_Root` (the `contactWindow` `scripts/ual-bake.mjs` exports), the sweep's plane and direction (from the tip's path: the site's `DIRS`), the root travel (`distance`), and its place in the chain; the blocks by incoming direction (`Stand_Block_SwingLeft/Right_01..04`), the blocked reactions by strike (`LightAttack_Blocked_01..06`), staggers, dodges, the dash and jump attack as heavies, the defeat. `combatRules.js`'s `stanceOf` returns a stance built from the table for a 2017 hero (`stanceFromTable`, pure, tested) and the UAL stance for anyone else; `saber.js`'s `swing({ clip })` and `strokeFor` need no change. The duel (`lib/combat/duel.js`) reads its cadence from the table: the return's length is the punish window.

**The hold.** A 2017 figure's clips pose both hands on the hilt; `saber.js`'s guard and off-hand poses (`pose`, `poseLeft`) are skipped for `fig.rig === 'walrus'`, the hilt rides `Wep_Root` (phase 1), the fingers are the clip's. `ARMS` gains `Spine1`, `Spine2`, `Neck`.

**The blade.** The game's blade rod (`lightsaberlukerod_meshp_mesh`, 16 triangles) gives the blade's length and radius and the emitter's point on each hilt; the hilt's `_RGB` mask says which parts glow. A lit blade is a `PointLight` within 12 m on high and ultra (the `scene.js:409` lamp pattern), bloom calibrated once against `post.js`'s threshold; the trail from the stroke's frames as today. The clash and ignition sheets are lane F's.

**Scope.** Lane X writes the tables, the rules and the hold; the saber-forms lanes (B the forms, D the Force, E dismemberment) read the tables instead of mirroring clips. Phase 1 (the heroes on the game's skeleton, the hilts in the socket) is its base and runs first.

### Rules kept

- The pipeline design's rules (section 8), the asset host's (`HANDOFF-planet-flight.md`, lane I), the layers (`docs/health/RULES.md`): `src/lib/**` pure, worlds islands, files under 800 lines, tests beside, no key in the client, nothing raw served.
- The game first: a world on its level pack draws nothing built for what the game has.
- Compare on the shot before replacing; the before/after sheets in `docs/superpowers/evidence/bf2017-<lane>/`.
- One lane per PR; `origin/main` merged before opening and before merging; a conflict in `scene.js` or a site file keeps both sides.

### What this design does not do

- Does not export anything from the game: lights, effects and audio are the desktop exporter's, asked for in the hand-off.
- Does not touch the worlds the game lacks (nevarro, mandalore, sorgan, lothal, coruscant, dagobah's and mustafar's layouts).
- Does not change the figure resolution order, `actors.js`, `placer.js`, the combat rules' interfaces, or `assetBase.js`'s contract; lane S extends it.
- Does not ship a sequel-era level, planet or clip.

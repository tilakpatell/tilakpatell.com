# The sky above white: Hoth's HDR panorama on high and ultra, and the game's sun lobe

2026-10-12. The owner's `bf2-re` workflow (findings 02, three readers and three refuters) read the game's sky as `(panorama texel × LuminanceScale + sun lobe × SunColor × SunScale) × exposure`, with the panorama `T_Hoth_Sunny_02_C` a BC6H float (values to 8.7) and the sun a Henyey–Greenstein lobe with g = 1 − SunSize clamped to 1. The site's copy of the panorama was the bucket's UASTC, clamped to 0…1, and its sun a smoothstep disc dimmed by the model's air. The pictures and the numbers are in `bf2017-sky-2026-10-12/`.

**In one sentence:** the HDR copy the desktop's panoramas job made (RGBA16F KTX2, Zstandard) now rides on the picture row as `hdr`, three's KTX2Loader reads it on the WebGPU renderer (a half-float DataTexture with its twelve mips, `format: 'rgba16f'` on the dev door, nothing in `missing`), the sun is the game's lobe, and at the record's clamp of EV 15 the drawn sky is the same white it was, because the display clips in the exposure and the LUT, not in the texture.

## 4. What the numbers say

- **The WebGPU path reads the RGBA16F KTX2.** three's KTX2Loader takes vkFormat 97 with Zstandard through its raw path (no Basis transcoder): a `DataTexture` of `HalfFloatType`, `RGBAFormat`, its twelve levels in `mipmaps`, which the WebGPU backend uploads level by level. Nothing in `missing`, the format read back, the sky drawn.
- **The drawn sky is the same at the record's clamp.** Every patch is within a few display steps of before. The reason is in the chain, not the texture: the sunny panorama's horizon row (about 1.0) × the gain 2.52 is 2.5, the tonemap clamps to 1 and the LUT is white from 0.875, so the lower half of the sky is white either way; the sun's surround (3.56 × 2.52 = 9.0) and the lobe (8.63) are white either way; only the zenith (0.07 / 0.13 / 0.30 × 2.52 = 0.17 / 0.32 / 0.77) shows a colour, and the UASTC copy had those values already. The decision of 2026-10-12 (`hoth-at-the-records-clamp.md`, "What is left") names this: `35,000 nits × LuminanceScale` over the same k is brighter than the sunlit snow, which a real sky is not. The panorama's own values and the record's LuminanceScale want reading together before the HDR copy can show on the sunny weather.
- **Where the HDR copy shows.** The bloom and the lens flare read the scene before the tonemap, so the sun's surround now drives them at 9.0 rather than 2.5 (the `low` patch of sunlit snow under the sunny sun moved from 189 / 193 / 197 to 195 / 200 / 205, the sunset's from 236 / 150 / 125 to 215 / 145 / 126; the two runs are separate matches, so part of that is the frame's own motion). On the sunset weather the sun's quarter peaks at 1.57 × 3.70 = 5.8 rather than 3.7, which the display still clips. A weather whose horizon sits well under white at its exposure (Kamino, Bespin at dusk: the panoramas lane W made for them) is where the copy's range reaches the screen.
- **The sun lobe.** At `SunSize` 0.004 the lobe is flat at `SunScale` out to about 1° and under 1 % by 5°; the test pins the shape and the peak (`sky.test.js`). On the sunny weather it is white against a white surround at EV 15; on the sunset weather it is the brightest thing in the frame, as in the game.

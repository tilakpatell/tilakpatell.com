- PR 2.4, Task 14: the far layer. `universe/farStars.js`'s `createFarStars(parent, { places, skyFar, slots = [], discs = 0 })` keeps its one `Points` and its one draw, and adds `setSlot(i, { centre, at, color, r, count, key? })`, `showSlots(list)`, `hideSlots(list)`, `fadeSlot(i, seconds)`, the `discs` writer (`begin()`, `push(x, y, z, r, cr, cg, cb, lx, ly, lz, alpha, id?)`, `end()`) and `pops()`. `slots` is each slot's size in specks (at most 200 slots: a uniform each); the vertices run places, then slots, then discs, and every vertex is drawn (the hidden are put out of the frame in the vertex shader). One draw blends in its vertices' order, so the discs go last: a dark rock hides the places and the specks behind it. The plan had the discs before the slots, which let a speck show through the disc in front of it. The `Points` sit at the camera in the parent's space and every position is written from the camera, so the hand-made places land where they did and a disc 10^9 out is exact. A slot's `at` is from its `centre` and rides in `position`; its `color` is a hex colour's sRGB bytes; a disc's colour is the working space's, as a `THREE.Color`'s, and `(0, 0, 0)` for its light means it shines. The far stars' numbers (`REAL_REACHES`, `REAL_LEAST`, `FADE`, `BRIGHT_AT`) now come from `expanse/scene/levels.js`, and the shaders take theirs from the same. The universe takes them through the Expanse's face, `expanse/shared/levels.js` (a re-export of all of `levels.js`, its names pinned by `shared.test.js`), so it doesn't reach inside another world; anything outside the Expanse that wants `levelWeights`, `discPx`, `FAR` or `FADE` (lane 4a's things, if they live in the universe) takes them from there too.
- The discs writer keeps the nearest it has room for and drops the farthest (the plan's test said the nearest dropped, which would drop the biggest and pop). `pops()` counts nothing till it's first asked; after that each asking reads what came in at over half in one frame since the last: a disc by the `id` it was pushed with (Task 15 should pass each body's id, or its discs go uncounted) and a slot shown out of nothing. `setSlot`'s `key` names what a slot holds (the sector's key): a slot shown out of nothing in the frame a slot drawn the frame before with the same key is hidden (or set again) is a swap, the same stars moved, not a pop. Any other show out of nothing is counted, and a slot with no key always is. Task 20 should pass each sector's key, so its one-call swap reads clean where it moves the same sectors and counts the sectors new to the sky.
- Task 14's review: the far layer writes depth. It tests none (`AlwaysDepth`), so nothing of it hides behind the sky or the far plane. A disc writes its own depth where it covers half a pixel or more, so a mesh farther off, drawn after it in the opaque list, fails against it; before, a ball 30,000 off drew over a dark disc 10,000 off. A star or speck writes the far plane's depth (1, what the depth is cleared to), not that of where it's drawn: it sits at `skyFar` (24,000), inside the 45,000 far plane, and that depth would cut a hole round it in any mesh in front of it. A faint disc, or a disc's rim, writes the far plane's too, so a mesh behind it shows through, not a hole of sky. The black hole's lens in `post.js` reads the scene's depth, so a disc in front of the hole is drawn unbent, as a mesh there is.
- PR 2.4, Task 15: the discs. `expanse/scene/discs.js` has `pickDiscs(xyzr, n, cam, { far = FAR, max, out })` (of `n` candidates, four numbers each, the indices within `far + r` of `cam`, the nearest `max` kept, a tie to the lower index, far to near) and `createDiscs(far, { budget, built })` → `{ feed(sectors, cam, t), setBudget(budget) }`. `far` is the far layer; `feed` takes the loaded sectors through `addBodies` (a `null` among them is skipped), the camera in the far layer's parent's space (the map's) and the shared clock in seconds (`spaceTime`), and pushes into the far layer's discs writer, with its id, each system's star (its size, shining in its colour), each planet (at `orbitAt`, lit from its star, in its `TYPE_LOOK`'s swatch), each moon (at `moonAt`, in its `MOON_LOOK`'s swatch) and each wonder but the nebulae (its ball's size and colour, shining as the ball does). It takes no camera: the far layer has it from its own `update`, and the writer measures from there. `sectors.js` now exports `MOON_LOOK` (`icy: 'hoth', rocky: 'moon-grey'`, the plan's looks for near.js's moons), `lookColour(id)` (a look's swatch), `WONDER` and `WONDER_SPECK` (0.4, a wonder's speck's share of its size, which says where its ball is real), so near.js builds what the discs show. `expanse/scene/fixtures/hand.js` is `sectors.test.js`' `HAND` with each orbit where its planet is and a ringed gas giant with two moons, for `near.test.js` too.
- How a disc gives way: every body of a system, star, planets and moons, fades out together by the camera's distance from the star against `system.reach`, over its last fifth (`levelWeights(dStar, { reach }).full`), as near.js builds whole systems by their reach (the spec's table: within a system's reach, everything is built). The plan had a planet hand over at 3 × its radius; an outermost planet with no moons reaches less than that past its orbit, so its disc would have gone with nothing built in its place. Moons are discs too, so they don't pop in when their system is built. Given `built` (near's `built()`), a system that isn't built keeps its discs whole however near, so a system left unbuilt by `systemsBuilt` never goes missing. `built()` gives an array or a set of the built systems' ids, each given up by its own reach, or a map of each id to the reach near.js builds it within, given up over the last fifth of that. A wonder gives way to its ball where the ball is real (`realAt({ r: size × WONDER_SPECK })`, as `sectors.js` shows it now).
- The budget: `feed` keeps the nearest `budget.discs` and fades its edge: past the budget, the far plane comes in to the nearest left out, so a disc comes and goes at nothing as the camera moves (the test sweeps a 200-star field with a budget of 20 at 15 units a frame: no alpha moves more than 0.05 a frame; with a hard edge, 1). Only what shows counts: a system given up to what's built, or a wonder to its ball, takes none of the budget.
- Task 15's review: the edge follows the camera at once (the nearest left out moves no faster than the camera does), and whatever else moves it, a new budget either way or a system given up or come back, it eases: its distance's inverse moves as fast as the biggest disc it fades allows at 4 px a frame (on a 1,080 px screen at the chase's 34°, the spec's pop), less what the camera's own move spends. While it eases in, it draws more than the budget, the farthest fading. Before, a system within its reach took the budget's nearest places and drew nothing there, a raised budget's edge grew 5% a frame (a third of a disc's alpha a frame) and a cut took at once. In the 3D 3 × 3 × 3 round `E:1,0,0`, at low: at 0.3 of the reach of `E:0,-1,-1:63` (25 bodies), a budget of 35 drew 10, none whole, and now draws 35, 22 whole; 10,000 above a system, step 3 to step 0 moved a disc 13.9 px in a frame and settled in 7 frames, and now 3.99 px and 21 frames; step 0 to step 3 took 260 discs at once, one 42 px, and now 3.98 px and 21 frames (358 discs at most on the way down to 140). `orbits.js`'s `orbitAt(planet, t, out?)` writes into `out`, and `moonAt(moon, planet, t, out?, planetAt?)` takes where the planet is, so `feed` works each planet out once and makes no array for a body: 436 KiB a feed to 24 (what's left is the sort's working copy and numbers the engine boxes at its calls), and 0.62 to 0.64 ms to 0.53 to 0.55 at low. `orbitAt` takes its axis's length by the square root of its squares (`Math.hypot` boxes what it's given), so 262 of 41,192 positions, planets and moons, moved in their last bit, 6 × 10⁻¹¹ at most. The numbers are in the evidence README.

## Left

It was run at the gate, on both branches, but the browser here draws in software and a frame can stall for minutes, so those numbers (in the lane's evidence README) can't show 2%. - The `landing-middleearth` pose isn't the same from run to run, on `main` as on the lane: it comes down where Middle-earth has turned to when the landing starts, and that goes by how long the page took to load. Held at one turn, both branches land on the same spot. If the pose is to be compared pixel for pixel, it wants the planet's turn held (or `?spot=`).
- `universe/battleSceneNodes.js`, the node renderer's twin, still sets its halves' cut in `update()`. The node renderer projects a `ClippingGroup`'s planes as it lists the scene, before any object's `onBeforeRender`, so the GLSL fix can't be copied there: it would be a frame late. Nothing draws the twin yet. When something does, set its planes before the render, for instance from the relative draw.
- Task 20 needs an edge fade for the beyond rings (`levels.js`'s `beyondFade`, the fourth row of its table) before its boundary pop check means anything. The far layer can't fade a ring at its edge yet: a speck shows to the ring's last sector. At a centre's swap the sectors newly in a ring come in at once, and `pops()` can't tell: keyed the same from centre to centre, a ring's swap reads as clean; keyed by its centre, the whole ring reads as a pop. Give the slots an edge (a second uniform array beside `uSlot`, whose four numbers are a slot's centre and how much shows), fade the specks to it in the vertex shader, and key a ring the same from centre to centre; then Task 22's `pops().px ≤ 4` at the boundary step speaks for the rings too.
- One thing the far layer's one draw can't do: a place's star that is nearer than a disc in line with it is covered by the disc. The places are drawn first, on the sky, and a disc covers whatever was drawn before it. This is rare (the star has to be nearer than the disc and in line with it) and brief, and it's left as it is. If it's ever seen, pass each place's true distance through and let its core write that depth, with the discs tested `LessEqual`. That costs the discs' smoothed rims where one disc crosses another.
- A disc in front of a mesh covers it only from half its cover up (`levels.js`'s disc weight over 0.5). Below that the mesh draws over it whole, and at 0.5 that part of the mesh turns to the disc over the sky at once. Discs fade in at the far plane and fade out where the built body takes over, and the built body sits in front of its own disc, so a disc mid-fade in front of another mesh is rare. Task 17's browser checks should still look for a disc in front of a mesh, fading and whole.
- The landmarks (`landmarks.js`: the Maw, the nebulae, the big stars) are drawn after the far layer with no depth test, so a landmark's impostor covers any disc in front of it. Lane 1 replaces the Veil's and the Cradle's. Once Task 15 feeds the discs, whatever stays an impostor should be drawn before the far layer, or test against the discs' depth.
- A disc is a point sprite. The chip caps a sprite's size (1,024 px on many, less on some phones), and a sprite whose middle leaves the frame goes all at once. A planet of radius 420 at its reach (three radii) is about 1,180 px across on a 1,080 px screen, so near its reach a big disc can be capped, or vanish at the frame's edge, as the built body takes over. Tasks 15 and 16 should hand over before a disc passes the cap (`gl.ALIASED_POINT_SIZE_RANGE`), or start the built body further out, and Task 17's browser checks should look for it.
- Task 16: build a system before the camera can come near any of its bodies, and wire `built`. With the hand-over by the system's reach, the camera can sit just outside a system's reach at an outermost moonless planet's surface, and that planet's disc is drawn whole: flying in along every planet's line to the first 40 systems of `E:1,0` (legacy and 3D alike), the biggest disc drawn at over half is 3,448 px across at 1,080 px (planet `E:1,0:4:5`, radius 44, the camera 2,078 from its star, whose reach is 2,077), far past any chip's point-size cap (1,024 on many, 511 under Metal). A disc stays under 512 px at 1,080 px from about seven of its radii out (fourteen at a 2,160 px buffer), so building a system while the camera is within `max(reach, |body − star| + 7 × r)` of its star, over its planets and moons, keeps every unbuilt disc under the cap. Then `built()` should give a map of each built system's id to the reach it's built within, so its discs give way over the last fifth of that; given ids alone, a built system's discs give way by its own `reach`, and between that and the build reach they stay whole under its meshes, hidden by depth but taking budget.

# The soldier's walk: the game's blend on the pack's clips

2026-10-12. The owner said the soldier "is not walking right". The reverse-engineering study (`bf2-re/findings/24-animation.md`, `12-soldier-locomotion.md`) says why: the game's locomotion is a phase-locked weighted blend (the neighbouring direction clips by the velocity's angle in the aim's frame, walk/run/sprint by speed, every transition on the stride phase through `PredictableBlend` and `TransitionPolicyNode`), and the site played one whole clip at a time, cross-fading 0.15 s at every 45° and speed-band boundary at unrelated phases, so the feet popped and slid at each crossing. The pictures and the numbers are in `bf2017-walk-2026-10-12/`.

**In one sentence:** the figures now run every loco clip the pack has on three's mixer at once, weighted by the velocity's angle and the body's speed, all slaved to one stride phase measured on the body, so a turn or a speed change moves weights and never cuts a clip; and the drawn body no longer stutters, because the figures were reading the sim's view from before the frame's steps.

## 2. The measure

`node scripts/bf2017-walk-check.mjs --tag <before|after>` with `npx vite --port 5577 --strictPort --host 127.0.0.1` up: headless Edge (`msedge.exe`, `--enable-unsafe-webgpu --use-angle=d3d11 --ignore-gpu-blocklist --enable-webgl`, 1920 × 1080, `tp-quality` ultra), `/battlefront/hoth/galacticAssault`, the level loaded, `deploy d-orig-assault`, `advance 10`, a warm-up on the move, then **W**, **W + D**, **S** and nothing, two seconds each, a shot halfway through each (the game's own third-person camera, and a second one with the camera held off the body's right side so the feet show). Every drawn frame records the body's place and the world positions of the Walrus rig's `LeftToeBase`, `RightToeBase`, `LeftFoot` and `RightFoot`, taken as the body is first drawn that frame (its first skinned mesh's `onAfterRender`), after the world's step.

Foot slide: the ground a foot should hold is the body's steady course (the window's mean velocity in the world's own time, since the runtime hands a world at most 50 ms a frame and the headless frames here run 35–100 ms); each foot is read in the body's frame and carried by that course; the planted foot of a frame is the one moving less over the ground (the swinging one goes at about twice the body's speed); a stance is a run of frames with the same foot planted, and only its middle three fifths count (the first and last fifth are the landing and the push-off, where a clip's own feet move). The planted foot's speed there is the slide; its move over 5 cm in one frame is a pop. The toe base is the headline (the ankle rolls over the ball of the foot through a stance and reads higher in both records).

Both records are on the same machine within minutes, the same route and keys; `before` is `origin/main` (3736c187b), `after` this branch. The headless frame rate at ultra is 10–25 a second, so the sim and the clips advance by the runtime's 50 ms clamp a frame rather than the clock; the slide is measured in that time.

Frames a window: before 33 / 57 / 63 / 101, after 53 / 60 / 62 / 66; planted mid-stance frames 14–32 a window; the body's course 3.7–3.9 m/s on the move. The ankle reads the same way (before W + D 3.03 m/s and 28 pops, after 1.10 and 9; before S 0.91 and 6, after 0.37 and 0).

- **Ahead** is the same before and after, as it should be: one clip (the run, 3.94 m/s) at 0.96 of its pace under a 3.8 m/s body either way. The 0.4–0.5 m/s mean is the game's own clip: its planted foot creeps through the stance (the authored run is not perfectly planted, and `FootPlantFeature` pinned it in the game; the site has no foot IK yet).
- **Ahead-right** was the fault: at 45° the old code's eighth rounded to the forward walk, pushed to the band's top (1.8×) under a body moving diagonally, so the planted foot slid sideways at 3 m/s and every stance popped. Now it is half the walk and half the right strafe, the same phase, the phase running at the body's speed over the two clips' travel summed as vectors (0.71 of either, so 1.8× again at full speed): the slide is 0.9 m/s and the pops are the blend's reach, not cuts. The rest of that slide is the pack's: the game blends Fwd with Left1/Right1 at 45° (`C_HM_Rifle_Walk_Right2`, `Run_Right2` and their Left twins are the 90° clips' neighbours) and the site's packs carry only the 90° strafes, so a diagonal at full speed has nothing paced for it.
- **Back** had the walk's backward clip (1.62 m/s) pushed to 1.8× under 3.0 m/s; now the rifle stance pack's `run.back` (`C_HM_Rifle_Run_Bwd_01`, 3.18 m/s) plays at 0.96 and the feet hold.
- **Stop**: the fade into the idle still moves the feet about 0.6 m over the 0.15 s (both records; an earlier `after` run whose window caught it counted 4 pops of up to 0.14 m), because no stop clip is wired (section 5).

The first `after` run, before the module.js fix, measured the drawn body moving 0.000 m one frame and 0.378 m the next at a steady speed; after it, 0.19 m a 50 ms step, every frame.

## 5. What the pack lacks for the rest

The humanoid pack (`clips-humanoid.glb`) has the walk (2 strides, 1.91 m/s), run (4, 3.94), sprint (4, 5.90), the three walking strafes (`walk.back` 1.62, `walk.left` 2.01, `walk.right` 1.91), the crouch run (2.54) and two turn-in-place loops (`turn.left`/`turn.right`: `L_HM_Pistol_Stand_TurnLeftSlow90_01`, 1.87 s, no root). The rifle stance pack (`clips-stance-t.glb`, the bucket's) adds the running strafes (`run.back` 3.18 m/s, 2 strides; `run.left`/`run.right` 3.71, 3 strides), `turn.back` (`T_HM_Rifle_Stand_Turn180Left`, 1.23 s) and one stop (`stop`: `A_HM_Rifle_Run_FwdToStand_01`, 1.0 s). Not packed, and in the drop: the 45° strafes (`C_HM_Rifle_Walk_Left2/Right2`, `Run_Left2/Right2`), the starts (`A_HM_Rifle_StandToSprintFwd_01`), the walk's stop (`Walk_FwdToStand_01`), the crouch's stop, the direction changes (`DirChange_Left1/2`, `DirChange_Run_Right1/2`) and the fast turns (`Stand_TurnInPlace_Left_Fast/Slow_02`).

So, in order: the 45° clips into the packs (`scripts/bf2017-clips.mjs`'s map in `walrusClips.js` and `walrusSets/stance.js`), which `RINGS` takes as four more slots; the stop clip wired to start on the stride phase it was authored at (its own foot phase measured as `stride.js` does, played once from `timeAt` when the speed drops, the idle after it); the starts likewise; the turn-in-place loops need the body's yaw to lag the aim (the game steers the body toward the aim through the clip at 0.2, `SteerAnimatableFactor`), which `facing.js` does not do yet; and foot planting (`FootPlantFeature`), which is what takes the last 0.4 m/s off the ahead row.

Checks: `npm run lint` clean; `figures`, `locomotion`, `stride`, `facing`, `held`, `ragdolls` and the Battlefront module tests pass (`npx vitest run src/components/battlefront`); `node scripts/health.mjs --check --skip build` within budget. The one failure in that run, `scripts/bf2017-clips.test.mjs`'s path separators, is the known Windows-only one (CI is the gate).

# The guns: how a blaster's textures reach the screen, and why the E-11 drew grey

2026-10-12. The owner asked how the gun textures work, and whether open-source models would do better; in the site's Hoth the soldier's E-11 drew as flat grey plastic where the game's is dark metal with worn edges (the owner's capture `bf2-re/data/drive-12.png`). The pictures are in `bf2017-guns-2026-10-12/`.

**Corrected the same day (§5):** sections 1–3 reasoned from the maps' statistics and read the `_Mask`'s green as the metal; the game's compiled weapon shader, disassembled that evening, says the metal is the NAM's blue after all, and the `_Mask` only gates the weathering. The channel change of §3 (PR #946) was reverted, and the cuts re-imported on the game's recipe. The sections are kept as they were written, for the record of how the wrong reading was reached.

**In one sentence (as first written):** the export's recipe for a weapon's occlusion-roughness-metal map read the metal from the wrong channel (the NAM's blue, which on a Walrus weapon is the edge-wear mask, nearly black), so every blaster came in with metalness about 0 and lit as matte dielectric plastic; the metal mask lives in the `_Mask` map's green, and the import now builds the ORM from it.

| the game's material | `BF2_Extract/upload_surfaces/materials.jsonl`, mesh `gameplay/equipment/rifles/e11/e11_3p_mesh` | shader `Shaders/Presets/Walrus/SS_WeaponsPreset` (and `_Alpha` for the sight parts), binding `_BaseColor` = `T_E11_CS`, `_Normal` = `T_E11_NAM`, `_Mask` = `T_E11_RGB`; the export's GLB keeps these in each material's `extras` |

The game packs its maps (`bf2-re/data/ebx/export-GUIDE.md` §7): `_cs` is colour with smoothness in alpha; `_nam` is the normal's x and y in red and green with two masks in blue and alpha; `_rgb` is a mask map. The uploader derives, per model, a `__normal` (z rebuilt) and an `__orm_<hash>` for glTF by a recipe written into `web/models.jsonl`. The E-11's:

```
orm:T_E11_NAM : ao = T_E11_NAM|a ; rough = T_E11_CS|a ; metal = T_E11_NAM|b
```

## 2. The E-11's GLB, and the maps' numbers

`public/models/galaxy/surface/e11.lod1.glb` before the fix (93,108 bytes, one primitive, material `M_E11`, `baseColorTexture` 0, `normalTexture` 1, `metallicRoughnessTexture` = `occlusionTexture` 2; no factors, so metal 1 × map, roughness 1 × map):

| image | size | channel means (0–255) | reads as |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 `t_e11_cs` | 512², RGB | 62 / 62 / 61 | a dark, near-grey colour: right for an E-11 |
| 1 `t_e11_nam__normal` | 256², RGBA | 128 / 129 / 243 | a sound normal map, z rebuilt |
| 2 `t_e11_nam__orm` | 256², RGBA | **251 / 162 / 9** | occlusion none, roughness 0.63, **metal 0.03** |

The game's own maps for the E-11 (`web/textures/gameplay/equipment/rifles/e11/`, all 2048² but the RGB at 1024²):

| map | channel means | what the channel is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `t_e11_cs` | 62 / 62 / 61 / **94** | colour; alpha = smoothness (0.37, so roughness 0.63) |
| `t_e11_nam` | 128 / 128 / **6** / **255** | normal x, y; blue = an edge-wear mask (thin bright strokes on edges and scratches, black elsewhere); alpha = 255 everywhere |
| `t_e11_rgb` | 0 / **110** / 0 / 254 | red and blue empty; **green = the metal mask**: white on the casing, rails and magazine, black on the grip, the sight glass and the rubber |

The same holds for all eight class weapons (NAM blue means 6–63, RGB green means 110–179, RGB red and blue 0) and for the 483 materials in the drop on the weapons preset, the hero hilts among them. So the uploader's `metal = NAM|b` put the wear mask where glTF wants the metal: metalness about 0 over the whole gun, and a dark dielectric with roughness 0.63 under Hoth's sky is exactly flat grey plastic. Nothing else was wrong: the colour space is sRGB, the normal map is good, the WebP loads (no "a map did not load" line names a weapon; the only such lines on Hoth are a rock-wall normal the bucket lacks), and the scene has its environment, which is what the metal now reflects.

Follow-ups, not done here:

- The NAM's blue is the game's edge-wear mask: in the game it lightens and bares the metal on edges. The import does not draw it; folding it into the colour (lighter) and the roughness (lower) where it is bright would give the worn edges the capture shows.
- The hero hilts (`hiltluke`, `hiltvader`, …) are on the same preset with a `_Mask`, imported `--native` (the bucket's own derived KTX2, with the same `metal = NAM|b`). They would need a non-native re-import, or the bucket's derived KTX2 remade, to get their metal.
- The `_Alpha` preset parts (sight glass, emitters) are drawn opaque, as the weapons research noted.

## 4. Open-source models instead?

No. The pipeline already brings the game's own licensed weapons and soldiers in at the game's geometry (DICE's LOD chain, 2,012 triangles for the E-11's light cut, 4,031 plain) with the game's own colour, normal and packed maps, in the game's frame with the game's muzzle bones, rigged to the game's skeleton for the game's clips; what was wrong was one channel in one derived map, which a one-function fix and a re-import mended. An open-source replacement (a Sketchfab CC E-11, Quaternius' low-poly guns, a KitBash3D set) would give a mesh with its own glTF-ready PBR maps and no packing to decode, which is the one thing that broke here; it would not give the exact film prop at the game's proportions, the game's wear and smoothness, the weapon skeleton (`Wep_Root`, `Wep_Muzzle`, `Wep_Trigger`) the held clips and the muzzle flash key off, the per-class set the records name, nor, for the soldiers, the Walrus rig with its faces, cloth and sockets that every clip, ragdoll and hurtbox in the site assumes. A generic soldier would mean re-rigging or retargeting every clip and re-fitting the hurtboxes and sockets; a generic gun would mean re-deriving the muzzle and grip for each. Recommendation: keep the game's models, keep decoding the game's packing (the shader name in each material's extras says how), and spend the open-source route only on things the drop lacks.

## 5. Corrected: the shader is the judge, and the metal was the NAM's blue all along

The same evening the owner's reverse-engineering study disassembled the game's compiled Walrus weapon preset shaders (`bf2-re/findings/18-textures.md`, the section "The E-11's metal: the shader is the judge"; the GBuffer compile `bf2-re/data/shaders/dis/rvm/weapons-gbuf-31465021.asm`, the forward compile `weapons-ps-big.asm`). What the shader does with the three maps:

```
sample r3.xyz ← t2 (_Mask RGB)                    ; R, G, B
mul_sat r2.z, r3.z, 100                           ; B → a hard gate on the weathering
add_sat r3.z, r3.y, r3.y                          ; sat(2·G) scales the weathering amount
… r1.w = the wear/weather amount (the grunge, the VE's thresholds, up-facing, the _Mask's gates)
sample r5.xyzw ← t3 (_Normal NAM)
add r2.w, -r1.w, 1 ; mul r2.w, r5.z, r2.w         ; metal = NAM.b × (1 − wear)
sample r13.xyzw ← t4 (_BaseColor CS)
add r3.z, -r13.w, 0.3 ; mad r3.z, r1.w, r3.z, r13.w ; smoothness = lerp(CS.a, 0.3, wear)
mov o2.xz, (0, 0.5)                               ; reflectance 0.5 → F0 = 0.16 × 0.5² = 0.04
mov o2.y, r2.w                                    ; the GBuffer's metal
```

So on a Walrus weapon the metal **is** the NAM's blue: a near-black channel, bright only on the worn-through edges, because the E-11 in the game is a painted dielectric (the study's samples at the mesh's vertices: metalness about 0.07 at the barrel, 0.09 at the stock, 0.02 at the grip; bare metal, with the albedo as F0, on the 1–8 % of texels where the edge wear is bright). The `_Mask`'s green never reaches the metal or the smoothness: it scales the amount of the weathering overlay (Hoth's snow, Jakku's dust), its red tints that overlay and its blue shuts it out. Section 2's "edge-wear mask" and the game's metal mask are the same channel. The export's recipe (`ao = NAM|a; rough = 255 − CS|a; metal = NAM|b`) was the game's, and §3's change made the casing 45–65 % metallic with a dark albedo as its F0: brighter under the site's flat lighting, but not the game's look. The flat-grey difference the owner saw is one of specular lighting and exposure (the game's probes carry the real sky panorama at EV 10–15), not of channels.

What changed back, and what is new:

- `scripts/lib/bf2017-textures.mjs`: `weaponRecipes` is gone; the uploader's ORM recipe stands for a weapon. `weaponMaterial(shader)` names a Walrus weapon preset (`SS_WeaponsPreset`, `_Alpha`, `_Transparancy`, `_TransparentDepth`) as `{ reflectance: 0.5, occlusion: false }`, and `specularFactorOf(reflectance)` turns Frostbite's `F0 = 0.16·r²` into glTF's `KHR_materials_specular` factor over the default dielectric F0 of 0.04: `4·r²`, clamped to 1. A weapon's 0.5 is exactly 1, glTF's default, so the import writes no extension for it and three lights the cut as a `MeshStandardMaterial` with F0 0.04 (the study's "specularFactor 0.5" would have halved F0 to 0.02: the factor scales 0.04, it is not the reflectance). The `rebuild` mark and `resolveImage`'s source-first path from #946 stay, generic and tested, for a recipe of the site's own; no recipe carries it now.
- `scripts/bf2017-import.mjs` `readLod`: every map goes through the manifest's own recipes again; a Walrus weapon material drops its occlusion slot (the ORM's red is the NAM's alpha, 255 everywhere; the game's material has no occlusion) and would take a specular extension only where the factor is under 1.
- The eight light cuts re-imported from the owner's export the same way as §3 (`--keep-origin --root C:/Users/tilak/Downloads/BF2_Extract`): the log reads `t_e11_nam__orm_4a0d6485.png ← t_e11_nam__orm_4a0d6485.png` (the uploader's own derived PNG). The E-11's ORM means **251 / 162 / 8.6** (metal 0.034 over the 256² map, the whole-map mean, as before #946: §2's 251 / 162 / 9), its material `metallicRoughnessTexture` 2 and no `occlusionTexture`. Sizes: e11 81,304 → 81,240 bytes, a280 88,260 → 88,676, dh17 251,328 → 251,992, dlt19 89,816 → 90,268, dlt19x 116,244 → 117,060, dlt20a 87,064 → 86,328, rk3 69,536 → 70,000, rt97c 95,688 → 95,824. Catalogue rows and credits unchanged.
- Tests: `bf2017-textures.test.mjs` pins the preset list, the reflectance-to-factor conversion and the uploader's recipe unmarked; `bf2017-import.test.mjs` pins the fixture hilt (on `SS_WeaponsPreset`) losing its occlusion slot and gaining no specular extension; `held.glb.test.js` reads every committed cut: an ORM on each mapped material, metallic factor 1 (× the map), no occlusion, no specular extension, and the material three's loader makes (`ior` 1.5 if physical, `specularIntensity` 1) at F0 0.04.

Checks: `npm run lint` clean; the three suites pass; `node scripts/health.mjs --check --skip build` within budget.

The picture (`hoth-e11-corrected.png`, `hoth-corrected.png`; headless Edge on WebGPU, `--use-angle=d3d11`, ultra, the dev server, deploy as `d-orig-assault`, advance 30 s, the camera beside the player's gun as before): the E-11 reads as a dark painted blaster, grey-black over the body with lighter worn edges along the rails, the receiver cover and the sight, the scope darker still; not chrome, not the flat grey of `hoth-e11-before.png`. The `_Mask`'s green is free to be what it is in the game: the site's own snow-coverage mask on Hoth, if a later change draws the weathering.

# The guns: how a blaster's textures reach the screen, and why the E-11 drew grey

2026-10-12. The owner asked how the gun textures work, and whether open-source models would do better; in the site's Hoth the soldier's E-11 drew as flat grey plastic where the game's is dark metal with worn edges (the owner's capture `bf2-re/data/drive-12.png`). The pictures are in `bf2017-guns-2026-10-12/`.

**In one sentence:** the export's recipe for a weapon's occlusion-roughness-metal map read the metal from the wrong channel (the NAM's blue, which on a Walrus weapon is the edge-wear mask, nearly black), so every blaster came in with metalness about 0 and lit as matte dielectric plastic; the metal mask lives in the `_Mask` map's green, and the import now builds the ORM from it.

## 1. From the export to the screen

| step | where | what |
| --- | --- | --- |
| the class's weapon | `src/data/bf2017/classes.json` (`weapon: "e11"`), `src/data/bf2017/weapons.json` (its blueprint) | eight ids on Hoth: a280, dh17, dlt19, dlt19x, dlt20a, e11, rk3, rt97c |
| the held row | `src/data/bf2017/held.json`, built by `scripts/bf2017-held.mjs` | the 3P mesh name, the muzzle in the weapon frame, the flash offset, the stance |
| the file | `src/components/battlefront/figures/held.js`: `weaponUrl(id)` → `/models/galaxy/surface/<id>.lod1.glb` | the light cut in `public/`, committed (69–245 KB each); the plain cuts are git-ignored and not in `src/data/assets-manifest.json`, so nothing draws them |
| the loader | `src/lib/three/gltfCache.js` → `lib/three/gltf.js` (three's GLTFLoader with meshopt; KTX2 only where a file needs it) | the held cuts carry WebP (`EXT_texture_webp`), not KTX2, so no transcoder is involved |
| the material | three's `MeshStandardMaterial` from the glTF: `map` (sRGB), `normalMap`, `roughnessMap` + `metalnessMap` + `aoMap` on one ORM texture, `metalness` 1, `roughness` 1 (the glTF defaults, the maps scaling them) | read in the page: every held gun has `map`, `roughnessMap`, `metalnessMap`, colour space `srgb`, and `scene.environment` is set (the game-lit probe, `galaxy/surface/gameLit.js`) |
| the import | `scripts/bf2017-import.mjs` (`readLod` → `scripts/lib/bf2017-textures.mjs` `resolveImage`) run with `--keep-origin` from the export at `C:\Users\tilak\Downloads\BF2_Extract` (`web/models.jsonl`, `web/models/…`, `web/textures/…`) | each GLB image the export names is found as the game's PNG, or the uploader's derived PNG, or built from the manifest's `derived` recipe, then resized and written as WebP (colour 512, maps 256 in the light cut) |
| the game's material | `BF2_Extract/upload_surfaces/materials.jsonl`, mesh `gameplay/equipment/rifles/e11/e11_3p_mesh` | shader `Shaders/Presets/Walrus/SS_WeaponsPreset` (and `_Alpha` for the sight parts), binding `_BaseColor` = `T_E11_CS`, `_Normal` = `T_E11_NAM`, `_Mask` = `T_E11_RGB`; the export's GLB keeps these in each material's `extras` |

The game packs its maps (`bf2-re/data/ebx/export-GUIDE.md` §7): `_cs` is colour with smoothness in alpha; `_nam` is the normal's x and y in red and green with two masks in blue and alpha; `_rgb` is a mask map. The uploader derives, per model, a `__normal` (z rebuilt) and an `__orm_<hash>` for glTF by a recipe written into `web/models.jsonl`. The E-11's:

```
orm:T_E11_NAM : ao = T_E11_NAM|a ; rough = T_E11_CS|a ; metal = T_E11_NAM|b
```

## 2. The E-11's GLB, and the maps' numbers

`public/models/galaxy/surface/e11.lod1.glb` before the fix (93,108 bytes, one primitive, material `M_E11`, `baseColorTexture` 0, `normalTexture` 1, `metallicRoughnessTexture` = `occlusionTexture` 2; no factors, so metal 1 × map, roughness 1 × map):

| image | size | channel means (0–255) | reads as |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 `t_e11_cs` | 512², RGB | 62 / 62 / 61 | a dark, near-grey colour: right for an E-11 |
| 1 `t_e11_nam__normal` | 256², RGBA | 128 / 129 / 243 | a sound normal map, z rebuilt |
| 2 `t_e11_nam__orm` | 256², RGBA | **251 / 162 / 9** | occlusion none, roughness 0.63, **metal 0.03** |

The game's own maps for the E-11 (`web/textures/gameplay/equipment/rifles/e11/`, all 2048² but the RGB at 1024²):

| map | channel means | what the channel is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `t_e11_cs` | 62 / 62 / 61 / **94** | colour; alpha = smoothness (0.37, so roughness 0.63) |
| `t_e11_nam` | 128 / 128 / **6** / **255** | normal x, y; blue = an edge-wear mask (thin bright strokes on edges and scratches, black elsewhere); alpha = 255 everywhere |
| `t_e11_rgb` | 0 / **110** / 0 / 254 | red and blue empty; **green = the metal mask**: white on the casing, rails and magazine, black on the grip, the sight glass and the rubber |

The same holds for all eight class weapons (NAM blue means 6–63, RGB green means 110–179, RGB red and blue 0) and for the 483 materials in the drop on the weapons preset, the hero hilts among them. So the uploader's `metal = NAM|b` put the wear mask where glTF wants the metal: metalness about 0 over the whole gun, and a dark dielectric with roughness 0.63 under Hoth's sky is exactly flat grey plastic. Nothing else was wrong: the colour space is sRGB, the normal map is good, the WebP loads (no "a map did not load" line names a weapon; the only such lines on Hoth are a rock-wall normal the bucket lacks), and the scene has its environment, which is what the metal now reflects.

## 3. What changed

- `scripts/lib/bf2017-textures.mjs`
  - `weaponRecipes(derived, materials)`: for a material on a Walrus weapons preset (`SS_WeaponsPreset`, `_Alpha`, `_TransparentDepth`) that binds a `_Mask`, adds after the uploader's ORM line a mended one with `metal = <_Mask>|g`, marked `rebuild`. Read from the export GLB's own `extras.shader` and `extras.textures`, so it needs no table and leaves every other shader alone.
  - `parseDerived` reads the `rebuild` mark; `resolveImage` builds a mended recipe from the game's PNGs ahead of the uploader's own derived PNG, and falls back to the uploader's (then its recipe, then the KTX2) when the mask is not on disk; it also recognises a derived map named as `.png` (the export's GLBs) as well as `.ktx2` (the bucket's).
- `scripts/bf2017-import.mjs`: `readLod` passes the mended recipes to every map lookup.
- Tests beside the file (`bf2017-textures.test.mjs`): the recipe mended, idempotent, left alone for another shader or an unbound NAM; the ORM rebuilt from sources before the uploader's PNG, as KTX2 and as PNG; the fallback when the mask is missing (the import test's hilt fixture has no `_RGB` on disk and keeps its old maps).
- The eight light cuts re-imported from the owner's export (`node scripts/bf2017-import.mjs <mesh> --kind <id> --as '…' --keep-origin --root C:/Users/tilak/Downloads/BF2_Extract`): the import log says `t_e11_nam__orm_4a0d6485.png ← t_e11_nam.png + t_e11_cs.png + t_e11_rgb.png (orm, rebuilt)` for each. The E-11's ORM now means 252 / 162 / **109**. Sizes: e11 93,108 → 81,304 bytes, a280 98,932 → 88,260, dh17 259,820 → 251,328, dlt19 100,580 → 89,816, dlt19x 134,576 → 116,244, dlt20a 95,840 → 87,064, rk3 77,988 → 69,536, rt97c 106,288 → 95,688 (the WebP of a mask that is mostly flat packs smaller). The catalogue rows and credits came out unchanged.

Checks: `npm run lint` clean; `bf2017-textures`, `bf2017-import`, `held.glb` tests pass; `node scripts/health.mjs --check --skip build` within budget. `npm test` on this Windows machine: 110 failures in 76 files, none in the changed areas: the galaxy surface suites fail at load with the Windows ESM `file://` scheme error, the path tests on separators, and the weather crossfade on load timeouts (the known Windows-only set; CI is the gate).

Follow-ups, not done here:

- The NAM's blue is the game's edge-wear mask: in the game it lightens and bares the metal on edges. The import does not draw it; folding it into the colour (lighter) and the roughness (lower) where it is bright would give the worn edges the capture shows.
- The hero hilts (`hiltluke`, `hiltvader`, …) are on the same preset with a `_Mask`, imported `--native` (the bucket's own derived KTX2, with the same `metal = NAM|b`). They would need a non-native re-import, or the bucket's derived KTX2 remade, to get their metal.
- The `_Alpha` preset parts (sight glass, emitters) are drawn opaque, as the weapons research noted.

## 4. Open-source models instead?

No. The pipeline already brings the game's own licensed weapons and soldiers in at the game's geometry (DICE's LOD chain, 2,012 triangles for the E-11's light cut, 4,031 plain) with the game's own colour, normal and packed maps, in the game's frame with the game's muzzle bones, rigged to the game's skeleton for the game's clips; what was wrong was one channel in one derived map, which a one-function fix and a re-import mended. An open-source replacement (a Sketchfab CC E-11, Quaternius' low-poly guns, a KitBash3D set) would give a mesh with its own glTF-ready PBR maps and no packing to decode, which is the one thing that broke here; it would not give the exact film prop at the game's proportions, the game's wear and smoothness, the weapon skeleton (`Wep_Root`, `Wep_Muzzle`, `Wep_Trigger`) the held clips and the muzzle flash key off, the per-class set the records name, nor, for the soldiers, the Walrus rig with its faces, cloth and sockets that every clip, ragdoll and hurtbox in the site assumes. A generic soldier would mean re-rigging or retargeting every clip and re-fitting the hurtboxes and sockets; a generic gun would mean re-deriving the muzzle and grip for each. Recommendation: keep the game's models, keep decoding the game's packing (the shader name in each material's extras says how), and spend the open-source route only on things the drop lacks.

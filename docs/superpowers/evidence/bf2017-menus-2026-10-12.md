## 2. The fonts

`public/fonts/bf2017/*.ttf` are all real TrueType files (every one starts `00 01 00 00`). The console's `Failed to decode downloaded font … LinotypeUnivers-520CnMedium.ttf, OTS parsing error: invalid sfntVersion 1008821359` was not about them: `1008821359` is `0x3C21646F`, the bytes `<!do` of `<!doctype html>`. `src/components/battlefront/hud/battlefront.css` declared three faces over `/battlefront/fonts/…`, a folder that was removed when the drop's set moved to `public/fonts/bf2017/` (HANDOFF-bf2017: "Lane 0's copies under public/battlefront/fonts/ were removed"), and the dev server's (and the host's) single-page fallback answered each request with `index.html`. The HUD, the spawn screen and the game's screens all fell to Arial (`before-spawn.webp`). The Battlefront world never imported `fonts.css` either (the galaxy's pages did).

Fixed: `battlefront.css` names `fonts.css`'s families by weight (`--bf-font-label` → `--font-bf-hud` at 500, `--bf-font-bold` at 700, `--bf-font-number` → `--font-bf-numerals`), `BattlefrontWorld.jsx` and `GameScreen.jsx` import `fonts.css`. No font warning in the console after; the HUD's labels and numerals and the menu's tabs are in Univers Condensed and RaxusPrime (`after-main-menu.webp`, `after-deploy.webp`).

## 4. The causes, and what changed

1. **A click on the spawn screen deployed no one.** `BattlefrontWorld.jsx` sent `world.world?.do(...)`; useWorld's handle keeps the world in a ref, `handle.world.current`. Every deploy, pick, squad spawn, the in-game menu's Respawn and the HUD's way out through `do` were no-ops since the page's first commit (b1420ec, 2026-10-10); only Enter, read by the module's own input, deployed. Now `send.js`'s `sendTo(handle, action, arg)`, tested. (`Spawn.jsx`'s effect returned the world's reply, which React refuses as a cleanup: in braces now.)
2. **React starved while shaders built.** three's `buildAsync` yields between its stages with `scheduler.yield()`, whose continuations run ahead of any new task of their priority; three's queue starts the next build in the microtask of the last one's landing; the guard handed the whole queue over at once. So after the deploy (the camera moves to the soldier, a hundred and more objects come into view) the main thread was one run of continuations for 6 to 9 s: the frames ran (rAF outranks them), the input ran (it outranks everything: the sim deployed in 1 ms of the click), but React's commit, every timer and every CDP call waited for the queue to drain. `pipelineGuard.js` now wraps `Nodes._processBuildQueue` and, every four builds, goes on from a timer task (`processInRuns`), so the page gets a turn within four builds' worth. Handing builds over one or a few at a time was tried first and does not do: a build started on its own, from a timer or a frame callback, took 150 to 300 ms where the chain's take 40, and Hoth's props stayed out of the picture for 35 to 50 s (`after8`-style runs, not kept).
3. **The fonts**, section 2.
4. **Duplicate keys in the screens.** 405 pairs of sibling boxes share an id in the game's screens (a footer's two `Select` cells, a stack's `DecoLineSeparation` lines, a `SpacerNull`): React warned once a render (250 times in a battle, each with a component stack in development) and dropped or doubled boxes. `screens.js` gives a second sibling of a name the id `name#2`; `screens.test.js` holds every screen to it.
5. **The check script could never reach the battle.** `scripts/battlefront-check.mjs` waited for the reticle `visible`; the kit's reticle is a 0 × 0 box with its ticks round it, so the wait timed out and the walk's in-world screens were recorded as "not reached in software GL" on every CI run. It waits `attached` now.

Not changed, and why: the world draws at the full chain behind the opaque loading screen (that is what queues the shaders); the frames while shaders build (7 to 8 a second for the first 6 to 7 s on the spawn screen and again after the deploy) are the builds' own cost, 40 to 90 ms each, which this change leaves as they were; the pace controller never stepped in any run (`pace` in the JSON: level 0, internal 1, source gpu, a chain of 10); the site's bar floats over the game's screens (the house rule).

# Hand-off: the world runtime, the renderers and performance

One runtime draws every world that has moved onto it: it owns the canvas, the renderer, the frame loop, input, audio, saves, assets, workers, the floating origin and the handover from one world to the next. Under it sit two renderers, three’s `WebGPURenderer` with TSL materials (WebGPU where the browser has it, its WebGL 2 backend everywhere else) and the classic WebGL renderer for the worlds whose shaders are still GLSL. Round them: the device tiers and the pace (what a chip can hold, what to shed when it cannot), the performance lanes that cut the remaining work into sessions, the NPC AI toolkit the worlds’ characters share, and the rigging layer that poses any figure from one set of code. This file replaces the seven hand-offs it was merged from.

## Landed

- **The runtime** (`src/runtime/`; the headers of `index.js`, `runtime.js` and `module.js` say what each does). A module is `{ id, shading, mb, label?, create(rt, props) → world }`; `fromScene` wraps a `useScene` scene unchanged. `rt` gives a module `gfx`, `input`, `quality`, `saves`, `assets`, `audio`, `events`, `workers`, `origin` and `handover` (`handover.js`: the old world draws on until the new one is ready, then its last frame fades out over it; the cover is a 2D snapshot, not a GPU crossfade). The page mounts a module with `useWorld.js` and `WorldHost.jsx`; `?debug` opens the tuning panel (`debug.js`). Earth is the reference migration (`src/components/earth/module.js`); the galaxy and its surfaces are `fromScene` with the dive and the climb flown as handovers (`src/components/galaxy/travel.js`, checked by `scripts/travel-check.mjs`). Dev hook: `window.__RUNTIME__`.
- **The backends** (`src/runtime/backend.js`, `webgpu.js`, `webgl.js`, `browser.js`). Three kinds: `'webgl'` (the classic renderer, every `'glsl'` module, always), `'webgpu'` and `'nodes-webgl'` (`WebGPURenderer({ forceWebGL: true })`). A `'nodes'` module never meets the classic renderer but through a classic path of its own: one with `classic: true` (the galaxy surface’s `looks/classic.js`, lane T2’s #912) draws on it at low and mid where WebGPU cannot be had, the node renderer over WebGL 2 at high and ultra, and `?gpu=classic` asks for it at any level (`backend.test.js` pins every cell). `?gpu=webgl|webgpu|classic` forces a kind and `tp-gpu` keeps it. A lost WebGPU device mounts its world again on WebGL 2; a first WebGL loss is recoverable too; a weak chip is held there (`src/lib/gpuProbe.js`’s `lossHolds`, `src/runtime/index.js`’s `onLoss`). Every context asks for `high-performance`, and a test fails one that asks for less.
- **The shading promise** (`src/runtime/shadingClosure.js`, `shading.test.js`): a `'nodes'` module fails on any GLSL site anywhere in its import closure (`ShaderMaterial`, `onBeforeCompile`, `EffectComposer` and its passes). The health measure `glsl-sites` (`scripts/health/glsl-sites.mjs`) counts what a port removes. Modules on `'nodes'` today: Earth, Minecraft, the galaxy surface, Battlefront.
- **The TSL twins** (new files beside a GLSL original that is never edited): `src/lib/three/*Nodes.js` (core, dust, foliage, grass, groundLook, grounding, groundingBake, groundmap, groundwork, hook, house, ink, kit, matcap, portalFx, puffs, recolour, scans, splat, wind), `src/components/galaxy/surface/nodes/`, `src/components/earth/nodes.js`, `src/components/minecraft/scene/nodes.js`. Two lessons every port needs: a world whose GLSL tone mapped sets `NoToneMapping` and linear output and writes the classic output itself (Earth: the whole 25 dB gap); a texture-array layer is rounded, since the node renderer cuts it off (Minecraft: 25.79 dB to 41.55).
- **Parity and the probe**: `scripts/twin-parity.mjs` (a twin against its original), `scripts/gpu-parity.mjs` with `scripts/gpu-parity/README.md` (a route at a ref and on both legs; the bar is 32 dB and under 2 % of pixels off by more than 16/255; it runs the dev server, since `__RUNTIME__` exists only there), `scripts/perf-probe.mjs` (`GPU=`, `QUALITY=`, `--heavy` for the heavy worlds in `scripts/lib/perf-routes.mjs`; the median and worst 1 % frame, main-thread and GPU ms, draws, programs, pipelines made late). The RTX 5090’s baseline is in the perf evidence README.
- **The device, the tiers and the pace** (`src/lib/device.js`, `src/lib/fit.js`, `src/lib/video.js`, `src/runtime/quality.js`, `cadence.js`, `dynres.js`, `src/lib/three/gpuTime.js`): Auto by the chip (a score under 50, or a renderer tier kept as weak, is Low); Low as the floor (720p, no bloom, 8 lamps and 2 spots, first-row maps, five bots a side); the cadence by the display’s beat with a pinned 60; GPU-clock dynamic resolution for `'nodes'` worlds on WebGPU; the Star Wars worlds need a real GPU (score 10, a card with Try anyway); the built-in graphics panel (`src/lib/gpuHint.js`, `src/components/worlds/GpuHint.jsx`); the PNG16 decode and meshopt in workers. Pull requests #985 and the one after it.
- **The GPU scene and the guards** (`src/lib/three/gpuScene/`: culling, HZB, LOD fade, packing, indirect draws in one buffer; `BundleGroup` in `gpuScene/three.js`), `src/lib/three/pipelineGuard.js` (no frame waits for a shader), `src/lib/three/frameGuard.js` (`heldBack()`; #693 fixed the background it had dropped), `src/lib/three/light/post.js` and `passes.js` (the post chain, TAAU and TRAA).
- **The small loops** (lane P8, #991): `src/lib/shownLoop.js`, a frame loop that runs only on screen in a showing tab, under the portal swirl, the TV static (now a noise picture moved by CSS), the hangar, the wardrobe and the Collection’s hero. Measured: the portal scrolled away 40.8 → 34.3 ms a second of main thread, the wardrobe open 352 → 294.
- **The main thread** (lane P4, #993): the runtime’s frame makes no garbage (one input snapshot refilled, its press sets taking turns, a world keeping none of it; the cadence’s argument; the GPU clock’s resolve; dynres’s median sorted as it goes; `readPad` without its spreads); the MiniMap on a paced clock (`src/runtime/hud/clock.js`) in a ResizeObserver’s box; `useWorld` gives a world `update()` only on changed props (`sameProps`), a frame after every render; Battlefront’s frame on a lean `view(b, { lean: true })`, the HUD’s snapshot the only full one. Those were below the noise: P0’s census put Battlefront’s main thread in three’s walk of its graph (5,617 objects, 4,261 of them the 101 soldiers’ bones, 1,057 the placed lights’ pool, every one recomposed and walked in each pass). So bones with nothing drawn under them are out of the walk (`src/components/battlefront/figures/lod.js` `quietBones`: 5,617 visible objects to 1,543), far soldiers animate every second or fourth frame (`animEvery`: 30 m, 70 m), and the lights’ pool (`src/lib/three/light/placed.js`) makes matrices only where it moves a light. Measured, Battlefront on Hoth at High on WebGPU on the 5090 (the same draws, 147 and 146): `_projectObject` 1,105 → 327 ms in 6 s of profile, the main thread 5.6 → 4.3 ms a frame, 157 → 200 fps; at Low 1.9 → 1.6 ms, 479 → 566 fps.
- **The HUD kit** (`src/runtime/hud/`; the rules in `docs/health/RULES.md`, “The worlds’ HUDs”): every world’s HUD on one set of parts taking plain values, the world’s own classes skinning them; `scripts/health/hud-kit.mjs` counts the worlds off it (one: the trench run, on purpose).
- **The NPC AI toolkit** (`src/lib/ai/`; `index.js` lists the modules): utility, behaviour trees, perception, search, context steering, spatial picking, influence maps, squads, needs, bodies and reactions, all pure and tested in Node. Wired: the universe map’s nemeses, law, pirates, hunters and director (`src/components/universe/npcRules.js`, `npcs/`; `scripts/universe-npc-check.mjs`), the surface’s hostiles and the Battlefront armies (`src/components/galaxy/surface/hostiles.js`, `activity.js`, `missions/assault.js`), the towns’ watchers (`src/components/middleearth/towns/watchers.js`), the Decepticons (`src/components/cybertron/game/rules.js`), the Goombas and Bob-ombs (`src/components/mario64/rules/actors/foes.js`), the Citadel’s crowd on needs (`src/components/rickmorty/citadel/ambient.js`), steering in the Rick and Morty, Middle-earth, Expanse and Death Star worlds. The game’s clips name the surface’s behaviours (`src/lib/three/walrusSets/npc.js`). The one difficulty knob, `createTokens`’ `scale`, is set from the tier on the surface.
- **The rigging layer** (`src/lib/three/rig.js`, `animator.js`, `locomotion.js`, `clipLibrary.js`, `figureCalls.js`, `ik.js`, `grip.js`, `meshyRig.fixture.js`; `scripts/ual-bake.mjs`, `scripts/anim-check.mjs`): figures rigged four ways posed by one set of code, in the figure’s own frame.

## The owner’s rules

- The owner’s word (2026-10-13): every world defaults to WebGPU, choosing whatever gives the best quality for the performance; a TSL shader is as good as the GLSL it replaces or better, never worse; at least 60 fps on a strong chip and 30 on a weak one, and faster is fine; the GPU asked for everywhere; no lag from a busy main thread; merge each task as it is finished.
- A port that fails parity, or is slower on the node renderer, is not merged. A GLSL original is never edited while another world draws with it; the module flips to `'nodes'` last.
- A level picked by hand and `?quality=`, `?tier=`, `?gpu=`, `?fps=` are never overridden. T1 and T2 chips keep everything; a WebGPU loss holds a weak chip only (settled 2026-10-13).
- Nothing a visitor can do is lost. Every key, chip, dialog, save key, achievement, sound, ghost and dev hook works after a migration as before; a migration moves the renderer, the loop, input, audio, saves and assets, never gameplay. Save keys keep their names.
- Every module is `'glsl'` until its shaders are TSL; `shading.test.js` enforces the promise. Do not flip early.
- Page scenes (ambience, stages, cartridges, the travel globe, the contact plane) stay on `useScene`. One canvas per runtime; two live backends at once were considered and dropped. The phone download gate stays on the route. A world draws every frame on screen unless its `wants()` says no.
- No runtime calls to asset services; never print or commit a key. Pure logic in tested modules apart from the drawing, tests first.
- One world, lane or phase per pull request, merged on its own: CI green, merge commit, never red, never force-pushed. Measure before and after with the probe and put the numbers in the pull request.
- Before a change is done: `npm run lint`, `npm test`, `npm run build`, `node scripts/health.mjs --check --skip build`; the smoke (`scripts/autopilot-check.mjs --only smoke`) on the world’s routes; a dev-mode flight through `__RUNTIME__`. A whole-repo scan test can pass its 5 s limit under the full suite in the cloud container: run it alone before calling it real. `npm run build` rewrites `public/github.json`; put it back. In the container the browser draws in software, so the Star Wars gate shows its card (`tp-software` on, or `?software=on`), and SwiftShader’s WebGPU device is lost: the WebGPU leg is measured on a real chip only.
- Docs, comments and commits in the repo’s voice: British spelling, curly quotes, comments say why; no model names.

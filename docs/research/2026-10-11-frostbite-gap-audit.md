# The Frostbite reconstruction against the GPU-first lanes: what Rapier, WebAssembly and WebGPU still leave out, what the assets carry, and where the lanes drift

Date: 2026-10-11 (late). Status: an audit and a proposal for the owner; no code. Written from `origin/main` at `59191ed5` (#903), the eight GPU-first branches (`claude/gpu-first-renderer` #915, `claude/gpu-g1-reference` #919, `claude/gpu-n1-scene` #923, `claude/gpu-n2-picture` #917, `claude/gpu-n3-light` #921, `claude/gpu-n4-stream` #918, `claude/gpu-n5-sim` #924) and lane T2’s (`claude/bf2017-t2-flip` #912), the design `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-11-gpu-first-renderer-design.md` and its research note (both on #915’s branch), `2026-10-11-bf2017-picture-everywhere-design.md`, `2026-10-10-bf2017-physics-design.md`, the stack pages `physics-rapier.md`, `webassembly.md` and `webgpu-tsl.md`, and three read-only sweeps of the repository (the renderer and physics code, the shipped assets under `public/`, the specs and hand-offs). Every number names where it was read.

## What the owner asked

“What can we improve in Rapier, WebAssembly and WebGPU to replicate the Frostbite engine with all the assets we have, the best quality a PC can run. Check the repo and architect what can be improved. Check all assets. Other PRs and sessions are working, so check the drift.”

## The answer in one paragraph

The architecture the owner asked for is already designed and half built: #915 is Unreal’s methods under the Frostbite layer in eight lanes, six of them in flight as draft PRs with 1,800 to 5,400 lines each. This note does not redesign it. It finds what the eight lanes still leave out of a Frostbite frame, measured against the game’s own records and the Frostbite papers, and what the lanes are about to collide on. **Rapier**: the right engine, pinned at the newest release, on the main thread by a measured 0.5 ms step; what is wrong is how it arrives: the `-compat` builds inline 2.9 to 3.1 MB of wasm as base64 inside a 4.1 to 4.4 MB module (1.58 MB gzipped against 1.1 MB for the bare wasm) and compile it from bytes instead of streaming, on every Star Wars world on every tier once T2 lands; and the worker’s switch (N5) is keyed on the step’s time alone when the main thread’s budget is what N1 is fighting for. **WebAssembly** is where it pays (Rapier, the Basis transcoder, meshopt, the 20 KB A* port); no new port is recommended without a profile; the load path is the gain. **WebGPU**: the eight lanes cover the static scene, the picture, three distances of light, the stream and debris, and leave four things Frostbite has in every outdoor frame: the figures (every soldier, bot and ragdoll is a `SkinnedMesh` on the per-object path N1 is built to escape), unified volumetrics (the record’s fog lit by every placed light in a froxel grid, not cones a volume), the sphere lights’ shape (the rulebook’s `PbrSphereLightEntityData` has a radius the clustered point light drops), and the terrain (the one layer in every frame, drawn at 1 m from a 0.5 m source with derived masks). **The assets**: 1.2 GB in git, of which the ten biggest models are Sketchfab-era landmarks at 8 to 15 MB each as one primitive with WebP maps and no LOD, 121 HDR files all at 512 × 256 or smaller, and a 16 MB asset manifest imported by client code. **The drift**: every GPU-first branch conflicts with `main` on the health snapshot, N4 on seven files it shares with the merged D2 and E3, N2, N3 and T2 all rewrite `light/post.js`, and N4 has put 100 horizon GLBs (9.8 MB) into git against the decision that assets live in the bucket. Section 6 proposes five lanes (figures, volumetrics, sphere lights, Rapier’s load path, the landmarks and the manifest), two desktop asks (terrain masks, HDR output) and a merge order; each waits on the owner’s word.

## 2. Rapier

**What is true today** (`src/lib/physics/`, `docs/stack/physics-rapier.md`, lane X2’s evidence): `@dimforge/rapier3d-compat@0.21.0` and `@dimforge/rapier3d-simd-compat@0.21.0`, the newest release (Rapier 0.36: soft bodies, the rewritten sleeper, sweep CCD against fixed colliders, the static-world broad phase); the SIMD build where `src/lib/wasm/features.js` validates a probe, the scalar one else; imported by `src/lib/physics/engine.js:21-22` only and dynamically; a fixed 1/60 s step with an accumulator and at most 4 substeps a frame (`world.js:90,190`); on the main thread (no `Worker` under `src/lib/physics`, `src/lib/wasm`, `src/lib/battlefront` or `src/runtime`); the Battlefront’s whole CPU step 0.5 ms a frame (lane X2). Seven worlds make a Rapier world (`battlefront/module.js:83`, `galaxy/surface/level/index.js:236`, `galaxy/surface/knocks.js:69`, `universe/landings/physics.js:49`, `cybertron/game/loose.js:30`, `avengers/world/lawnProps.js:30`, `albuquerque/world/roadside.js:24`). Lane T2 puts Rapier on every Star Wars world on every tier and a phone; lane N5 puts the same face in a Worker behind `createPhysics({ worker: true })`, off until a step is measured over 2 ms.

The design is right about the engine and about the thread. What it leaves:

**2.1 The `-compat` build is the wrong shape for a world that should play within seconds.** The compat packages inline the wasm as base64 inside the JavaScript module, so that no bundler plugin is needed (`engine.js:9`’s reason). The cost, measured in `node_modules` on 2026-10-11:

| file | raw | gzip |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `rapier3d-compat/dist/rapier.mjs` (glue with the wasm inlined) | 4.14 MB | 1.58 MB |
| `rapier3d-compat/dist/rapier_wasm3d_bg.wasm` (the same wasm, bare) | 2.94 MB | 1.11 MB |
| `rapier3d-simd-compat/dist/rapier.mjs` | 4.41 MB | 1.58 MB |
| `rapier3d-simd-compat/dist/rapier_wasm3d_bg.wasm` | 3.14 MB | 1.09 MB |

So the visitor downloads about 0.5 MB more than the engine weighs, the base64 is decoded on the main thread, and the module is compiled by `WebAssembly.instantiate(bytes)` after the whole download instead of `instantiateStreaming` during it. On a phone at mid the physics is on the path to the first step the body takes. Vite 8 serves a `.wasm` as an asset (`new URL('./rapier_wasm3d_bg.wasm', import.meta.url)` or `?url`) and `WebAssembly.instantiateStreaming(fetch(url), imports)` compiles as the bytes arrive; the bare packages `@dimforge/rapier3d@0.21.0` and `@dimforge/rapier3d-simd@0.21.0` ship the same `rapier_wasm3d_bg.wasm` and a glue that takes the module. The floats are the same wasm’s, so every `src/lib/physics` test and `x2-profile.mjs`’s replay (0 of 2,946 searches differ) must stay exactly as they are. **Proposed lane X3** (§6): the two bare packages behind `engine.js`’s same `loadEngine`, the wasm published as a hashed asset (or from the bucket, as the packs are), `instantiateStreaming` with the `instantiate` fallback where the server sends the wrong MIME type, the two compat packages removed, `docs/stack/physics-rapier.md` and `webassembly.md` updated. Gate: bytes over the wire and time to `status().ready` on the laptop and a phone, before and after; the replay identical.

**2.2 The worker’s switch measures the wrong budget.** N5 turns the worker on only “where a world’s measured step passes 2 ms a frame” (its plan’s constraints). The step is 0.5 ms; but the main thread is what the frame is short of: three’s per-object path costs 29 ms for 5,000 meshes (issue #30560, the design’s §1), N1 exists to take `render()` under 2 ms, and every 0.5 ms the step takes is 0.5 ms of that same thread. The criterion should be the main thread’s headroom: `render()` CPU time plus the step plus the sim against the frame’s budget, from G1’s clock and `renderer.info`, not the step alone. The hop (0.1 to 0.5 ms, the research note) is paid on the worker’s side of the message, not the renderer’s. N5’s face is already built for this; only the rule in `tiers.js` (N6) changes.

**2.3 The step to measure is the one during a cell swap.** N5’s profile (`x2-profile.mjs`) measures a steady battle. The stream adds and removes a cell’s colliders in a burst (`level/levelPhysics.js`, P0: near 3 × 3 at high is 1,123 bodies and 6,160 colliders); Rapier 0.35’s broad phase is tuned for large static worlds, and a burst of removals and insertions is where it is least tuned. The gate N5 and N6 should add: step p95 over the frames of an `onCell` and an `onDrop`, on Hoth at ultra, on the laptop. If that is where 2 ms is crossed, the fix is staging the inserts over frames (`budget.js` already paces the queries), not the worker.

**2.4 Determinism across the two builds.** The scalar and SIMD builds are the same Rust, but SIMD lanes and fused operations round differently, so two visitors on different builds do not get bit-identical ragdolls or sweeps. The sim is pure JavaScript at 50 ms (`src/lib/battlefront/sim.js`), which is right; `lib/physics/queries.js` (“eyes: raycast and shape queries for brains and bolts”) is read by `galaxy/surface/scene.js`. The rule to write into `docs/stack/physics-rapier.md`: nothing Rapier answers enters state another player’s sim must agree with; a bot’s brain that reads a Rapier query is per-client presentation, never the shared truth. The multiplayer stays on the seed and the sim.

**2.5 What is fine as it is.** Four substeps at 1/60 after a hitch is 2 ms at the measured step; the soldier on `KinematicCharacterController` is the player’s alone (`character.js`), the bots on the navgrid; trimesh for the static Havok shapes and hulls under 64 points (P0) is the right split; sleeping hulls becoming static instances (N5) is Chaos’s own trick; the 15-body ragdolls with the live-hull cap counting them (N5’s Review Focus 4) is the right cap. P3 (the vehicles’ bodies, feet, the rides on the walls) is still unstarted and is the one physics lane with no session.

## 3. WebAssembly

**What is true today** (`docs/stack/webassembly.md`, `src/lib/wasm/`, `wasm/navsearch/`): four modules run as wasm. Rapier (above); the Basis transcoder (527 KB, fetched the first time a GLB carries a KTX2, `lib/three/wasmDecoders.js:41`); meshopt’s decoder (every GLB); and `navsearch` (Rust, `wasm-bindgen` 0.2.100, 20 KB, the Battlefront navgrid’s A*, 153 → 51 µs a search, lane X2). No threads: GitHub Pages sets no COOP and COEP headers, the service-worker shim broke the bucket’s assets, Safari has no `credentialless` (the decision). `features.js` detects wasm, SIMD and threads by validating tiny modules. `load.js` is one try with a 20 s timeout and a named `WasmError`.

What to change, and what not to:

- **The load path, not more ports.** The design’s rule (“WebAssembly ports only where a number says so”; “branchy object-graph logic stays JavaScript”) is right and this audit found no kernel that passes it. The candidates looked at: the cell `.bin` unpack (already typed-array views, zero copy); N1’s `pack.js` quantising an instance table to 32 bytes (once a cell, microseconds); Havok shapes to Rapier colliders (`havok.js` hands hull points to `ColliderDesc.convexHull`, whose quickhull already runs in Rust); the bolts’ sweeps (`bolts.js`, a few hundred a frame, branchy). The gain left is §2.1: streaming the two big modules.
- **The transcoder is the right one.** The 17 KTX2 files in `public/` are all UASTC with Zstd and full mips (every header read); the packs’ maps on the bucket are the game’s ETC1S at 1,024 (the picture-everywhere design, §1), with N4 adding UASTC data rows. Both codecs are needed, so the universal transcoder stays; three’s `KTX2Loader` already transcodes in its worker pool, and UASTC to BC7 is the cheap path on the 87 % of WebGPU adapters with BC.
- **Memory.** Rapier’s linear memory grows and never shrinks; the runtime keeps one renderer across routes (`src/runtime/runtime.js`), and a world change that makes a new Rapier world without freeing the old keeps both. `world.js` frees its queries (`:121`) and has a `dispose()`; the check to add to `world.load.test.js`: after `dispose()` the wasm memory’s `buffer.byteLength` does not grow across ten world changes.
- **Not to do.** No Emscripten port of anything (nothing here is C++); no `wasm-bindgen-rayon` (threads are off and Rapier’s npm build is single-threaded regardless); no relaxed SIMD (flagged); no memory64 (no module here nears 4 GB).

## 4. WebGPU: what the eight lanes build, and what a Frostbite frame still has that they do not

**What the lanes build** (#915, §4): G1 the GPU clock, the renderer tier and FLIP references; N1 the GPU scene (instances in a storage buffer, compute culling with the LOD cut and a depth pyramid, one indirect draw a material group, bundles); N2 the stable picture (GPU-time dynamic resolution on Unreal’s constants, TRAA or TAAU with a sharpen pass, mip bias, velocity everywhere, reversed depth, specular anti-aliasing, a 30 fps cadence); N3 three distances of light (the Enlighten atlases or the site’s own bake far, a budgeted probe grid mid, SSGI near, SSR under roughness 0.4, cached far cascades over the export’s shadow caches); N4 the stream (a coarse horizon grid first, a boot slice, priority by heading, low mip first, UASTC rows, byte budgets); N5 debris and particle lights with Rapier in a worker behind the same face; N6 the tier table; N7 the cluster spike. On the branches: N1 has `gpuScene/{scene,cull,cullPass,hzb,draws,lodFade,lodRule,pack}.js`; N2 has `runtime/dynres.js`, `cadence.js`, `light/velocity.js`, `mipBias.js`, `sharpen` in the chain; N3 has `light/baked.js`, `probes.js`, `shadows.js` with `farCache`, the Enlighten fixture; N4 the horizon writer and the Hoth horizon; N5 `physics/{worker,workerClient,breaks,poses}.js`, `particles/{debris,collide,emitter}.js`; G1 `gpuTime.js`, `gpuProbe.js`, `jitter.js`, `frame-score.mjs`.

**What Frostbite has in every outdoor frame of this game that none of the eight lanes draws:**

**4.1 The figures.** N1’s GPU scene holds “the level’s instances” (static); `gpuScene/scene.js` on the branch has no skinning path. Galactic Assault is 40 soldiers, the bots, the ragdolls (15 bodies each, on every surface since T2) and the vehicles, every one a `SkinnedMesh` through three’s per-object path, each with its own uniform group and bone texture, each a `Mesh` the renderer iterates: exactly the cost issue #30560 names, and the one path N1 does not touch. Frostbite’s ANT skins on the GPU with the palettes in one buffer. The reconstruction on three r186: one `Mesh` per (rig, LOD, material group) with `count` instances, the bone palettes as an `instancedArray` of `mat4` (bones × figures), written once a frame from the animators (`lib/three/animator.js`, `animBudget.js`) or from a compute pass that samples packed clips, a TSL `skinning` written to read the palette by instance id, last frame’s palette kept for N2’s velocity, and a ragdoll’s pose from Rapier written into the same rows. The 16-sampled-textures and 8-storage-buffers floors hold (one palette buffer, one texture array a material group). Gate: at 40 and at 64 figures on Hoth, `render()` CPU time and the figure draws before and after; the figure FLIP unchanged.

**4.2 Unified volumetrics.** Lane V draws `volumes.json`’s glows and cones as `VolumeNodeMaterial` boxes on a layer at a quarter of the resolution, and the record’s fog as `fogVolume` (`light/volumetrics.js`, `fog.js`). Frostbite (Hillaire 2015, the paper the picture-everywhere design cites) does not draw volumes: it fills a camera-aligned 3D grid (froxels; 160 × 90 × 64 on the consoles of the time) with the participating media of the fog, every local volume and the particles, lights each froxel from the sun through its cascades and from every clustered local light, integrates temporally against last frame’s grid, then integrates along the view into a 2D scattering and transmittance texture that every surface and particle reads. The differences the eye reads: the hangar’s hundred lamps each lift the haze round them (today only the cones in `volumes.json` do); the dust in the Hoth day is lit by the sun’s shadow (god rays without a separate march); the fog has no seams between volumes. On WebGPU in 2026 it is one TSL compute pass into a `texture_storage_3d` (shipped; `texture-formats-tier2` not needed for `rgba16float` write-only), 0.9 M froxels at that size, reading `ClusteredLighting`’s list and the CSM; a few tenths of a millisecond on a discrete chip. `nodes-webgl` and `T0` keep lane V’s path by data. Gate: the hangar and the Hoth day on the lit fixture, FLIP against G1’s reference; GPU time under 0.8 ms at 1,600 × 900 on the laptop.

**4.3 The sphere lights’ shape.** The rulebook reader keeps two light kinds from the levels: `PbrSphereLightEntityData` and `PbrSpotLightEntityData` (`scripts/lib/bf2017-rulebook-look.mjs:6,67,105`). A sphere light has a radius; `light/placed.js` feeds `ClusteredLighting` point lights, which have none. Frostbite’s sphere light (Lagarde and de Rousiers 2014, §4.7) widens the specular highlight to the sphere (the representative-point method with its energy normalisation) and clips the diffuse by the horizon, and that is what makes a lamp read as a lamp on a wet floor or a helmet. The reconstruction: carry `Radius` through `lights.json` and `placed.js`, and apply the representative point and the normalisation in the game material’s direct term (a TSL `lightingModel` hook in `surface/compose.js`), the clustered list unchanged. Small, measurable on the hangar fixture against the game’s hangar frame.

**4.4 The terrain.** The one layer in every outdoor frame, and the one furthest from the game. The export has the 0.5 m detail heightfield over the arena and the 4,097² world field (`HANDOFF-bf2017-physics.md`, “the terrain”), and 173 terrain maps (Q2); the pack resamples the detail to 1 m and the far ground to 4 m (`terrain/near.png` and `far.png`, the levels-lighting design), and Q2’s layer masks are derived, not the game’s (the picture-everywhere design, §1). Frostbite draws terrain as a quadtree of patches with a procedural virtual texture of the layer masks. The reconstruction, in two halves: the desktop half is lane W’s job to read the `TerrainStreamingTree` and the layer mask textures from the depots where the records allow, so the masks are the game’s; the renderer half is a GPU clipmap in TSL (rings of one quad grid displaced from the heightfield as a texture, morphing between rings, no CPU mesh a cell), which also means the heightfield Rapier stands on (`physics/heightfield.js`) and the one drawn are one texture. After N4, since the horizon grid is where the far ground lives.

**4.5 HDR output, an ultra option.** three r186’s WebGPU backend configures the canvas with `toneMapping: { mode: 'extended' }` when the renderer’s `outputType` is `HalfFloatType` (`node_modules/three/src/renderers/webgpu/WebGPUBackend.js:356`), which on Chrome 129 and later puts the frame on an HDR display in HDR. The game’s picture is a linear tone map followed by its 33³ LUT (Q6), with no SDR shoulder to undo, so the HDR frame is the same chain with the output’s range widened. The runtime does not set `outputType` today (`src/runtime/webgpu.js`). One line and a measurement on the laptop with an HDR monitor; off by default, an ultra setting.

**4.6 What is fine as the lanes have it.** The tone mapper (the record’s, never ACES or AgX); GTAO with the record’s HBAO numbers; the five-Gaussian bloom; TRAA and TAAU with the sharpen (N2); reversed depth; specular anti-aliasing; the probe grid and the atlases (N3); the stream’s two grids and UASTC data rows (N4); debris and particle lights (N5); the cluster tier as a measured spike (N7); no feedback virtual texturing at these map sizes; pipelines warmed as cells stream (N4; WebGPU has no pipeline cache of its own to ask for). Glass without transmission (`lib/three/glass.js`) is M2’s family to finish; `decals.json` empty is the desktop’s (W).

What the lanes already carry: the packs’ ultra cut and 2,048 maps, anisotropy 16, the probes at 128² with the sun, the gate counting KTX2 and bins, the space packs out of git (D2, #926 merged); the HDR panoramas, the 200 Enlighten atlases, the 4,149 textures and the UASTC colour row (W, the desktop); UASTC data rows and the horizon grid (N4). What they do not:

1. **The landmarks are the one set of models no lane touches.** The nine `*.ultra.glb` surface landmarks (about 102 MB) and the Death Star are drawn under the same sky as the game’s packs and are the biggest things on screen at Theed, Tipoca, the Senate and the citadel. One primitive of 180,000 triangles means no LOD cut and no culling below the whole; WebP maps mean a CPU decode and a full-size upload with no streaming by size. The same pipeline the packs went through (`scripts/ktx2.mjs` is there, `meshoptimizer`’s simplifier is a devDependency): UASTC on the normal and data maps, ETC1S on colour below ultra, a `.lod1` and `.far` cut each by the same rule as the vehicles (`catalog/bf2017-vehicles.js`), and material groups so N1 can draw them. Gate: the `models MB` row and the landmark’s FLIP at its gallery pose unchanged.
2. **A 16 MB manifest ships in the galaxy’s chunk.** `bf2017-library.js:26` imports `galaxyAssets.json` whole, and Vite inlines a JSON import as a module. The build of 2026-10-11 (`npx vite build` at `59191ed5`) emits `dist/assets/echoLayout-*.js` at 13.19 MB (1.30 MB gzipped), the biggest chunk of the 960 by three times, loaded by the `Galaxy`, `Fly` and `Front` chunks, and the manifest’s hashes are in it (a row’s hash, `5d0d49d971a1`, is found there and in no other chunk). Of the 20.2 MB of JSON that client code imports across 83 files, this one file is 15.5 MB; the next is `bf2017/screens.json` at 0.63 MB. So every visitor who lands on a Star Wars world parses 13 MB of JavaScript for 62,860 pack rows the library never reads: it looks up the rows of the objects it places. The fix is a build step that writes the library’s own slice beside it (`scripts/build-manifest.mjs` already writes `assets-manifest.json`), and the pack rows stay a script-side file that `assets-check.mjs` reads. Gate: the chunk’s size before and after; `npm run coverage:bf2017` and `assets-check` unchanged.
3. **The environment light of every non-Battlefront world is a 512 × 256 panorama.** Every `.hdr` and `.exr` under `public/` is that size or smaller, so reflections on the universe’s ships, the office, the Avengers’ HQ and the CC0 sets are 512 wide before PMREM. That is the blur the owner describes, on the worlds the game’s records do not cover. 2,048 × 1,024 RGBE (or RGBA16F KTX2 once `basisu` is at a version with UASTC HDR) is a quarter of a megabyte more a world and a different picture on every metal and glass.
4. **`models/galaxy` at 371 MB in git.** The decision puts assets in the bucket as hashed files; D2 moved the space packs; the rest of `models/galaxy` (the surface landmarks, the fleet, the kits, the clips) is the same case, and `public/sw.js` already caches bucket URLs for an installed world.
5. **Licence lines.** `docs/assets/colliders.md` has none. A line that says whose shapes they are (the game’s, under the same permission) keeps `docs/assets/` the one place the credits live.

The 5,265 MP3 files (161 MB) are the voices and the game’s sounds and are not this note’s subject.

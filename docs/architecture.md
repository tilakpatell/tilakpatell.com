# Architecture notes

Where things live and how the bigger pieces work. The [README](../README.md) has the overview. This file has the detail.

## Where things live

- `src/data/` — the single source of truth for roles, projects, skills and education. Every page and the terminal read from here.
- `src/theme/` and `src/index.css` — the company themes (AWS, RTX, Bose, Pendar, Empowerreg, SRC). AWS is the default; the Experience page switches theme as each role scrolls past, and the switcher in the nav pins one for the session.
- `src/components/ambience/` — a live background behind the portfolio pages for each fan theme ([the design](superpowers/specs/2026-10-05-theme-ambience-design.md)). `Ambience.jsx`, in the app's shell, picks the theme's family (`kinds.js`, tested) and, only on the portfolio pages, on a device that can afford it and unless switched off under the colour picker (`setting.js`), loads `Layer.jsx`: a fixed box under `<main>` that draws `scenes/<family>.js` through `lib/three/useScene`. Every scene is built on `kit.js`: the renderer at a low pixel ratio and 30 frames a second, an orthographic camera in CSS pixels, the pointer and scroll eased, a click on empty page as a burst, and shared pieces (a GPU particle field, a burst pool, a full-screen shader, silhouettes, text sprites). The company themes get a quieter motif in CSS alone (`motifs.css`, with each project's own on its page; nothing downloaded: AWS's rack dots and smile, RTX's drawing grid, Bose's sound rings, Pendar's spectrum, Empowerreg's knowledge graph, SRC's radar sweep). The page's colour moves from `<body>` to `<html>` while either is up (`html[data-ambience]`), so the layer shows through.
- `src/components/RouteLine.jsx` — the line that draws itself down each page as you scroll. Any `[data-waypoint]` element is a stop.
- `src/components/feed/` — the portfolio as a feed: the six portfolio pages (Home, Experience, Projects, Résumé, Travel, Contact) one under the other, so the end of one runs into the next, with a divider between them and an end card after the sixth. `feed.js` is the order and the matching (tested); `Feed.jsx` is the element of all six routes, keeps the stack of pages mounted so far (the next one's chunk fetched as soon as it is next, the page mounted a viewport ahead with its hero held still until it is in sight), and replaces the address with whichever page holds the middle of the screen, carrying `state.feed` so `App`'s `ScrollToTop`, the nav and the theme can tell the feed's moves from a visitor's click (which starts the feed over at that page). `lib/page.js` gives each page whether it is the one on the address: only that one sets the title and drives the theme, and `usePageParams` keeps a page's own search params (a filter, a tab) while it is off the address and puts them back when it is current again. The design is in `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-05-feed-design.md`.
- `src/stages/` — the live demo on each project page (the Game Boy one is playable).
- `src/components/contact/PaperPlane.jsx` — a paper airplane folded from the memo pad (`plane/scene.js`) glides a figure of eight beside the contact page's heading, banking into its turns, trailing dots in the theme's colour and leaning toward the pointer; a click loops it, and the memo form's send (`tp:memo-sent`) throws it off the screen while a fresh sheet folds itself into another.
- `src/lib/ai/` — the AI toolkit the worlds' NPCs share (pure, each module tested in Node; [the design](superpowers/specs/2026-10-07-npc-intelligence-design.md), [the research](../research/2026-10-07-game-ai-npcs.md)): `utility.js` weighs options (considerations through response curves, multiplied; momentum, rank, a spread for variety; `runtime` and `cooldown`), `tree.js` scripts a meeting (a behaviour tree whose state lives in the agent's blackboard, with a utility node), `perception.js` keeps what an NPC believes (a belief per target that rises along a detection timer in its cone, holds the truth a moment after sight is lost, then coasts and fades; a shot heard raises one), `search.js` looks for someone lost (a two-phase search shared by a group, and a flood on a grid for where a chase should go), `steer.js` moves without hitting things (context steering: interest and danger rings), `spatial.js` picks a place (scored candidates with hysteresis and a tie-breaking bias), `influence.js` reads the battlefield (proximity and threat stamps on a grid, a working map of layers) and `squad.js` makes a group act as one (squads by reach, a confidence that spreads, a frontline with lanes, retreat and press in halves, flankers, and attack tokens with a difficulty scalar). `body.js` turns a brain's step into what its body does (motion in the figure's own frame, a look target, a base pose and an action, from a per-world `MODE_BODY` table: a searcher walks scanning with its head, a suspicious one stops and stares, cover crouches, a strafer's hips face its travel and its chest its aim) and `react.js` what happens to it (`REACTIONS`: a greet waves, a line gestures, a hit flinches on the upper body, a death falls the way the shot went, gunfire scares, a win cheers; each with a cooldown and a chance, so a crowd doesn't react as one). [The design](superpowers/specs/2026-10-07-living-characters-design.md).
- `src/lib/three/animator.js`, `clipLibrary.js`, `locomotion.js`, `gait.js`, `animBudget.js` — the animation layer every character goes through ([the design](superpowers/specs/2026-10-07-living-characters-design.md)). `clipLibrary.js` (tested) names every clip by what it is (Rick's idle, walk, run and sit; Meshy's thirteen shared clips; the troopers' deaths; thirty-six clips from Quaternius's Universal Animation Library, baked onto the Meshy rig one GLB each by `scripts/ual-bake.mjs`) and retargets one to a figure's hips; the old `rickmorty/portal/clips.js` path re-exports it. `locomotion.js` (tested; moved from `universe/`) paces the walk and run to the ground covered, measured once a template. `animator.js` (tested on `meshyRig.fixture.js`, a Meshy skeleton with Luke's rest) is one body per rigged figure: locomotion and base states (sitting, crouching) on the mixer with weights that always sum to one, one-shots that restart when replayed, upper and lower layers laid over the mixer's pose by sampling, a head that looks, seeded fidgets, a queue of steps. `gait.js` (tested) is the same for figures without clips: a phase from distance, eased starts and turns, breath. `animBudget.js` (tested) steps far figures every second or fourth frame and unseen ones not at all.

## The world runtime

`src/runtime/` is one runtime for the full-screen worlds (the design: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-06-world-runtime-design.md`; the hand-off for moving each world: `docs/superpowers/HANDOFF-world-runtime.md`): one renderer (`webgl.js` on lib/three/renderer's WebGLRenderer; `webgpu.js` on three/webgpu's WebGPURenderer, for a module whose materials are nodes), one frame loop, one input snapshot a frame (`input.js`: the keys held and pressed, the gamepad, the pointer in the host box, the touch stick, with named actions a module binds), one quality level (`quality.js`: lib/device's tier and lib/three/pace's steps, setting the pixel ratio in one place), one save store (`saves.js`: localStorage and sessionStorage, the keys unchanged, with versions and migrations), one asset cache with an owner (`assets.js`: a module's textures and models are forgotten when it goes, unless retained; a prefetch queue by priority), and a gain per module on the site's audio context (`audio.js`, faded at unmount). `runtime()` makes it the first time a world asks and keeps it across routes: its one canvas is moved into whichever world's box is mounted, so a world change doesn't make a renderer, and `rt.handover(nextModule, props, host, { fade, held, after })` lets the next world take over without a cut (the old world draws on, seen, until the new one is ready, and then until `after` resolves, so a world can be built behind a dive or a climb that's still being flown; then the old world's last frame, a 2D snapshot, is kept over the new one and fades out: `handover.js`; `held` keeps it up until the page that shows the new world adopts it, for a handover the route changes after; it answers whether the new world is the one drawing, so a page knows not to steer when something newer was asked for meanwhile). A world is a module, `{ id, shading, mb, create(rt, props) }`, returning `{ ready, resize, step(dt, input, now), draw(frame), wants, handoff, dispose, … }` (`module.js` checks it; `fromScene` wraps a scene written for `useScene` unchanged). A page mounts one with `useWorld` into a `WorldHost` (a world of the same module already up is adopted, unless a page made it for another `rebuild`, the surface's mission, say: then it's made again, `adoptable`, tested); the module tells the page what happened through `rt.events`. The page scenes (the ambience, the stages, the cartridges) stay on `useScene`. `shading.test.js` reads every module that says `'nodes'` and fails if its folder makes a `ShaderMaterial`, patches `onBeforeCompile` or builds an `EffectComposer`, which WebGPURenderer can't run; every world is `'glsl'` until its shaders are ported. Worlds on it so far: Earth (`components/earth/module.js` is the flight, `EarthWorld.jsx` the HUD over it); the galaxy and its worlds' surfaces (`components/galaxy/module.js`, `galaxy/surface/module.js`: both `fromScene`, drawing with the runtime's renderer and putting back what they set on it). The trip between them is flown (`galaxy/travel.js`): landing dives the ship on the planet in space (`planDive`/`diveAt`, the air's glow coming up over it) while `pages/Galaxy.jsx` has the surface module built behind it (`rt.handover` with `after`: the dive's end), and the route follows; the surface goes on with its approach and landing. Taking off, the ship climbs out on the surface while `pages/GalaxySurface.jsx` has the galaxy built behind it, which takes over 3.4 s in under the sky's glare, the galaxy starting with the ship climbing off the planet (`LAUNCH_KEY`). Without 3D flying, both go the old way (a fade and the page). `scripts/travel-check.mjs` flies the round trip in headless Chromium.

Installs ([the design](superpowers/specs/2026-10-07-infinite-worlds-design.md), answer 2): each world declares what it fetches in `src/components/<world>/pack.js` (`PACK`: its page modules, its source, single URLs and folder globs, and the folders its source builds paths in (`computed`), with only the files it takes from them; `src/components/worlds/packs.js` gathers them, `packFor(pathname)`). The build's `packs()` plugin (`scripts/packs.mjs`) writes `dist/packs/<slug>.json` (every file, the page's chunks from `dist/.vite/manifest.json` included, with sizes and content hashes) and `dist/packs/index.json` (`{ [to]: { slug, bytes, v } }`), and fails when a world's source names an asset its pack misses (`scripts/pack-check.mjs`). `src/runtime/install.js` (`rt.install`, `installer()`) fetches a pack into the Cache API as `tp-pack-<slug>-<v>`, four files at a time with progress in bytes, skipping what is cached (an install resumes), carrying over files whose hash held from an older version, and asks once for persistent storage. `public/sw.js` is passive: registered (`src/lib/sw.js`) only while a pack is installed, it answers a file in an installed pack's current version from the cache and leaves everything else alone (no precache, no navigations, no sockets). `WorldGate.jsx` is the install's front door: a held world's card gives the true size and time, Install with a bar, then Open; a desktop is offered a heavy world's install in a pill. `/worlds` (`pages/Worlds.jsx`, `InstalledList.jsx`) lists what is installed with Remove, and the rest to install. Check: `scripts/sw-check.mjs` against `vite preview`.

The heavy assets ([the entry](decisions/2026-10-09-heavy-assets-mirrored-on-supabase-storage.md)): the models and textures of 64 KB and more under `public/hq/models`, `hq/tex`, `cc0/galaxy`, `models/gen3d` and `kit` (248 files, 83 MB of `public/`'s 1 GB) are mirrored to a Supabase Storage bucket at `<hash12>/<path>` by `scripts/assets-upload.mjs`, from the owner's shell, which writes `src/data/assets-manifest.json`. With `VITE_ASSET_BASE` set at build, `src/lib/assetBase.js` sends `lib/three/gltf.js`'s and `textures.js`'s fetches (and `loadGltfFile`'s) for those files to the bucket; the first failure sends that load and every later one to the site's own file for the visit. The build bundles only the manifest's entries whose file on disk still has that hash (`scripts/assets-manifest.mjs`), so nothing drawn differs. A pack lists a bucket file by its URL with `local` beside it; the install fetches it across origins (the site's copy if the bucket fails) and keeps it under that URL, and `public/sw.js`, told the base in its script's URL, answers that URL and the site's path of it from the copy, and no other URL of the bucket or the project. Check: `node scripts/sw-check.mjs --bucket`, against a stand-in bucket it serves itself.

The chunk services (`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-07-infinite-worlds-design.md`, answer 1), pure and tested in Node: `chunkGrid.js` (which cells round the player to ask for, nearest and ahead first, a cap in flight, which to let go of past the radius plus a band, and a generation so a late answer for a dropped cell or an older seed is refused), `workers.js` (`rt.workers`: a pool of workers per name, one job a worker, lowest priority first, cancel and transferables; a world calls `define(name, make)`), and `origin.js` (`rt.origin`: a floating origin moved in whole cells of 50,000 after a world's `anchor()` before its step, the shift sent as the event `origin`). Minecraft streams its chunks through the first two (`components/minecraft/stream.js`).

The driven planet of the Expanse (`/universe/expanse/:seed`) was a study, and it has gone: it was there to learn how Bruno Simon’s folio-2025 streams an endless land in chunks and keeps physics only where the player is, so the universe and the existing worlds can do the same. ## Graphics

Multiplayer across the worlds ([the design](superpowers/specs/2026-10-05-multiplayer-across-worlds-design.md)): the site's link (`universe/online/client.js`, held by `OnlineProvider` above the pages) is one room on the Nostr relays for everyone online, carrying the roster, the ships and crews, and the pointers on ordinary pages; each walkable world opens a room of its own for the people walking it (`middleearth/towns/useTravellers.js` → `travellers.js`, drawn by `ghosts.js`). Every room is joined with the visit's one key (`nostr.js`'s `visitKeys`, `joinAsVisitor`), so a traveller's id is their roster id: a block in the roster hides their ghost too (`travellers.js`'s `hidden`). `where.js` keeps each of Middle-earth's chapters a place of its own (`/middle-earth/bree`), named for the roster, whose Go button takes you into that world. While a world's up (`useOnline`'s `enterWorld`, from `useTravellers`) the page pointers aren't drawn over it, and the pages with their own corner (`ownCorner`: in flight, and down on a galaxy world) get no second one. `scripts/online-check.mjs` checks it in two browsers on the real relays. A room for a world too big for everyone to hear everyone (the flight's, `fly-v1:<planetId>`) is heard by cell: `src/lib/net/cells.js`'s grid, the `g` tag on what goes out and `#g` in the REQ, re-asked through `pool.js`'s `refresh()` when the cells round the pilot change; `expanse/flight/flightProtocol.js` is that world's wire.

Graphics: the site and its games are 3D first: they draw in WebGL (Three.js, loaded only when it's used) wherever the browser has it, a graphics chip or WebGL run in software, and lower their own resolution and effects when frames can't keep up (judged on real time, so a laptop's battery-saving 30 fps cap doesn't count). On the core pages that's the Experience diagrams, the travel globe, the mist over the Travel photos, the lamplight on the Peace section, the Akshardham day, the jump to lightspeed and the GPU project's demo; `src/lib/three/` holds their shared renderer, the theme-colour reader and the `useScene` hook that loads a scene only near the viewport and draws only while it's on screen. Every scene and game compiles its shaders in the background before its first frame (`renderer.js`'s `precompile`, with the browser's parallel compile where it has it), so something new coming into view doesn't stop the page while the graphics chip catches up; the first frame waits at most four seconds for it. A scene made while something covers the whole page (the universe, behind the intro's crawl and cockpit) also warms up out of sight, a slice at a time while the page is idle (`useScene` and the scene's `warmUp`): its pictures go to the graphics chip a few at a time, then one small draw, passes and all, sends the meshes and makes the passes' shaders, so its first frame at the cockpit's flash has nothing left to send. On a first visit the cockpit also tells the universe the ship you're sitting in (`tp:board`), so it's made, and its model fetched, before the launch (lib/three/renderer's `revealAll` and `uploadTextures` are the shared pieces; a low tier skips the warm-up). `src/lib/gpu.js` checks once. Only a browser with no WebGL at all, or a GPU context that's lost or fails, gets the SVG and 2D versions. Visitors can switch 3D off under the trench run. The universe map goes further, since a dogfight or a boost can push any graphics chip: `src/lib/three/pace.js` watches every frame and draws the scene a step softer within a second of frames coming late, and sharp again once they don't (the names and the HUD over it stay crisp); hunters and traffic ships are built ahead while the page is idle, so a pack arriving doesn't stall the fight; what a far planet shows waits until it's near (`universe/nearby.js`: its models and the landing's code, `footScene.js`; from the home system the fandoms' planets are a few pixels across); and multiplayer's signing and checking run in a worker (`online/signer.worker.js`), off the frames.

Textures and models: every texture goes through `src/lib/three/textures.js`'s `sharpen` (the device tier's anisotropy, 16 on a desktop, 4 on a phone; the right colour space; wrapping), and `loadTexture` decodes images off the main thread (`ImageBitmapLoader`, where the browser handles it) and shares one texture per URL across scenes. Every model comes through `src/lib/three/gltf.js`: one `GLTFLoader` with meshopt that also reads GPU-compressed KTX2 textures, fetching its transcoder only for a file that carries one (`scripts/ktx2.mjs` makes those, and reports per texture whether the bytes are worth it: the measurements are in `docs/research/2026-10-05-textures-and-asset-quality.md`). `prepare` gives a loaded model shadows, uncullable skinned meshes and sharpened maps; `tune` clamps a generator's material defaults. Big tiled grounds (the compound's lawn and apron, Roll out's desert, the music courtyard's dunes) go through `src/lib/three/surface.js`'s `antiTile`, which blends a turned second copy of the same maps in by a slow noise of world position so the repeat never lines up, with a fine detail normal fading in underfoot.

One look ([the design](superpowers/specs/2026-10-07-house-look-design.md), after [why theirs look expensive](research/2026-10-06-why-theirs-look-expensive.md)): `src/lib/three/house.js` is the house style every world moves onto, one world at a time (the Shire first). `createHouse(look)` patches every lit material of a world (`adopt(root)`, after its floor light) or makes new ones (`material()`): after three has lit a point, how lit it is against the world's full light picks between that light and the albedo times the look's shadow colour, so shade is a colour (violet by day, blue at night) with a soft edge, never grey; and fog takes the sky's colour along the view ray, so far ground melts into the sky. Neutral is the house tone mapper (`house.exposure` keeps a world's brightness from its ACES days). A world supplies only its look: the Shire's moods in `shire/sky.js` carry a `shadow` per time of day, and `makeAtmosphere` writes the look's light, shadow and sky each frame. A world's ground is data (`src/lib/three/groundmap.js`): it paints `paint(x, z) → { colour, grass }` and its height once into a ground map, and one `groundColour(xz)` colours the floor (per point, `map.paint(material)`), the grass that grows on it and the light it bounces up: `house.ground(map)` turns low, downward faces toward the ground's colour under them (the Shire's `groundAt` in `shire/ground.js` is its painter; its floor light's own bounce is off). Grass is Bruno's (`src/lib/three/grass.js`): one draw, a triangle a blade (78,400 on a desktop), wrapped round a point ahead of the camera in the vertex shader, each blade the ground map's colour with its normal up, as tall as the map's grass says, its root counted as shade; one wind (`src/lib/three/wind.js`, two scrolling lookups of one noise) moves the grass tips, and `wind.sway(material)` the flowers and the oaks' crowns with them. The galaxy's worlds (`galaxy/surface/`) are on it too: `look.js` makes a site's look from its sky (the shadow a third of the way from zenith to horizon, darkened) and its own `look`, their post's tone map stays (the exposure is the site's), their fog stays the dome's own colour (`skyfog.js`, which takes the look's halo and the haze below the horizon), `groundPaint.js` is their ground map's painter (ground.js's palette rule once in JS, the ground darker and the grass thinner under the trees' crowns), Bruno's grass stands on that map, one wind leans the grass and the kit's plants and cloth, every solid kit material wears its core scan in the world (`lib/three/core.js`'s `wear`; built things that move wear twins dressed by their UVs, `kit.moving`), and `?debug` puts it all on sliders, copied out as the site's own blocks (`tune.js`).

Every world is now on the look. A world with its own renderer joins in one call, `houseOn({ renderer, scene, sun, hemi | ambient, env })`, which sets the house tone mapper (lifting an exposure set under ACES unless asked to keep it), adopts the scene and returns `follow({ adopt })`: the full light from those lights, the shadow colour from the sky light (`shadowFor`), and, for a world lit mostly by an HDR, from its environment (`envLevel`: an equirect's mean radiance by latitude; `lib/hdri` stores it on the PMREM it makes, `texture.userData.level`, since a PMREM has no pixels to read). Worlds whose lights change with the area or stage (Roll out, Cybertron's city game) hand it two stand-ins, `{ color, intensity }`, copied from whichever lights are on. `look: { fog: false }` leaves a world's own fog alone, and `toneMap: false` a world whose post pass already tone-maps the house's way (the universe map, the galaxy's space, Cybertron's city game). Left as they are, on purpose: dot-matrix (its 1-bit dither is its look), Mario 64 (the N64's), Earth (the globe's own shaders do its light), the Ring's close-up (all gold metal, which the look leaves alone), the Middle-earth map's backdrop, the cockpit viewer and the hyperspace jump.

## Phones and device tiers

Phones: `src/lib/device.js` decides once what a device can afford (`high` for a desktop with a graphics chip, `mid` for a phone or a small or low-memory computer, `low` for software WebGL, a budget phone or very little memory) and every renderer starts from its budget (pixel ratio, multisampling, shadows, bloom). Beside the tier it decides the quality *level* (`low`, `mid`, `high`, `ultra`; [the design](superpowers/specs/2026-10-07-quality-modes-design.md)): Auto gives a laptop or desktop with a graphics chip `ultra` (`high` where the chip grades `mid`), a phone `mid`, software WebGL `low`; the visitor can pick one in the settings panel (`quality()`, `setQuality()`, kept as `tp-quality`, a `tp:quality` event), and a level picked by hand is never held down by the strain cap. How much a scene draws at each level (triangles, draw calls, model megabytes, props, LOD1, grass, terrain, water, which gen3d cut) is one table, `src/lib/budgets.js` (`budget(level)`, pure, read by `scripts/galaxy-check.mjs` too, which holds each world to its level's row and `lab/baseline/surface-<level>.json`). Add `?quality=low` (or `mid`, `high`, `ultra`) to the address to try a level. A world that downloads a lot (`WORLD_MB` in `src/components/worlds/worlds.js`) asks first on a phone, with Data Saver on, on a weak device or when storage is short: until the visitor says, `components/worlds/WorldGate.jsx` holds its 3D (lib/gpu's `Hold3D`), so the page shows its 2D versions and downloads nothing. The WebGL-only games mount only once they're scrolled near. The 3D scenes use CC0 photo-scanned materials and HDRI lighting from Poly Haven and ambientCG, kept in `public/cc0` (see its README), with procedural textures as the fallback.

## The WebGL-only games

Their assets are all CC0, in `public/games/` with each one credited in `public/games/credits.json`. The output is committed, so the site never calls these services at runtime.

The Aztek's driving is `rules.js`'s `stepCar` and `stepSteer`, worked out in slices of 1/120 s so it's the same at any frame rate. The car has a speed along its nose, a slide across it and a rate of turn: turning swings the nose and leaves the car going where it was, and the tyres pull the two back together as hard as they grip (most on tarmac, less on dirt and sand, a fraction with the handbrake on: Space, X, B or RB on a pad, the Slide button on a phone), so it handbrake-turns, drifts (held on the throttle) and J-turns out of reverse; the wheel locks less the faster it goes and never asks the tyres for more than they hold on the road. A wall met square stops the car and one met at a slant is scraped along; running wide onto the sand takes its speed off over a second. Keys are all or nothing, so the wheel is turned toward them at a rate (slower the faster the car is going, quicker back to the middle, quickest from lock to lock); a stick or a pad's triggers say how far. The driving settings (`O`, kept as `tp-abq-driving`) are how quickly the wheel goes over, how much the car straightens itself out of a small slide, and how tightly the camera follows; the chase camera sits between where the car points and where it's going, so a slide shows, and `scene.js` draws the tyre smoke and the rubber left on the road. `sounds.js` is the engine and the tyres. Every number is in `CAR` and `DRIVING`.

## Minecraft's world

**The game itself, behind a password** (`src/components/eagler/`). The island's crafting table and `/dot-matrix/minecraft` open `Eaglercraft.jsx`: Eaglercraft's offline clients (the real Minecraft 1.12.2 and 1.8.8, compiled to JavaScript by TeaVM) sealed with the site's password, since GitHub Pages has no server to keep anything private. `scripts/eagler-pack.mjs` patches each client (`clients.js`: its own worlds database and settings namespace, no launch countdown), gzips it and seals it (`crypt.js`: PBKDF2-SHA-256 stretches the password once into 32 bytes, which HKDF expands into an AES-GCM key and an HMAC key that picks each file's nonce, so an unchanged file seals to the same bytes) into `public/eagler/<version>.bin`, with `manifest.json` holding the salt and a sealed check line. The page tries the password on the check; the game runs in the player (`public/eagler-player/index.html`, published by `scripts/eagler-player-publish.mjs` to its own repository and origin, https://tilakpatell.github.io/minecraft-player/), a frame that tells the page it's ready, is sent only the stretched key and the sealed file's address by `postMessage`, fetches and opens the file and becomes the game. So the game's code and its worlds (IndexedDB on the visitor's device) are walled off from this site and its storage. Exported worlds sealed the same way are offered to download and import. "Walk the tribute" opens the from-scratch tribute below. `scripts/eagler-check.mjs` unlocks and plays it in headless Edge. The hand-off is `docs/superpowers/HANDOFF-eaglercraft.md`.

**The tribute.**

`/dot-matrix/minecraft`, and the giant crafting table on Dot Matrix island, open a Minecraft tribute (`src/components/minecraft/`; the design, plan and hand-off are `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-07-minecraft-world-design.md`, `plans/2026-10-07-minecraft-world.md` and `HANDOFF-minecraft-world.md`). It is a world module on the runtime (`module.js`) whose rules are plain tested code that never touches three.js, so they run in vitest and in a worker alike: `rules/blocks.js` is the block registry with the game's hardness, tools and drops (a block is a byte, its index); `chunk.js` keeps a 16 × 16 × 256 chunk as three typed arrays (ids, light, state) with a run-length log of the player's edits; `noise.js`, `worldgen.js` and `trees.js` make any chunk from the seed alone (each chunk grows its neighbours' trees too and keeps what lands in it, so a tree across an edge is whole whichever side loads first); `mesher.js` turns a section into quads with the game's corner shading, six 16-bit numbers a vertex; `physics.js` and `player.js` are the game's own movement at 20 ticks a second; `game.js` and `world.js` are the sim and the chunks it holds; `jobs.js` is the worker's queue and the page's client for it. `worker.js` makes each chunk and meshes it against its eight real neighbours, which it generates itself. `scene.js` draws a mesh per pass per chunk column (`scene/chunks.js`) in one block material (`scene/shaders.js`: the game's flat light, the texel times the face's shade, the corner's shade and the light curve, in sRGB as the game does it) over a texture array (`scene/atlasTexture.js`), under the game's sky (`scene/sky.js`). The textures are the game's own (1.21.11, used with Mojang's permission): `scripts/mc-atlas.mjs` builds `public/mc/` from the owner's game jar through `pack/atlas.js`, which reads any pack laid out as the game's (Pixel Perfection builds the same way); grass and leaves are tinted from the pack's own colormaps (`pack/colormap.js`). The survival rules are the same kind of plain code: `build.js` (digging, building, falling sand, dropped items, the bed, doors), `fluids.js` (water and lava by the game's levels and delays), `light.js` (sky and block light, lit in the worker), `furnace.js`, `hunger.js` (the game's `FoodStats`), `gui.js` (the screens' clicks) and `save.js`. `Minecraft.jsx` is the HUD, the screens, the title, the game menu, the death screen and the touch pad. `scripts/mc-check.mjs` plays it in a headless browser.

## Third-party models

**New 3D models are made here, not bought or fetched: `scripts/gen3d/` (its README is the manual).** A picture (a film still, a production painting, a render of the model the site already has on a white background; or a prompt FLUX draws from) goes through TRELLIS.2 on the site owner's RTX 5090 at full quality (~300k faces, a 4096 PBR atlas), is baked in Blender onto a mesh of 60k faces with colour, roughness, metal and normal maps, cut for the web (`web.mjs`: welded, WebP, meshopt, under 4 MB) and credited in `public/games/credits.json` as `gen3d/<name>`; `public/models/gen3d/<name>.glb` is the result. Every model is judged on `judge.mjs`'s four-view sheet against what it's meant to be before it ships, and remade until it's right: accuracy first, size second. From a phone or another session, a GitHub issue labelled `gen3d` (title = name, body = what/prompt/image, or several pictures for Hunyuan3D multi-view) is a job for `runner.mjs` on the desktop, which opens the PR with the sheet. **Voice lines work the same way:** an issue labelled `voices` (body: `only:` speakers, and `who: line` for lines wanted ahead of their code) has `scripts/voices/runner.mjs` on the desktop make every unrecorded line and open the PR; `scripts/voices/README.md` says who has a voice. Reach for it before Meshy (`scripts/meshy*.mjs`, which costs credits and gets famous designs wrong) or a Sketchfab search; the Meshy and Sketchfab scripts stay for what's already on the site. The trench run's X-wing and the TIE fighter are the first.

A few more models are other people's, from Sketchfab under Creative Commons attribution licences: the universe map's sitar, Minas Tirith and Orthanc on the Middle-earth map (`node scripts/sketchfab-batch.mjs <folder of downloads> [name …]` brings each down to what it's seen at, one mesh and one small texture or none, meshopt, into `public/models/sketchfab/`), and the universe map's Star Wars models (the Star Destroyers, TIE interceptors, corvettes, the Death Star and its trench: `scripts/build-universe.py`). Who made each, its licence and its source are in `src/data/modelCredits.json`, which `src/components/ModelCredits.jsx` shows on the page that uses it. The downloads themselves stay out of the repo.

**The worlds' kit is Quaternius's CC0 packs** (the nature megakit's trees, ground cover and rocks, the space kit's props and figures, the farm animals), imported once into `public/kit/<pack>/` by `scripts/kit/import.mjs` (an FBX-only pack goes through `scripts/kit/fbx.mjs` first): one GLB a family, every model beside its LOD1 (bark and solid parts simplified to a quarter, a crown's leaf cards thinned to 40 % and grown to cover), leaves cut out at 0.3, the nature pack's vertex colour turned into a per-vertex wind weight, textures WebP. Each pack's manifest, `index.json` (`scripts/kit/manifest.mjs`), says what every model is (family, file, kind, parts, triangles, footprint, trunk, a crown's two far tones, a rig's clips) and carries the CC0 licence and source that `npm run credits` reads; `node scripts/kit-check.mjs` holds every pack to its budgets (1.5 MiB a file, 12 MiB the nature megakit in all, 15,000 triangles a tree, a tree's LOD1 at most 40 % of it) and finds files no model is in. At run time `src/lib/three/kit.js` loads a pack once, one material a name (the world's house Lambert where it has one, bending in the wind where the manifest says, a bending part's geometry baked into metres so the wind bends it by its real height), and draws its models through keyed instanced pools: a cell, a town or a site hands its placements in (`set(key, items)`) and takes them back (`free(key)`), and each instance is drawn at the band its distance calls for, the full model within `near`, its LOD1 to `mid`, a far stand-in to twice `mid`, by the level's columns in `src/lib/budgets.js`. A galaxy scatter row or thing whose `model` is `kit:<pack>/<Name>` is that kit model, a still one (a rigged model is refused, its kind's build standing in) (`src/components/galaxy/surface/placer.js`). The manual, the manifest's fields and what each pack came to: [`scripts/kit/README.md`](../scripts/kit/README.md).

**The galaxy’s Battlefront II (2017) models are EA DICE’s, used with permission** ([the decision](decisions/2026-10-10-battlefront-2017-assets.md), [the drop](assets/battlefront-2017.md)). `scripts/bf2017-fetch.mjs` takes a model by its manifest name from the owner’s private bucket into the git-ignored `lab/assets/bf2017/`; `scripts/bf2017-import.mjs` picks the site’s cuts from the drop’s own LOD chain, makes its KTX2 or PNG maps WebP, keeps a person’s 2017 rig whole, nothing pruned or renamed (fingers, face, physics, sockets), grounds it, and writes the row into `catalog/bf2017.js` (last in `GROUPS`, so its kinds take over from every other group) and the credit. The pure parts are tested under `scripts/lib/` (`bf2017-manifest`, `bf2017-paths`, `bf2017-textures`, `rig-parts`, `catalog-write`). No visitor ever reaches the bucket. A level's modular system can be made one file of its pieces (`scripts/bf2017-kit.mjs` over `scripts/lib/bf2017-kit.mjs`: a node a piece by its name at the game's own origin, the pieces' parts joined by material and the materials shared, written under `public/models/galaxy/kits/`). The surfaces' roles (the ground's grain, the props' trims) have the game's own maps beside the site's scans (`public/textures/galaxy/bf2017/`, made by `scripts/bf2017-textures.mjs` from `scripts/lib/bf2017-roles.mjs`'s `ROLE_SOURCES`): `lib/three/scans.js` wears one set for the page (`wearScanSet`), which `surface/scene.js` takes from a site's `look.scanned` (`'bf2017'` for the game's; no world asks yet). The levels' probes are published as data under `public/textures/galaxy/sky/` for the owner's lighting lane (`scripts/bf2017-sky.mjs`); nothing loads them.

**The galaxy’s vehicles are the game’s too** (lane V: [the cast](superpowers/evidence/bf2017-vehicles/cast.md), `scripts/bf2017-vehicles.mjs`). They have a catalogue group of their own, `catalog/bf2017-vehicles.js`, after `bf2017`: a `.lod1` and a `.far` cut each, a cockpit as a second GLB in its hull’s frame (`<kind>cockpit`). The walkers and the droideka play the game’s own clips on the game’s own skeletons (`lib/three/ownRig.js`, which names the clips through `lib/three/rigSets.js`). Each rig’s clips are packed by `scripts/bf2017-rigclips.mjs` into `public/models/galaxy/bf2017/clips-<rig>.glb`. `walkers.js` sends a kind whose model is the game’s through it, and the AT-ST’s rigid composite is bound to its skeleton at import (`--bind`, `scripts/lib/rig-bind.mjs`). The space layer’s Sketchfab fighters and capitals are written over by `scripts/bf2017-fleet.mjs` at the files `galaxy/models.js` names, with their far-off copies made again by `scripts/galaxy-lod.mjs`.

## The durable world

What players build on a planet outlives the visit in Supabase (PostgreSQL with PostGIS; why a server at all: `docs/decisions/2026-10-09-supabase-for-durable-shared-state.md`; the design: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-09-planet-flight-and-shared-world-design.md`, Pillar 2). The schema is `supabase/migrations/` and the planets a thing may be built on are `supabase/seed.sql` (written by `scripts/supabase-seed.mjs`; applying both: `supabase/README.md`). The client is `src/lib/durable/`, pure of three.js and React: `supabase.js` makes the one client a page holds from `VITE_SUPABASE_URL` and `VITE_SUPABASE_ANON_KEY`, or `null` when the build has neither (a fork, a local run, CI), and then the game plays with no durable layer; `entities.js` is the 2048 m grid (the network's cell) and the row shape; `entityLoader.js` asks for a planet's entities a cell's envelope at a time round the ship (four asks at once, refreshed after 20 s with `since`), lets go of the cells left behind, folds Realtime's changes in, and places, removes and damages through the table and `damage_entity`, saying what it did as events a scene draws from. Its tests run against a fake client (`src/lib/durable/fixtures/fakeClient.js`); `scripts/supabase-check.mjs` walks the real project by hand. Only the public anon key reaches the page; row-level security on every table is what guards the data.

The shared world over a planet (Pillar 3, lane D) is `src/components/expanse/flight/shared.js`, which composes two paths that never mix. The room (`online.js`: `fly-v1:<planetId>` on the universe's Nostr relays, joined while you're online) is heard by cell: your events carry the 2,048 m cell you're in (`src/lib/net/cells.js`'s `NET_CELL`, which `src/lib/durable/entities.js`'s `CELL` imports) and the relays pass you only the 3 × 3 round it, asked again when you cross a line; poses, shots and the `built`/`gone` hints go this way (`flightProtocol.js`). The durable world (`lib/durable`'s loader, by the same cells) holds what's built: B puts a turret down through the loader, then a `built` hint names its cell so the pilots near you ask for it now (Realtime is the backstop); X takes your own down. Every client runs every turret it holds against every ship it sees (`turretRules.js`): a turret's bolt at your ship wears your shield, your bolt on a turret goes to `damage_entity`, and at nothing it leaves every world. Others' ships, built things and bolts are pools (`peers.js`, `structures.js`, `bolts.js` on `pools.js`). With no Supabase in the build a turret is the visit's own, and B says so once. A thing built on an older ground (`planetSpec.js`'s `TERRAIN_VERSION`, held to a hash of the ground by its test; `terrain_version` on the rows) is drawn on the ground there is now (`buildRules.js`'s `grounded`). `node scripts/online-check.mjs --fly` checks it in two browsers on the fake relays and a fake store (`scripts/lib/fake-durable.mjs`).

## Tests

Tests: `npm test` runs the games' rules and every pure module beside it (Vitest, each file under a second); CI runs it on every pull request, and deploy again before it publishes.

The AI and the models (the gen3d and voices pipelines, their judges, the shipped models and voice lines, the desktop jobs, the NPC brains, the autopilot) are tested end to end in tiers, `scripts/ai-e2e/README.md` the manual:

- `npm run test:ai` (`vitest.ai.config.js`), CI's `AI` job on every pull request: the pipelines from issue to pull request with fake engines behind their own seams (tier 1), the models, credits and voice lines as shipped (tier 2), every brain scripted and fuzzed and the AI toolkit fuzzed (tier 4), the autopilot's guards and the desktop jobs' contract (tier 7).
- `npm run test:ai:render` (`vitest.render.config.js`): every gen3d model rendered headless and made to draw (tier 3), on a pull request that touches a model and every night.
- `npm run test:ai:gpu`, on the desktop: the judges evaluated against labelled sets (tier 5), one real model and one real line made start to end and held to a blessed golden (tier 6).
- `.github/workflows/ai-health.yml` runs the GPU tiers on the desktop's runner at 04:00 UTC and keeps one issue labelled `ai-health`: opened on a red night, closed on a green one; `node scripts/desktop/status.mjs` shows the last night.

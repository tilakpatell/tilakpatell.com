# Architecture notes

Where things live and how the bigger pieces work. The [README](../README.md) has the overview. This file has the detail.

## Where things live

- `src/data/` — the single source of truth for roles, projects, skills and education. Every page and the terminal read from here.
- `src/theme/` and `src/index.css` — the company themes (AWS, RTX, Bose, Pendar, Empowerreg, SRC). AWS is the default; the Experience page switches theme as each role scrolls past, and the switcher in the nav pins one for the session.
- `src/components/ambience/` — a live background behind the portfolio pages for each fan theme ([the design](superpowers/specs/2026-10-05-theme-ambience-design.md)). `Ambience.jsx`, in the app's shell, picks the theme's family (`kinds.js`, tested) and, only on the portfolio pages, on a device that can afford it and unless switched off under the colour picker (`setting.js`), loads `Layer.jsx`: a fixed box under `<main>` that draws `scenes/<family>.js` through `lib/three/useScene`. Every scene is built on `kit.js`: the renderer at a low pixel ratio and 30 frames a second, an orthographic camera in CSS pixels, the pointer and scroll eased, a click on empty page as a burst, and shared pieces (a GPU particle field, a burst pool, a full-screen shader, silhouettes, text sprites). The company themes get a quieter motif in CSS alone (`motifs.css`, with each project's own on its page; nothing downloaded: AWS's rack dots and smile, RTX's drawing grid, Bose's sound rings, Pendar's spectrum, Empowerreg's knowledge graph, SRC's radar sweep). The page's colour moves from `<body>` to `<html>` while either is up (`html[data-ambience]`), so the layer shows through.
- `src/components/RouteLine.jsx` — the line that draws itself down each page as you scroll. Any `[data-waypoint]` element is a stop.
- `src/components/feed/` — the portfolio as a feed: the six portfolio pages (Home, Experience, Projects, Résumé, Travel, Contact) one under the other, so the end of one runs into the next, with a divider between them and an end card after the sixth. `feed.js` is the order and the matching (tested); `Feed.jsx` is the element of all six routes, keeps the stack of pages mounted so far (the next one's chunk fetched as soon as it is next, the page mounted a viewport ahead with its hero held still until it is in sight), and replaces the address with whichever page holds the middle of the screen, carrying `state.feed` so `App`'s `ScrollToTop`, the nav and the theme can tell the feed's moves from a visitor's click (which starts the feed over at that page). `lib/page.js` gives each page whether it is the one on the address: only that one sets the title and drives the theme, and `usePageParams` keeps a page's own search params (a filter, a tab) while it is off the address and puts them back when it is current again. The design is in `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-05-feed-design.md`.
- `src/stages/` — the live demo on each project page (the Game Boy one is playable).
- `src/components/contact/PaperPlane.jsx` — a paper airplane folded from the memo pad (`plane/scene.js`) glides a figure of eight beside the contact page's heading, banking into its turns, trailing dots in the theme's colour and leaning toward the pointer; a click loops it, and the memo form's send (`tp:memo-sent`) throws it off the screen while a fresh sheet folds itself into another.
- `src/lib/view.js` and `src/components/ViewSwitch.jsx` — the two views of the site, the universe (the map) and the classic site (the pages), and the Universe | Classic switch beside the name in the nav (and in the phone menu) that moves between them. `classicPathFor` and `universePathFor` (tested) land on the same place in the other view (`/experience/aws` ⇄ `/universe/experience`, a world's page → its planet); picking one is kept as `tp-start`, which the front door (`pages/Front.jsx`) opens on.
- `src/components/Guide.jsx`, `GuidePanel.jsx` and `guide/` — the "?" guide. `guide/routes.js` is small and loads with the page: which guide a path gets (the deeper paths share their section's), its title, and whether a first visit gets a note by the button (`nudge`, for pages with controls; remembered in `tp-guide-seen`). `guide/pages.js` loads with the panel: each page's controls as tables of keys (`keys` and `touch`, written as `guide/keys.js` reads them: `'hold F'`, `'W A S D / ← ↑ ↓ →'`) and its tips. A new world needs an entry in both (`guide/pages.test.js` checks that every world has one and that the two agree); its first-arrival hint ends with `<GuideCue touch={touch} />` ("· ? all the controls"), and a page's own Controls button opens the guide with `openGuide()` (`lib/palette.js`). Where the corner button would sit over a page's panel (the universe's and the galaxy's), the panel carries `guide/GuideLink.jsx` instead.
- `src/components/projects/Cartridges.jsx` — the projects page opens on a hand of game cartridges in 3D (`cartridges/scene.js`), one per project, moulded in its theme's colour with a printed label; the hand leans to the pointer, the one pointed at rises (tested against where each rests, so it stays pointed at as it moves), and a click opens it. The list below stays the way in for a keyboard or without 3D.

The Iron Man armour on its plinth by the workshop door can be suited up in (`E` within `SUIT.r`: `suitUp`, mode `suit`, `stepSuit`, tested): a hover flight on its repulsors, Space up, Shift down, the stick driving it, walls and the lawn's edge holding, roofs landed on, `E` to step out anywhere and fall as Spider-Man; `scene.js` puts the armour on him leaning into its speed with repulsor trails at its boots, and flies it home to the plinth after; over the roofs in it is the `suitup` achievement. Air tricks (`T`, a pad's d-pad down, the touch Trick button: `TRICK`, `startTrick`, tested) are flips, backflips with the stick back and twists with it to a side, in free flight only; each and each perfect release is worth style points, more for each in a row, banked on landing (`bank`) or lost by landing mid-trick (`bail`); the best bank is kept as `tp-hq-style-best` and 2,000 in one flight is the `showboat` achievement. Photo mode (`P`, or the chip: `PHOTO`, `newPhoto`, `readPhoto`, `photoView`, tested) stops the clock, hides the HUD and puts the camera anywhere round him (drag, wheel, `[` `]` for the lens), never in a building or under the ground; the picture is drawn once more and read off the canvas in the same task, so the renderer needn't keep its drawing buffer. The settings (`O`, or the chip: look sensitivity, inverted pitch, camera distance, swing assist, camera follow and kick; `SETTINGS`, `readSettings`, kept as `tp-hq-settings`) are live, read by the frame loop, and the swing assist goes into `stepHero` as `assist`. The street lamps, benches, planters, flags, roof plant and comms mast stand where `rules.js` says (they're things to bump into, and the flags and masts things to swing from) and are drawn by `grounds.js`. `swing.js` draws the web, the splash where it stuck, the zip and the mark on the next anchor; `scene.js` blends the rig's swinging, flying, climbing and landing poses over his walking clips, leans him into turns, widens the view and pulls the camera back with speed, and `scene.js` draws it in the HQ games' materials under the airfield sky. - `src/components/earth/` — Earth, the world: the Travel planet's page (`#/earth`) opens in orbit over the globe as it is right now and flies you down into the seat behind a little plane over Syracuse. `rules.js` (tested) is the flying with no drawing in it: the plane as a point on the unit sphere and a heading along the ground, flown along great circles (`fly`: turn, climb and descend between `ALT.min`, under the cloud deck `CLOUD_ALT`, and `ALT.max`, boost, a barrel roll `ROLL` that leaves the course alone), the autopilot (`autopilot`: the bearing to a place, slowing down to turn when it's well off the nose or close by), the arrivals (`arrivals`: a stamp within `CAPTURE` of each place, a welcome home), the flight log (`logTrail`: a point every `TRAIL.step` radians with its height, the last `TRAIL.max` kept; - `src/components/universe/` — the universe map, the whole site as places to fly to (`universes.js`, `layout.js`), in a ship with a crew. `ship.js` is the flying, as plain tested numbers: it flies like a Battlefront starfighter, free all the way round (`orient.js` keeps which way round it is, as quaternions and the angles the rest of the map reads): W and S are the throttle, A and D roll, ← and → swing the nose and ↑ and ↓ pull it up and down, each about the ship's own axes with a little inertia and less of it at speed, so it loops, rolls and flies upside down, and lets go back to upright on its own (as quickly as the settings say, or never); it flies along its nose and rounds out before the ceiling or the floor, the chase camera rolls and swings round after it, the brakes bite harder out in deep space, and the autopilot flies you to any planet, station or wonder (click a wonder out in deep space) without going faster than it can stop from. A new ship starts somewhere different each time, unless a place is picked (`startAt`): at the edge of the home system, or out in deep space facing one of the fandoms' planets or a wonder (never by the Maw), so the pilots joining don't all turn up in one spot. `targeting.js` (tested too) is the guns: they lock on to a hunter ahead (the ones coming at you first, and the next one when it goes down; a lock the guns picked themselves moves on when its target has swung wide or flown out of reach and another is squarely ahead, one picked by hand stays), work out where to shoot to hit it, and bend a shot home when the nose is near enough, more forgiving up and down; hold `F` and they fire at the ship's own pace, `T` moves to the next target and `Q` back, and a tap on a hunter locks on. `hunterRules.js` (tested) is the hunters themselves, as plain numbers, with `hunters.js` only drawing them: who comes and how many (more, and the ace more often, the more trouble you've made; the first pack of a visit is a small one, and the director sends nobody new while your shields are under half), where they come in (never inside a planet), and how each flies its attack: out to a station round you that's fixed when it's picked (so turning on the spot doesn't shake them off), in on a run with a real lead on you in all three dimensions, only two or three of a pack on a run at once, the quick ones sometimes staying on your tail; they turn like fighters (a nose that comes round at its own rate, slower into a hard turn), keep clear of each other, and steer round planets, moons and stars, which are cover: they hold their fire behind one and a laser stops at it. Online, the hunters after each pilot are in everyone's sky (`online/protocol.js`'s `pack`: the pilot they're after flies them and says where they are, `online/pilots.js` draws them, and a hit on one by someone else is told to that pilot, whose browser checks it could have been one), so a friend can shoot one off your tail; a pilot just back from being shot down is marked safe and the guns leave them alone, and one whose shots have been landing on you gets a threat arrow like a hunter's. The HUD shows a tough one's hits left and an arrow at the edge for each one coming at you off the screen. `O` opens the flight settings (`controls.js`, `FlightSettings.jsx`, kept between visits): steering, pitch and roll speed, self-levelling, drag sensitivity, aim assist, camera follow, inverted up and down, whether A and D roll or turn, and what dragging up and down does. The HUD over the map (`UniverseMap.jsx`, placed by `scene.js`) shows the gun line, the lock with its name and range, the lead pip and the way to wherever you're going, as an arrow at the edge when it's off the screen (the diamond round a place is never more than a quarter of the frame tall, and round the Citadel's core alone while it's down). The planet maps and the sky come at lib/detail's level (`planets.js`'s `mapFile`, tested: `-sm` on a phone or a weak device, the standard file on a desktop, the `-hq` set from `scripts/build-universe-textures.py --hq` on a strong card). The Citadel's siege (`siege.js`, tested) keeps its blast to the Citadel's own scale (`blastShape`), which `citadelSiege.js` draws. `V` (View on a phone) swaps the chase camera for the pilot's seat: the cockpit from the intro (`cockpit/vehicles`), drawn over the world with the horizon rolling with the ship, all the way round; the choice is remembered.
- `src/components/universe/hunterRules.js` and `targeting.js`, the fight's pace (`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-05-dogfight-pacing-design.md`): the hunters fly the fight at your pace (`fightSpeed`: in the fight, a little faster than you're going, never under a floor of their own top speed nor over it, so at cruise a pass takes seconds and when you boost they open up with you, a TIE a shade slower than your boost and an interceptor not; far off they close flat out, and swinging out each hurries the further its station is, so it gets out ahead of you to turn in), and turn a little quicker than you at the fight's speed and less at their top (`turnRateAt`); a shot counts within `hitRadius`, the same for the hunters seen in another pilot's sky. The map turns under the camera as you fly, and `lookAt` takes a point in the world, so every model that's turned to face the way it flies (the hunters, the other pilots' hunters, the wingmen, their shots) has its map point carried into the world first. The lock picks up within about 29° and holds to 60°, with two seconds' grace, and the nose follows it (`trackNudge`: a nudge on the stick toward the lead point, at most half of full stick, growing with the angle off and fading at the edge of a 40° cone, in the ship's own frame, letting go on an axis the pilot pushes the other way at all; only for a lock on a hunter, one picked by hand or one just fired at, never in the whole-map view). The Lock tracking flight setting scales it (`controls.js`'s `track`, 0 is off).
- `src/components/universe/traffic.js` — everyone else out there, flying the lanes in `lanes.js` (tested): round the place you're at and away from it; where it's somewhere to land (a planet, a station, a giant; not a star, the black hole, a nebula, the Citadel or the Star Wars gate: `dockable`), in to land on it high on one side, shrinking into it over the last stretch, and launching out of it the same way (`laneDock`, `dockScale`), alone; the straight runs before and after a lane go only as far as they're clear of anything solid, and a ship whose run was cut short fades into or out of sight there rather than flying through a planet; out in the open, across the space round you. A ship too small to see (landing, launching or fading) can't be shot. A wing of fighters that's come past peels apart behind you, each rolled away to its own side; while hunters are on you the ordinary ships near you run for it (faster, weaving); a convoy is four to seven freighters with an escort at the front and the back, one either side, and one in the middle of a long one; and each weaves on a phase of its own, run on at its own rate, so nothing jumps as it speeds up. `traffic.test.js` flies it in Node with stand-in models.
- `src/components/universe/wingRules.js` — friends in a long fight, as plain tested rules (`wingmen.js` draws them with the traffic's own models, `scene.js`'s `helpFrom` calls them): once a hunt has run fourteen seconds, or five with the shields under 55%, a wing of two (three against four or more) comes up from behind, more often than not and once a hunt. It forms up off your wings for a moment, then covers you: each goes after a hunter on a run at you or on your tail, never one another has while there's a choice, flies in behind it at the fight's pace (`fightSpeed`) for a pass of a few seconds and comes back on your wing between passes, and fires real bolts (swept against where the hunter was and is) that lead short and miss more than they hit. What they hit is put on the hunters (`hunters.damage`); with nobody left to fight they form up a while, then climb away (and come back if someone comes at you again before they're gone). They're kept out of anything solid, and their slots are off your own wings, rolled with you, so a loop doesn't cross them through each other. The crews say hello and goodbye (`crews.js`'s `wingmen` and `wingmenGone`).
- `src/components/universe/skirmish.js` — someone else's fight, as plain tested rules (`skirmishes.js` draws it with the traffic's and the hunters' own models; `scene.js`'s `farFight` sets one going now and then, 70 to 110 units out ahead while nothing's after you, with the crew's word on it): a freighter circling slowly, a pack of hunters on it (`hunterRules.js`'s own fight, the freighter as the one they're after) and a wing of two escorting it (`wingRules.js`), going after the ones on a run at it. Both sides' shots are real: the hunters' lasers that reach the freighter (within its own size) take a piece off it, the escort's bolts take hunters down, and a hunter with an escort in its sights fires at that instead. It's already a few seconds in when you come on it; its hunters are in the guns' candidates (`skirmish.targets`, none of them a threat to you) and your shots hit them (`skirmish.hit`). It ends when the hunters are gone (the freighter goes on its way and jumps, short of anything solid, and its crew thank you if you helped), when the freighter's shot down (the hunters give up and peel away, each removed only once well away from you: the hunt's `leave()`), or, past 75 seconds, with the freighter jumping away; a jump flashes as the ship's own does. Its hunters are numbered apart from yours (createHunt's `firstId`), since the lock follows a number, and join the guns' candidates only while nothing's after you. It waits till there are no hunters about at all, pirates included.
- `src/components/universe/landings/` — each planet from the ground, once the ship's set down on it (`G`; `footScene.js` runs it). `landings.js` (tested) is each one as data: its name for the card that comes up as you land, its ground (`ground.js`'s styles: grass, sand, tiles, plating with glowing seams, pixels, asphalt), its sky (`sky.js`: a dome round the camera, drawn over space by day) and its things and scatter, in metres round where the ship comes down. `furnish.js` loads the planet's own file (one per planet: `middleearth.js`, `breakingbad.js`, `rickmorty.js`, `transformers.js`, `marvel.js`, `office.js`, `gaming.js`, `music.js`, `travel.js`, `caribbean.js`, `invincible.js`) only when you land there, builds each thing (a builder there, a GLB the data names through the shared loader, `models.js`, or a person, `people.js`, idling on the crews' borrowed clips), stands it on the sphere (`foot.js`'s `place`) and hands walk() its solids (`solidsOn`); a thing of many small meshes is merged into one a material (`kit.merge`), a long flat one bent to the ground's curve (`kit.bend`). A thing can have a door (G there opens the planet's page, through the page's own `onOpen`) or someone's line for when you come up to it (`spots`; the prompt over the map shows it). ## The world runtime

`src/runtime/` is one runtime for the full-screen worlds (the design: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-06-world-runtime-design.md`; the hand-off for moving each world: `docs/superpowers/HANDOFF-world-runtime.md`): one renderer (`webgl.js` on lib/three/renderer's WebGLRenderer; `webgpu.js` on three/webgpu's WebGPURenderer, for a module whose materials are nodes), one frame loop, one input snapshot a frame (`input.js`: the keys held and pressed, the gamepad, the pointer in the host box, the touch stick, with named actions a module binds), one quality level (`quality.js`: lib/device's tier and lib/three/pace's steps, setting the pixel ratio in one place), one save store (`saves.js`: localStorage and sessionStorage, the keys unchanged, with versions and migrations), one asset cache with an owner (`assets.js`: a module's textures and models are forgotten when it goes, unless retained; a prefetch queue by priority), and a gain per module on the site's audio context (`audio.js`, faded at unmount). `runtime()` makes it the first time a world asks and keeps it across routes: its one canvas is moved into whichever world's box is mounted, so a world change doesn't make a renderer, and `rt.handover(nextModule, props, host, { fade, held, after })` lets the next world take over without a cut (the old world draws on, seen, until the new one is ready, and then until `after` resolves, so a world can be built behind a dive or a climb that's still being flown; then the old world's last frame, a 2D snapshot, is kept over the new one and fades out: `handover.js`; `held` keeps it up until the page that shows the new world adopts it, for a handover the route changes after; it answers whether the new world is the one drawing, so a page knows not to steer when something newer was asked for meanwhile). A world is a module, `{ id, shading, mb, create(rt, props) }`, returning `{ ready, resize, step(dt, input, now), draw(frame), wants, handoff, dispose, … }` (`module.js` checks it; `fromScene` wraps a scene written for `useScene` unchanged). A page mounts one with `useWorld` into a `WorldHost`; the module tells the page what happened through `rt.events`. The page scenes (the ambience, the stages, the cartridges) stay on `useScene`. `shading.test.js` reads every module that says `'nodes'` and fails if its folder makes a `ShaderMaterial`, patches `onBeforeCompile` or builds an `EffectComposer`, which WebGPURenderer can't run; every world is `'glsl'` until its shaders are ported. Worlds on it so far: Earth (`components/earth/module.js` is the flight, `EarthWorld.jsx` the HUD over it); the galaxy and its worlds' surfaces (`components/galaxy/module.js`, `galaxy/surface/module.js`: both `fromScene`, drawing with the runtime's renderer and putting back what they set on it). The trip between them is flown (`galaxy/travel.js`): landing dives the ship on the planet in space (`planDive`/`diveAt`, the air's glow coming up over it) while `pages/Galaxy.jsx` has the surface module built behind it (`rt.handover` with `after`: the dive's end), and the route follows; the surface goes on with its approach and landing. Taking off, the ship climbs out on the surface while `pages/GalaxySurface.jsx` has the galaxy built behind it, which takes over 3.4 s in under the sky's glare, the galaxy starting with the ship climbing off the planet (`LAUNCH_KEY`). Without 3D flying, both go the old way (a fade and the page). `scripts/travel-check.mjs` flies the round trip in headless Chromium.

## Graphics

Multiplayer across the worlds ([the design](superpowers/specs/2026-10-05-multiplayer-across-worlds-design.md)): the site's link (`universe/online/client.js`, held by `OnlineProvider` above the pages) is one room on the Nostr relays for everyone online, carrying the roster, the ships and crews, and the pointers on ordinary pages; each walkable world opens a room of its own for the people walking it (`middleearth/towns/useTravellers.js` → `travellers.js`, drawn by `ghosts.js`). Every room is joined with the visit's one key (`nostr.js`'s `visitKeys`, `joinAsVisitor`), so a traveller's id is their roster id: a block in the roster hides their ghost too (`travellers.js`'s `hidden`). `where.js` keeps each of Middle-earth's chapters a place of its own (`/middle-earth/bree`), named for the roster, whose Go button takes you into that world. While a world's up (`useOnline`'s `enterWorld`, from `useTravellers`) the page pointers aren't drawn over it, and the pages with their own corner (`ownCorner`: in flight, and down on a galaxy world) get no second one. `scripts/online-check.mjs` checks it in two browsers on the real relays.

Graphics: the site and its games are 3D first: they draw in WebGL (Three.js, loaded only when it's used) wherever the browser has it, a graphics chip or WebGL run in software, and lower their own resolution and effects when frames can't keep up (judged on real time, so a laptop's battery-saving 30 fps cap doesn't count). On the core pages that's the Experience diagrams, the travel globe, the mist over the Travel photos, the lamplight on the Peace section, the Akshardham day, the jump to lightspeed and the GPU project's demo; `src/lib/three/` holds their shared renderer, the theme-colour reader and the `useScene` hook that loads a scene only near the viewport and draws only while it's on screen. Every scene and game compiles its shaders in the background before its first frame (`renderer.js`'s `precompile`, with the browser's parallel compile where it has it), so something new coming into view doesn't stop the page while the graphics chip catches up; the first frame waits at most four seconds for it. A scene made while something covers the whole page (the universe, behind the intro's crawl and cockpit) also warms up out of sight, a slice at a time while the page is idle (`useScene` and the scene's `warmUp`): its pictures go to the graphics chip a few at a time, then one small draw, passes and all, sends the meshes and makes the passes' shaders, so its first frame at the cockpit's flash has nothing left to send. On a first visit the cockpit also tells the universe the ship you're sitting in (`tp:board`), so it's made, and its model fetched, before the launch (lib/three/renderer's `revealAll` and `uploadTextures` are the shared pieces; a low tier skips the warm-up). `src/lib/gpu.js` checks once. Only a browser with no WebGL at all, or a GPU context that's lost or fails, gets the SVG and 2D versions. Visitors can switch 3D off under the trench run. The universe map goes further, since a dogfight or a boost can push any graphics chip: `src/lib/three/pace.js` watches every frame and draws the scene a step softer within a second of frames coming late, and sharp again once they don't (the names and the HUD over it stay crisp); hunters and traffic ships are built ahead while the page is idle, so a pack arriving doesn't stall the fight; and multiplayer's signing and checking run in a worker (`online/signer.worker.js`), off the frames.

Textures and models: every texture goes through `src/lib/three/textures.js`'s `sharpen` (the device tier's anisotropy, 16 on a desktop, 4 on a phone; the right colour space; wrapping), and `loadTexture` decodes images off the main thread (`ImageBitmapLoader`, where the browser handles it) and shares one texture per URL across scenes. Every model comes through `src/lib/three/gltf.js`: one `GLTFLoader` with meshopt that also reads GPU-compressed KTX2 textures, fetching its transcoder only for a file that carries one (`scripts/ktx2.mjs` makes those, and reports per texture whether the bytes are worth it: the measurements are in `docs/research/2026-10-05-textures-and-asset-quality.md`). `prepare` gives a loaded model shadows, uncullable skinned meshes and sharpened maps; `tune` clamps a generator's material defaults. Big tiled grounds (the compound's lawn and apron, Roll out's desert, the music courtyard's dunes) go through `src/lib/three/surface.js`'s `antiTile`, which blends a turned second copy of the same maps in by a slow noise of world position so the repeat never lines up, with a fine detail normal fading in underfoot.

## Phones and device tiers

Phones: `src/lib/device.js` decides once what a device can afford (`high` for a desktop with a graphics chip, `mid` for a phone or a small or low-memory computer, `low` for software WebGL, a budget phone or very little memory) and every renderer starts from its budget (pixel ratio, multisampling, shadows, bloom). Add `?quality=low` (or `mid`, `high`) to the address to try a tier on a desktop. A world that downloads a lot (`WORLD_MB` in `src/components/worlds/worlds.js`) asks first on a phone, with Data Saver on, on a weak device or when storage is short: until the visitor says, `components/worlds/WorldGate.jsx` holds its 3D (lib/gpu's `Hold3D`), so the page shows its 2D versions and downloads nothing. The WebGL-only games mount only once they're scrolled near. The 3D scenes use CC0 photo-scanned materials and HDRI lighting from Poly Haven and ambientCG, kept in `public/cc0` (see its README), with procedural textures as the fallback.

## The WebGL-only games

Roll out and Portal panic are the exception: they need the graphics chip, so with hardware acceleration off they say so, with the steps for the visitor's browser, and offer to play anyway. Surfaces that are painted in code share `src/lib/texture.js` (tiling noise, and colour, normal and roughness maps from one function).

The Aztek's driving is `rules.js`'s `stepCar` and `stepSteer`, worked out in slices of 1/120 s so it's the same at any frame rate. The car has a speed along its nose, a slide across it and a rate of turn: turning swings the nose and leaves the car going where it was, and the tyres pull the two back together as hard as they grip (most on tarmac, less on dirt and sand, a fraction with the handbrake on: Space, X, B or RB on a pad, the Slide button on a phone), so it handbrake-turns, drifts (held on the throttle) and J-turns out of reverse; the wheel locks less the faster it goes and never asks the tyres for more than they hold on the road. A wall met square stops the car and one met at a slant is scraped along; running wide onto the sand takes its speed off over a second. Keys are all or nothing, so the wheel is turned toward them at a rate (slower the faster the car is going, quicker back to the middle, quickest from lock to lock); a stick or a pad's triggers say how far. The driving settings (`O`, kept as `tp-abq-driving`) are how quickly the wheel goes over, how much the car straightens itself out of a small slide, and how tightly the camera follows; the chase camera sits between where the car points and where it's going, so a slide shows, and `scene.js` draws the tyre smoke and the rubber left on the road. `sounds.js` is the engine and the tyres. Every number is in `CAR` and `DRIVING`.

## Third-party models

A few more models are other people's, from Sketchfab under Creative Commons attribution licences: the universe map's sitar, Minas Tirith and Orthanc on the Middle-earth map (`node scripts/sketchfab-batch.mjs <folder of downloads> [name …]` brings each down to what it's seen at, one mesh and one small texture or none, meshopt, into `public/models/sketchfab/`), and the universe map's Star Wars models (the Star Destroyers, TIE interceptors, corvettes, the Death Star and its trench: `scripts/build-universe.py`). Who made each, its licence and its source are in `src/data/modelCredits.json`, which `src/components/ModelCredits.jsx` shows on the page that uses it. The downloads themselves stay out of the repo.
